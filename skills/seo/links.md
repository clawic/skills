# Links — Internal Equity, Acquisition, and Cleanup

Two link economies exist. Internal links you control completely and can change this afternoon; external links you can only earn. Almost every site is under-spending the first while obsessing over the second.

## Internal Linking

- Every important page within 3 clicks of the homepage — click depth correlates with crawl frequency and with perceived importance.
- Descriptive anchors ("SEO audit checklist", not "click here"). Internal anchors are the one place exact match is safe: you control them and Google expects them.
- Contextual in-content links carry more weight than navigation and footer boilerplate, which appear on every page and therefore differentiate nothing.
- New page → link it from your 2-3 strongest related pages the day it publishes. Orphans get crawled late and rank late.
- Fix broken internal links before any outreach: reclaimed equity at zero outreach cost.
- Point links at the canonical URL, not at a redirect. Internal redirect chains are self-inflicted latency.

**Internal link audit, in order:** (1) list pages with traffic and few inbound internal links — quick wins; (2) list pages with many inbound links and no traffic — wasted signal, repoint; (3) find orphans by diffing the crawl against the sitemap and GSC's URL list; (4) check that the money pages have the most contextual links; (5) check anchors for variety and accuracy.

## Redirects And Equity

- 301s no longer lose PageRank (Google confirmed in 2016) — the old "10-15% loss" rule is dead, and consolidating via 301 is safe.
- Keep chains ≤3 hops; long chains slow crawling and can be abandoned.
- Redirect to the closest matching page, never mass-redirect to the homepage: Google treats irrelevant redirects as soft 404s and passes nothing.
- Reclaim lost equity: find 404s with external links (GSC's Not found report plus any backlink source) and redirect them to the closest live page. This is the cheapest link building there is, and it is finished in an afternoon.

## External Anchor Text

- Exact match: powerful, and the first pattern link-spam systems check — keep it a small minority.
- Partial match and branded anchors: the bulk of a natural profile.
- Naked URLs and generic text ("read more", "here"): natural filler, expected in real profiles.
- A profile dominated by exact-match commercial anchors reads as manipulation regardless of link quality.
- You rarely control the anchor of an earned link, which is precisely why natural profiles look messy — engineering yours to look tidy is the tell.

## Nofollow, Sponsored, UGC

- Since 2019 (in effect for ranking from March 2020), nofollow is a hint rather than a directive — Google may still count it.
- `rel="sponsored"` for paid placements, `rel="ugc"` for comments and forum posts.
- The violation is undisclosed paid follow links; correctly attributed paid links are policy-compliant and still carry referral traffic and brand value.
- Nofollowed links from high-traffic places are worth having on their own merits; treating them as worthless is a link builder's superstition.

## Evaluating A Prospect

Better than domain rating alone: **does the linking page itself rank and receive traffic?** A link from a page with search traffic beats a link from a high-authority site's orphaned post. Then check:

| Signal | Good | Bad |
|---|---|---|
| Topical relevance | Same or adjacent subject | A general "business blog" that covers everything |
| Page traffic | The page ranks for something | Zero impressions, orphaned |
| Outbound link pattern | A handful of editorial links | Dozens of unrelated commercial links per post |
| Audience | Real readers, real comments, real social presence | Content published only for link buyers |
| Placement | In-body, in context | Author bio, footer, sidebar, "partners" page |
| Price list exists | — | Any site with a rate card is selling links, whatever it calls the arrangement |

## Earning Links

- **Linkable assets**: original data, free tools, calculators, and research — the things writers cite without being asked. One good asset outperforms a year of outreach emails.
- **Digital PR**: a newsworthy angle built from your own data, pitched to journalists who cover that beat. Survey and index-style studies work because they give a reporter a number to quote.
- **Journalist requests**: HARO shut down in 2024; its successors (Source of Sources, Qwoted, Featured, and similar) work the same way — fast, specific, quotable answers from a named expert.
- **Broken link building**: find dead resources in your niche, offer your page as the replacement. Low volume, high acceptance rate.
- **Unlinked mentions**: search for your brand name minus your domain; a polite request converts a decent share because the writer already cited you.
- **Resource pages and industry directories** that a human curates.
- **Guest posts** on relevant sites with a contextual in-body link; author-bio-only links are worth little.
- **Partnerships, sponsorships, and community**: real relationships produce links that no outreach template can, and they are the only ones that keep arriving.

Outreach reality: response rates on cold link outreach are low single digits for generic pitches. The variables that move them are relevance of the target, a specific reason this page helps their reader, and a short email. Volume without relevance produces spam complaints, not links.

## What Not To Do

- Buying links, PBNs, and scaled exchanges → manual actions; recovery takes months of cleanup plus a reconsideration request.
- Automated comment, forum, and profile links: worthless at best, a footprint at worst.
- Scholarship and "write for us" schemes at scale: known patterns.
- Sitewide footer links from clients or partners: they look like paid placements because usually they are.
- Sudden velocity spikes on a site with no history: velocity itself is not a penalty, but the pattern invites a look at where the links came from.

## Disavow

- Only with a manual action naming links, or an unmistakable spam attack. Google ignores most spam links automatically, and a careless disavow cuts real equity you cannot get back.
- Disavow at domain level for spam sites (`domain:spamsite.com`); page-level entries are for special cases.
- Effects take weeks to months as links are recrawled — there is no immediate feedback, which is exactly why it gets over-used.
- Negative SEO exists but is rarer than the invoices claim. Before assuming an attack, rule out your own changes, a core update, and a competitor's improvement.

## Link Traps

| Trap | Why it fails | Do instead |
|---|---|---|
| Buying links from a rate card | Payment footprints are the pattern spam systems look for | Earn or do not have the link |
| Chasing domain rating | The metric is a third-party estimate, not a Google signal | Judge the page, its traffic, and its relevance |
| Outreach before the asset exists | There is nothing worth linking to | Build the asset first |
| Ignoring internal links | The one lever you fully control, free and immediate | Audit and repoint quarterly |
| Disavowing on suspicion | Cuts real equity, no upside without a manual action | Disavow only on evidence |
| Counting links as the KPI | Links are an input; rankings and revenue are the outcome | Report links alongside the pages they lifted |
| Redirecting old link targets to the homepage | Treated as a soft 404, passes nothing | Redirect to the closest topical match |
