# Commercial Pages — The Bottom-Funnel Page Types

Most content programs publish "how to" articles and wonder why organic traffic does not convert. The pages that turn search into revenue are a small, well-known set of types, each matched to a query pattern where the searcher is already choosing. Build these before the top-of-funnel library.

## The Page Types And Their Queries

| Page type | Query pattern | Why it converts |
|---|---|---|
| Alternatives | "{competitor} alternatives", "{competitor} vs alternatives" | Searcher has already rejected a product; you are one of the options being compared |
| Head-to-head comparison | "{you} vs {competitor}", "{competitor A} vs {competitor B}" | Final-stage evaluation, highest intent of all |
| Best-of / category listicle | "best {category} for {segment}" | Owns the shortlist; also the format AI summaries and assistants pull from |
| Pricing | "{product} pricing", "how much does {category} cost" | Answering price questions publicly filters out bad fits and wins a query competitors dodge |
| Integrations | "{product} + {tool} integration" | Long-tail with near-zero competition, one page per real integration |
| Use case / persona | "{category} for {industry or role}" | Matches the words buyers use for themselves, not your feature names |
| Template / free tool | "{thing} template", "{thing} calculator" | Earns links and email addresses; the most link-attractive asset a B2B site can build |
| Glossary / definition | "what is {term}" | Cheap topical coverage and internal-link fuel; low conversion, do it after the above |
| Anything else | Check the SERP; if it is dominated by review sites, plan to appear on them instead of outranking them | — |

## Alternatives And Comparison Pages

- Be honest about where the competitor wins. A comparison that says you are better at everything gets discounted by readers and does not survive scrutiny; naming their genuine strengths is what makes the rest credible.
- Structure: the summary verdict first (who each product is for), then a table of the differences that decide the purchase, then the detail.
- Keep facts checkable and dated. Pricing and feature claims about a competitor age quickly, and stale claims are both a credibility and a legal problem.
- One page per competitor for "{you} vs {them}", plus one alternatives page for "{them} alternatives" — different intents, different searchers.
- Comparison pages are among the most-cited page types for both classic snippets and assistant answers; tables with clear rows are what gets extracted.

## Best-Of Pages For Your Own Category

- If you sell in the category, including yourself is expected — but rank yourself honestly and give the criteria before the list.
- The pattern that ranks: explicit selection criteria, a comparison table, a paragraph per option with who it suits, and evidence you actually used them.
- These SERPs are usually owned by review sites and publishers. Check page 1: if it is all third-party media, the winning move is getting reviewed by them, not competing head-on.

## Pricing Pages

- Publish real numbers or a real range. "Contact us" loses the query and the trust, and a page with no price cannot answer the searcher's question.
- Where pricing is genuinely bespoke, publish the model, the drivers, and typical ranges by segment — that is the answer people are searching for.
- The pricing page is a ranking page: it deserves a title targeting the query, an FAQ covering total cost, and internal links from the money pages.

## Integrations And Use Cases

- One indexable page per real integration, with setup steps and what the combination accomplishes. Skip the ones that do not exist — placeholder integration pages are doorway pages.
- Use-case pages must be written in the reader's vocabulary. "Inventory reconciliation for multi-warehouse retailers" is a query; "advanced sync engine" is not.
- Both types age fast; assign an owner and a refresh cadence, or they become the site's stale corner.

## Free Tools And Templates

- The single most reliable link-earning asset for a commercial site: a calculator, generator, or template pack solving one narrow problem completely, free, no signup to use.
- Make the tool page indexable and self-contained; do not hide it behind a form. Gating it converts a few and forfeits the links that would have paid for it.
- Add the surrounding content the query needs — the methodology behind the calculator is what makes the page rankable rather than a bare widget.

## Ordering The Build

`priority = query volume × conversion rate of that intent × winnability`

Bottom-funnel first, in this order, because each earns while the next is written: pricing → comparisons and alternatives → integrations and use cases → best-of → tools → glossary. A site with 200 informational articles and no comparison pages has built the top of a funnel with no bottom.

## Commercial Page Traps

| Trap | Why it fails | Do instead |
|---|---|---|
| Comparison pages that only flatter you | Readers discount everything; conversion drops | Concede the competitor's real strengths |
| "Contact us for pricing" | Loses the pricing query outright | Publish numbers, ranges, or the model |
| Integration pages for integrations that do not exist | Doorway pages; disappoints the visitor who arrived | Only real ones |
| Gating the free tool | Trades the links that make it rank for a few emails | Free and indexable, capture later |
| Writing bottom-funnel pages last | The traffic that pays for the program arrives last | Build them first |
| Never updating competitor claims | Stale facts damage credibility and invite complaints | Date the page, schedule a review |
| One page for "alternatives" and "vs" | Different intents, both underserved | Separate pages |
