# Deal Desk: Discounts, Terms, and Approvals

Finance's job on a deal is not to say no. It is to ensure every concession is paid for and that nothing signed today creates an obligation nobody priced.

## The Trade Rule

Never give price alone. Every discount buys one of: a longer term, prepayment, a wider scope, a public reference, or a case study. A discount with nothing in return resets the price for that customer's renewal and for every prospect they talk to.

## Discount Math

```
Gross-profit loss % = discount % ÷ gross margin %
```

At 80% gross margin, a 20% discount destroys 25% of the gross profit on that deal (list 100 → GP 80; discounted 80 → GP 60). At 60% margin, the same 20% discount costs 33%. Sales sees "20% off"; the P&L sees a third of the contribution. Show this number in the approval request, not the discount percentage.

## Approval Matrix

Discount bands are percentages and hold at any deal size; the deal size that pulls the CEO in scales from `approval_threshold` (50× it — $50K at the default):

| Discount off list | Approver | Required in the request |
|---|---|---|
| 0–10% | Account executive | Nothing |
| 11–20% | Sales manager | What was traded for it |
| 21–30% | VP Sales + Finance | GP impact, payment terms, term length |
| 31%+ or any non-standard term | CFO (and CEO above 50× `approval_threshold`) | Full deal economics, rev rec impact, precedent risk |

Publish the matrix and the turnaround SLA (same day for the first two bands). An approval process slower than the customer's patience is a process sales will route around.

## Payment Terms Have a Price

- Annual prepayment accelerates roughly half a year of cash on a 12-month contract. Its maximum worth to you: `cost of capital × months accelerated ÷ 12`. At a 30% cost of capital and ~5.5 months of average acceleration, a prepay discount above ~14% is you paying more for the cash than the cash is worth.
- Net-60 and net-90 are a loan from you to a customer, priced at your cost of capital. Concede longer terms only on deals whose margin covers it, and never to a customer already paying late — every extra 30 days of terms is 30 days of DSO you are financing.
- Quarterly or monthly billing on an annual contract is the standard consolation prize when you cannot get prepay — it keeps the term while limiting cash exposure.

## Non-Standard Terms Finance Must Price or Veto

| Term | Why it matters | Default position |
|---|---|---|
| Termination for convenience | Removes the contract from ARR quality; acquirers discount it | Veto below enterprise scale; if granted, cap notice and exclude from ARR disclosures |
| Uncapped liability or uncapped indemnity | Unbounded downside on a bounded deal | Veto; counsel decides any exception |
| Most-favored-nation pricing | One future discount reprices this customer retroactively | Veto — MFN converts every later negotiation into a company-wide price cut |
| Custom SLA credits | Variable consideration reduces recognized revenue | Cap total credits as a percentage of fees; model the expected claim |
| Auto-renewal removed | Every renewal becomes a new sale | Trade for a longer initial term |
| Non-standard security or compliance commitments | Costs land in engineering, never in the deal margin | Price the engineering cost into the deal or decline |
| Pilot converting automatically to paid | Rarely recognized as revenue on the schedule sales assumes | Define the conversion trigger in writing |

## Commissions

- Pay on **bookings** for standard deals with standard terms; pay on **cash collected** for deals that are high-risk, long-payment-term, or with a new customer of unknown credit. The plan should make the safest deal the most attractive to sell.
- Clawback window on early churn or non-payment — commonly the first 6–12 months — written into the plan and applied consistently. An unapplied clawback is not a policy.
- The commission itself is capitalized and amortized over the period of benefit under ASC 340-40, with a practical expedient to expense it when that period is a year or less. Cash timing is unaffected either way.

## Quarter End

- Discount depth rises with the day of the quarter, and customers learn the calendar faster than sales leadership does. The counter is a published policy that does not move: same matrix on day 5 and day 90.
- Deals that need finance's approval on the last afternoon of the quarter are the ones most likely to contain a term nobody read. Set an internal cutoff 48 hours before quarter end for anything non-standard.
- A deal pulled into this quarter with a discount is next quarter's hole plus a repriced customer. Count it as both when the CEO asks.

## Renewals and Uplift

- Put an annual uplift clause (a fixed percentage or an inflation index) in the standard contract. Absent it, every renewal is a fresh price negotiation with an incumbent's disadvantage.
- Model the renewal at the actual contracted rate, not at list. A multi-year discount that expires at renewal is a price increase the customer will experience as one.
