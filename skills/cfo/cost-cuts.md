# Cash Crunch: Cutting, Extending, and the Wind-Down Floor

A layoff costs cash before it saves cash. Every decision here is arithmetic first and emotion second — do the arithmetic before the conversation, because once the decision leaks you lose the option to change its shape.

## Trigger Ladder

Keyed to the canonical runway table — 18+ months strong, 12–17 healthy, 6–11 act now, under 6 emergency. `runway_alert_months` in config sets when this file gets opened unprompted.

| Runway | Move |
|---|---|
| 18+ months | Nothing. Efficiency work, not cutting |
| 12–17 months | Freeze low-conviction hiring; renegotiate the three largest vendor contracts; start the raise |
| 6–11 months | Cut in one pass to a plan that extends past 12 months — aim for 18 if the cut can reach it. Run the raise in parallel |
| Under 6 months | Emergency: bridge, deep cut, or sale process — all three explored simultaneously, board involved this week |
| A payroll date inside the 13-week forecast turns the balance negative | Stop everything else; this is a Red Flag with counsel and the CEO today |

## Order of Cuts

Work the list top to bottom; each level is fully exhausted before the next is opened.

1. **Discretionary and unused** — unused software seats, travel, events, perks, contractor scope, agency retainers. Fast, reversible, and usually 5–15% of non-payroll spend sitting in plain sight.
2. **Vendor renegotiation** — the single highest ratio of savings to pain (script below).
3. **Committed spend restructuring** — cloud commitments, lease sublet or surrender, marketing contracts, deferred capex.
4. **Executive and founder comp reduction** — small in dollars, decisive in signal, and it buys credibility for step 5.
5. **Headcount** — last, once, and sized for the bear case.

Uniform percentage cuts belong nowhere on that list. A 10% haircut across every team protects the weakest activity in each department and taxes the strongest. Cut whole activities and name them.

## The Layoff Arithmetic

```
Payback (months) = (severance + accrued-leave payout + benefit continuation
                    + legal + recruiting write-offs + equipment/space costs)
                   ÷ monthly run-rate savings
```

- Typical payback for a startup RIF is under 3 months. **Above 6 months, you cut the wrong line** — you spent cash to save a cost that was not the problem.
- Month one of a layoff usually shows burn going **up**. Plan the cash for that month explicitly; a company that cuts because it cannot make payroll may not be able to afford the cut.
- Size for the bear case and cut once. A second layoff destroys more trust than a single deep one, and every strong performer starts interviewing after the second.
- The savings only exist if the work stops. A cut that leaves the same commitments in place returns as contractor spend within a quarter.

## Mechanics You Cannot Improvise

Requirements are jurisdiction-specific and change; counsel reviews the plan before any notification. Common US-federal reference points: mass-layoff notice obligations under the WARN Act apply to employers with 100+ employees and generally require 60 days of notice, with state equivalents that trigger at smaller sizes; benefit continuation (COBRA) applies to employers with 20+ employees. Final-pay timing, accrued-leave payout, and notice periods vary by state and are far longer in most of Europe and Latin America.

Sequence for the day:
1. Board approval and counsel sign-off on the list and the criteria used to build it.
2. Cash confirmed for severance, final pay, and the next payroll — before anything is announced.
3. Individual conversations first, all-hands within the hour, customers and investors the same day.
4. Access, payroll, and benefits changes executed on the agreed date, not before the conversation.
5. Equity treatment stated in writing: vesting stops at the termination date, and the post-termination exercise window starts — default 3 months, after which ISOs become NSOs — this is the question every departing employee asks second.
6. Remaining team gets the new plan the same week: what changed, what the runway is now, and what will not change again.

## Vendor Renegotiation Script

- Rank vendors by annual spend. The top five usually hold more than half of non-payroll cost; ignore the tail.
- Ask in this order: extend the term for a lower rate → convert annual prepay to monthly (a cash win even at the same price) → reduce seats or tier → pause with a restart right → terminate.
- Say the real thing: "We are extending runway and reviewing every contract. I would rather keep you at a lower number than replace you." Renewal dates are leverage; ask 60 days out, not on the renewal date.
- Cloud and infrastructure: right-size before renegotiating a commitment, or you lock in the waste for another year.

## Buying Time Without Cutting

Ranked by cost, cheapest first: collections push and early-pay incentives on the largest overdue balances → vendor term extension → an undrawn facility already in place → annual-prepay offers to existing customers → an insider bridge note → new debt → a structured down round. Structure taken under duress is deferred dilution with interest; price it as such.

## The Wind-Down Floor

Compute this number early and put it on the cash page of every board pack:

```
Floor = final payroll + statutory severance + accrued leave + payroll taxes
        + wind-down legal and accounting + lease termination + dissolution costs
```

Below the floor an orderly shutdown becomes impossible, and the board's duties begin shifting toward creditors as insolvency approaches — a legal question for counsel, not a judgment call for management. No forecast crosses the floor without an explicit board decision, and a soft landing or acqui-hire negotiated **above** the floor is worth several multiples of one attempted below it.

## What Not To Cut

- Anything that produces the metric your next raise is priced on.
- The person who knows how the money actually moves — losing finance during a crunch removes the instrument panel.
- Security, insurance, and compliance obligations: an incident during a cash crisis is the compounding event.
- Customer-facing quality on the accounts funding the runway. Churn during a cut converts a cash problem into a business problem.
