# Cap Table, Options, and Employee Equity

The cap table is a legal record that happens to look like a spreadsheet. Errors compound out of sight and surface in diligence, when fixing them costs deal time.

## Cap Table Hygiene

- One system of record (Carta, Pulley, or a lawyer-maintained ledger) and one owner. A founder-maintained spreadsheet plus a law-firm version guarantees they disagree by the next round.
- Fully diluted means issued shares + all options granted + the unallocated pool + warrants + as-converted notes and SAFEs. Ownership quoted on issued shares only is a number that flatters everyone in the room.
- Reconcile the cap table to board consents quarterly: every grant, every exercise, every repurchase, every transfer. Anything without a signed consent is not a grant.
- Model an exit waterfall against the preference stack before you need it — founders routinely discover the payout math for the first time inside a deal, when the buyer's model arrives with the answer already computed.

## 409A Valuations

- Required **annually** and after any **material event**: a priced round, a tender or secondary, a large acquisition offer, a major pivot, or a big miss against plan.
- A grant priced off a stale 409A creates a cheap-stock problem for **every grantee**, not for the company alone — the spread becomes taxable income. Options granted on a 409A older than 12 months or issued after a material event are a Red Flag: pause grants and commission a new valuation.
- The safe harbor is what you are buying: an independent appraisal shifts the burden to the tax authority to prove the value unreasonable.
- A tender offer at a price above the current 409A usually pushes the next 409A up, which raises the strike for future hires. Sequence tenders and hiring waves deliberately.

## Option Pool Sizing and Refreshes

- Size the pool from a **named 12–18 month hiring plan** with a grant per level, not from a round-number percentage. In a round the investor usually requires the top-up pre-money, so existing holders pay for it — counter with the named plan, not their round number.
- Run a refresh budget as an annual allocation approved with the comp cycle, expressed as a percentage of fully diluted per year and set with the board — one-off retention grants negotiated individually are how pools evaporate.
- Returns to the pool: unvested shares on departure, and unexercised options after the post-termination window expires. Track them; teams routinely re-request pool increases while holding returned shares.

## Grant Mechanics That Bite

| Item | Rule | What goes wrong |
|---|---|---|
| Board approval | A grant exists when the board approves it, at that day's fair market value | Offer letters promising a strike price before approval; backdating is a fraud exposure, not a paperwork slip |
| 83(b) election | Filed within **30 days** of a restricted-stock purchase or early exercise; no extensions, no exceptions | Missed by founders and early exercisers; the tax bill arrives as the stock vests |
| ISO $100K limit | Options first exercisable in a calendar year above $100K of grant-date value are NSOs by operation of law | Large grants split into ISO and NSO tranches nobody tracked |
| ISO AMT | The spread at exercise is an AMT preference item even with no sale | Employees exercise late in a high-value year and owe tax on paper gains |
| NSO exercise | Spread is ordinary income at exercise, with employer withholding | Payroll is unprepared for a large exercise; the company owes the withholding |
| Post-termination window | Default 3 months, after which ISOs become NSOs; some companies extend to 7–10 years | A departing employee who cannot fund the exercise forfeits everything — decide the policy before the first departure, not during it |
| ISO reporting | Form 3921 to the employee and the tax authority for exercises | Missed filings surface in diligence |

## Secondaries and Tenders

- Employee liquidity is a retention tool with three costs: 409A impact, the signal to the market about who is selling, and the administrative load.
- Set rules before demand appears: who may sell, what percentage of holdings, at what cadence, with what board and ROFR process. Ad-hoc approvals create precedent you cannot revoke.
- Founder secondaries at a round are common and negotiated; investors read size as commitment. Advise, never decide (SKILL.md Human-in-the-Loop).

## Employee Communication

- Every grantee should be able to answer three things: how many shares, what percentage of fully diluted, and what it takes for them to be worth something. Publish the fully diluted count — withholding it makes the grant unvaluable and reads as evasive.
- Explain the preference stack honestly: at a $50M exit with $60M of preference, common is worth zero. The employee who learns this at the exit is a permanent detractor.
- Never model an expected value with a promised valuation. Give the share count, the current 409A, the strike, and the exercise window, and let them do their own math.
