# Board Reporting and the Meeting

The board pack is the CFO's highest-leverage artifact: it sets what the board believes about the company for the next quarter. A meeting where the numbers are explained for the first time is a meeting where no decision gets made.

## Cadence by Stage

| Stage | Board meetings | Between meetings |
|---|---|---|
| Pre-seed / seed | Quarterly, 60–90 min | Monthly investor update |
| Series A | Quarterly, half day | Monthly metrics email, ad-hoc calls on decisions |
| Series B+ | Quarterly plus an annual planning session | Monthly reporting pack, committee meetings between boards |

Send the pack **3–5 days before** the meeting; 48 hours is the floor below which directors read it in the room and the discussion collapses into narration. State explicitly at the top: "this pack is pre-read; the meeting starts at the decisions."

## Pack Anatomy

1. **One-page CEO summary** — state of the company in five bullets, including the bad one.
2. **KPI dashboard** — the same metrics, same definitions, every quarter, with 6–8 periods of trend. A metric that appears once was chosen because it looked good.
3. **Cash and runway** — closing cash, forecast burn, runway in months, next raise trigger date — each stated with the burn assumption behind it.
4. **P&L vs plan** — variance by line above materiality, each with cause, impact, and action.
5. **Forecast** — rolling forecast vs the board-approved budget, both shown, difference explained.
6. **Hiring** — plan vs actual headcount, open requisitions, cost impact.
7. **Decisions requested** — each with options, recommendation, and what happens if deferred.
8. **Appendix** — cap table, detailed financials, cohort data, anything a director might ask for.

The pack is a document, not a deck read aloud. If the meeting time goes to slide narration, the pre-read failed.

## The Pre-Wire

- Call every director individually before a consequential meeting: 15–30 minutes, the number that will surprise them, and your recommendation. A director who first hears bad news in the room defends a position instead of thinking.
- The purpose is not agreement, it is **no new information in the room**. Surprise converts a supporter into an auditor.
- Pre-wire the loudest director first and the most skeptical last, so you enter the room having already answered the hardest objection.

## Delivering Bad News

- The plan travels with the problem. Never present a miss without the diagnosis, the response, and the date you will know if the response worked.
- Own the number before anyone asks. "We missed by 18%; here is why, here is what we are doing" ends a topic that "we saw some headwinds" would have stretched across the whole meeting.
- Restate a metric definition only with the history restated alongside it, and flag the change in writing. A definition that changes without notice reads as concealment even when it was an improvement.
- Escalate between meetings when a material event lands: a covenant risk, a key customer loss, a fraud signal, or a forecast change that moves the raise date. Waiting for the calendar is how a solvable problem becomes a governance issue.

## Committees and Corporate Hygiene

- **Audit committee** — forms when an external audit starts or a lender requires it, typically Series B+. It is also the escape valve for a fraud or control-override signal that involves management (SKILL.md Red Flags).
- **Compensation committee** — forms when the board must approve exec comp and equity grants independently; it owns the annual comp cycle and the refresh pool.
- The CFO owns the consent queue: option grants, 409A adoption, budget approval, banking resolutions, and share issuances. Unsigned consents are the most common cap-table defect found in diligence.
- Minutes are legal documents. Record decisions and the basis for them, not debate transcripts; approve the previous minutes at each meeting so the record never runs a quarter behind.

## What Investors Are Contractually Owed

- Financing documents define **information rights**: which investors get monthly or quarterly financials, on what deadline, and often inspection rights and an annual budget delivery.
- Major-investor thresholds mean small holders get less; sending everything to everyone waives a distinction you may want later.
- Missing a contractual reporting deadline is a covenant-grade failure with investors just as it is with lenders. Put the delivery dates in the same close calendar that carries the lender reporting deadlines.

## Meeting Anti-Patterns

| Anti-pattern | Effect | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| Pack sent the night before | Directors read live, ask basic questions, no decisions | 3–5 day rule, enforced by the close calendar |
| Metrics that change shape each quarter | The board cannot see a trend and stops trusting the set | Fixed KPI page; changes flagged and history restated |
| Only the CEO speaks | The board never assesses the team | Each functional lead owns 10 minutes on their area |
| Decisions with no options presented | The board either rubber-stamps or relitigates | Two or three options, a recommendation, and the cost of deferral |
| No executive session | Directors have no forum for concerns about management | Standing closed session at the end of every meeting |
| Approvals handled verbally | Nothing is legally effective | Written consents prepared before the meeting, signed after |
