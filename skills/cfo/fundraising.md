# Fundraising and Capital

## Timing

Fundraising keys off runway state: 18+ months is strong (raise only opportunistically), 12–17 is healthy (prepare the next round), 6–11 means act now (active raise or cuts that extend past 12), and under 6 is an emergency (cut deep, bridge, or accept dictated terms). The math behind "raise when you can, not when you must": a priced round takes 3–6 months from first deck to wire, so starting at 12+ months of runway means even the slow case closes with leverage intact; starting at 8 means you may negotiate the final terms under 6, and investors read your bank balance in diligence.

- Raise for 18–24+ months of runway: enough to hit the milestones that price the next round, plus a buffer for the raise itself.
- Investors invest in lines, not dots (Mark Suster): consistent monthly updates for a year beat a cold deck — start the updates long before the raise.

## Round and Dilution Math

- Post-money = pre-money + amount raised. Raise $5M at $20M pre → $25M post → new investor owns 20%; everyone else is diluted by 20% proportionally.
- Typical dilution per priced round: 15–25%. Two rounds at 20% each leave founders with 64% of their starting stake before any option pool.
- **Option pool shuffle**: investors usually require the pool topped up pre-money, so existing holders pay for it. A "$20M pre" with a 10% post-money unallocated pool demand is effectively ~$18M for current shareholders. Counter: size the pool from a named 12–18 month hiring plan, not the investor's round number.
- Model before every round: current ownership → new shares → post-money ownership → next-round assumptions → exit scenarios. A cap-table spreadsheet with scenario tabs; recompute when any term changes.

## Term Sheet Terms

| Term | What it means | Market standard | Red flag |
|------|---------------|-----------------|----------|
| Liquidation preference | Who is paid first at exit | 1x non-participating | >1x, or participating preferred |
| Anti-dilution | Protection in a down round | Broad-based weighted average | Full ratchet |
| Board | Control and governance | 2 founders / 1 investor (or 2-2-1) at seed–A | Investor majority pre-B |
| Option pool | Employee equity dilution | Sized to hiring plan, pre-money | Arbitrary 15%+ demand |
| Pro-rata rights | Follow-on rights | Standard for major investors | Super pro-rata |
| Tranched milestones | Money released on targets | Rare, avoid | Any tranching — it's an option, not a commitment |

Valuation is the term founders over-negotiate; preference stack and board control are the terms that decide who gets paid and who gets fired. Trade valuation for clean terms, not the reverse.

## Down Rounds

- A down round with clean terms beats a flat round with structure. Structure (participating preferred, ratchets, senior stacks) is deferred dilution with interest — it detonates at exit, when you can least renegotiate.
- Reprice fast, communicate the plan, reset employee options in the same motion. A slow-motion down round demoralizes twice.

## Data Room

Ready before the first partner meeting — assembling it mid-process telegraphs disorganization and slows momentum when it's most expensive:

**Required**: financial statements (since inception), monthly P&L and balance sheet, fully diluted cap table, 12 months of bank statements, revenue by customer, key contracts, employee roster with compensation.
**Strengthens the raise**: cohort analysis, unit economics, pipeline, roadmap, competitive analysis.

What diligence probes first — pre-answer these in the room:
- Customer concentration: any customer above ~20% of revenue gets a dedicated question; above ~30% it prices the round.
- Revenue recognition: annual contracts booked as monthly revenue is the classic startup restatement.
- Related-party transactions and verbal side letters: disclose before they're found.

## Investor Updates

Monthly, especially when it's hard — silence is read as bad news at maximum volume.

```
Subject: [Company] Monthly Update - [Month Year]

TL;DR: [One sentence state of company]

Key Metrics: MRR $X (+Y% MoM) · Customers X (+Y) · Runway X months
Wins: [2-3, concrete]
Challenges: [each with what you're doing about it]
Asks: [specific intros or help]
```

Same metrics, same definitions, every month. Changing how you calculate a metric mid-stream reads as hiding something — if a definition must change, flag it and restate history.

## Debt vs Equity

| Factor | Favor debt | Favor equity |
|--------|------------|--------------|
| Cash flow | Predictable, positive | Variable, negative |
| Dilution tolerance | Low | Higher acceptable |
| Use of funds | Asset purchase, bridge to known event | Open-ended growth |
| Timeline | Short-term need | Long-term runway |

Venture debt is typically sized at ~25–35% of the last equity round and raised within 6–12 months of it, while metrics are fresh; its covenant trap is that MAC clauses can make the facility vanish exactly when you need it, so it extends a good runway but never rescues a bad one. Debt is cheap until a covenant trips; equity is expensive but can't be recalled.
