# Protocols — Picking, Scheduling, Progressing

## Decision Table

| User profile | Start here | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Never fasted, wants to try | 12:12 → 14:10 → 16:8 ramp | Hold each step until hunger waves settle (typically 1-2 weeks); jumping straight to 16:8 works for many, but the ramp costs nothing |
| Wants a daily routine that survives social dinners | 16:8, window 12:00-20:00 | Evening-weighted window fits real life; the most sustainable protocol |
| Skips breakfast anyway | 16:8 or 18:6, late window | Formalizes an existing habit — the highest-adherence path there is |
| 16:8 established, wants more | 18:6, or 16:8 + one 24h fast weekly | Two levers — daily depth vs weekly spikes; change one at a time |
| Wants one food decision per day | OMAD (~23:1) | Simplicity is the feature; the one meal must carry the day's protein and micros |
| Prefers not to fast daily | 5:2 (two non-consecutive 500-600 kcal days) or ADF | Calorie-budget days, not clock days — track kcal, not hours (`tracking.md`) |
| Metabolic reset, experienced faster | 36-72h, occasional | Prep and electrolytes mandatory → `extended.md` |
| Anything 5+ days | Medical supervision | Not a self-serve protocol; decline to coach it solo (Red Flags) |

## Protocol Mechanics

- **16:8** — 16h fast, 8h window, daily. Example: last bite 20:00, first bite 12:00. Failure mode: window creep — 20:00 quietly becomes 21:30 (`tracking.md` detects it, `weight-loss.md` explains the damage).
- **18:6 / 20:4** — longer daily ketosis exposure; the shrinking window's appetite ceiling becomes the limiter. 20:4 ("warrior") is OMAD-adjacent: judge it by protein actually eaten, not hours achieved.
- **OMAD** — ~23h fast, one meal. The trap is chronic under-eating: one sitting rarely carries a full day comfortably, and the resulting fatigue gets misread as "adaptation". Watch protein and energy across weeks.
- **5:2** — five normal days, two non-consecutive days at 500-600 kcal. Hypoglycemia rules for medicated diabetics apply on the low days exactly as on clock fasts (`safety.md`).
- **ADF** — alternate-day fasting, minimal intake on fast days. The strongest trial literature in the intermittent family; the hardest social fit. Offer it, don't push it.
- **36h ("monk fast")** — dinner day 1 → breakfast day 3. The first protocol where rule 4 electrolytes always apply.
- **48-72h** — full prep, daily electrolytes, calendar cleared of training and high-stakes work → `extended.md`.
- **Religious calendars** (Ramadan, Yom Kippur, Lent) are observance, not optimization — separate rules → `religious.md`.

## Scheduling the Window

- Anchor the window to the immovable meal (family dinner, work lunch) and let the other edge float. Adherence beats theoretical optima; a perfect window nobody keeps is worse than a decent one kept daily.
- Early windows (e.g. 08:00-16:00) showed better glucose and insulin responses in early time-restricted-eating trials; late windows fit real evenings. Default late; mention the tradeoff once if the stated goal is metabolic (`metrics.md`).
- Shift workers: define the day wake-to-wake and place the window in the first half of the waking span; on rotating shifts keep the window LENGTH fixed and let clock times slide (`tracking.md`).
- Weekend drift: a ~2h weekend shift is normal and breaks nothing. A different weekend pattern (16:8 weekdays, none on weekends) is legitimate — log it as the pattern it is, not as five successes and two failures.

## Progressing and Switching

- One variable at a time: lengthen the fast OR move the window OR add a weekly long fast — never together, or results can't be attributed.
- Hold a new protocol ~2 weeks before judging it: week-1 discomfort is adaptation, not a verdict.
- Step DOWN when: sleep degrades, training quality collapses across weeks (`training.md`), the cycle turns irregular (`women.md`), or food preoccupation creeps in (Red Flags if it patterns like disordered eating).
- There is no podium. When asked "which protocol is optimal": the one that survives the user's actual calendar. Say exactly that.
