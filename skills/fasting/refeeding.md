# Refeeding — Breaking a Fast Without Undoing It

## Portion Scaling (canonical numbers: SKILL.md rule 5)

| Fast length | First meal | Then |
|---|---|---|
| <24h | Normal meal, protein first | Resume normal eating |
| 24-48h | ~1/2 of a normal portion, protein/fat over refined carbs | Wait 30-60 min before more |
| >=48h | ~1/4 of a normal portion, protein/fat over refined carbs | Wait 30-60 min; keep every meal modest for the rest of the day |
| 72h+ | Staged: broth or eggs, wait, then soft food | Small meals for 24-48h; a fast this long should already have a clinician involved (Red Flags) |

## First Foods

| Good first | Why | Avoid first | Why |
|---|---|---|---|
| Eggs, fish, poultry | Protein blunts the post-fast glucose spike | Fruit juice, soda | Fast sugar into an insulin-sensitive system: spike, then crash |
| Bone broth | Fluid + sodium + gentle protein in one | Large rice/pasta/bread plates | The refined-carb bolus is what refeeding risk rides on |
| Cooked vegetables, avocado | Gentle fiber and fat | Big raw salads | Raw fiber volume on a rested gut = bloating |
| Yogurt, kefir | Protein plus easy digestion | A fried or very fatty feast | The gallbladder dumps concentrated bile after days off duty — cramping; keep first-meal fat moderate |

## Refeeding Syndrome (the risk that scales with length)

- Mechanism: the insulin surge on refeeding drives phosphate, potassium, and magnesium into cells; serum levels crash. Severe form: edema, muscle weakness, confusion, heart-rhythm problems — a medical emergency.
- Risk becomes meaningful past 72h of minimal intake. NICE high-risk criteria include very low intake for 5+ days, BMI <18.5, alcohol misuse, or already-low electrolytes — any of those plus a fast means clinician before refeeding, not after symptoms.
- Prevention at self-serve lengths (<=72h) is exactly the scaling table: small, protein/fat first, no carb bolus.
- Swelling, palpitations, or confusion in the day after breaking a long fast → Red Flags action, urgently.

## After the Break

- GI grumbles for a few hours after a correctly-sized break meal are common; escalating pain is not. Right-upper-abdomen pain after a long fast → gallbladder, clinician (`safety.md`).
- Resume the regular protocol the next day. Never schedule a compensatory extra-long fast after a "bad" break — compensation loops are a disordered-eating pattern (Red Flags row).
- Log the fast closed at first caloric intake, not at the "real meal" (`tracking.md`: the clock has one definition in both directions).
