# Pricing and Packaging — the Marketing Half

Pricing is a joint decision with finance and product; marketing owns the *packaging*, the *communication*, and the *discount discipline*. Willingness-to-pay research methods and tier modelling in depth belong to `pricing-strategy`; what follows is what a CMO must get right.

## The Value Metric

Pick the unit the customer's own success grows with: seats, records, transactions, workspaces, GB, agents. Tests for a good value metric:

- The customer can see it growing and would accept paying more as it does.
- It is not gameable by punishing the customer for using the product well (charging per API call while asking them to automate more is a contradiction).
- It correlates with your cost to serve, so growth does not erode gross margin.
- It is countable without a spreadsheet argument at renewal.

A wrong value metric shows up as: constant negotiation at renewal, customers architecting around your meter, and expansion revenue that does not track usage.

## Packaging Rules

- **Three tiers by default**, differentiated by *who the buyer is*, not by an arbitrary feature ladder: individual, team, organization. Ten tiers means nobody chose.
- Put the features an enterprise buyer legally requires (SSO, audit logs, data residency) in the tier that enterprise will buy — and know that pricing security as a luxury attracts public criticism; decide deliberately.
- Free → paid boundaries follow the loop rule in `product-led.md`: never gate the mechanism that grows your user base.
- Anchor with a higher tier you genuinely sell. A visible enterprise option makes the middle tier the reasonable choice; a fake one you never close is a credibility cost.
- Annual vs. monthly: the discount for annual is buying cash and retention. Price it against what the cash is worth to you, not against what competitors do.

## Discount Discipline

Break-even unit lift for a discount:

```
break-even lift = d ÷ (m − d)      d = discount points, m = gross-margin points
```

Worked: 20% off at 50% gross margin → 20 ÷ (50 − 20) = 67% more units just to stand still. At 30% margin, a 20% discount needs 200% more units — arithmetic that ends most discount conversations.

Rules:
- Every discount needs a **reason code** (multi-year, case-study commitment, volume, launch cohort) and an **expiry**. A discount without a reason is a permanent price cut applied unevenly.
- Publish an internal discount ladder with approval thresholds, or the largest discount any rep ever gave becomes the company's real price.
- Discounting to hit a quarter borrows revenue from the next one and trains the buyer to wait (SKILL.md Traps).
- Prefer scope changes over price cuts: fewer seats, a shorter term, a narrower package. Price integrity survives; the deal still closes.

## Communicating a Price Increase

Sequence that survives contact with customers:

1. **Decide the grandfathering policy first** and write it down: who is exempt, for how long, and what ends it. Ambiguity here creates a support crisis on announcement day.
2. **Notify existing customers before the public page changes** — never let a customer discover it on the website or from a competitor.
3. **Lead with what changed on your side**: added value, new capabilities, cost realities. "Prices increase on [date]" without a reason reads as opportunism.
4. **Give a concrete date and a concrete number** per plan, with an example bill. Percentages hide the impact and generate a second wave of questions.
5. **Offer one honest bridge**: lock the current price by moving to annual before the date. This converts a churn risk into cash.
6. **Arm support and sales the same day**, with the objection list and the exact exception authority. Improvised exceptions become the new policy.
7. **Watch churn and downgrade by cohort for 90 days**, not aggregate churn — the effect concentrates in the smallest and oldest plans (`measurement.md`).

Expect noise from the loudest segment and check whether it is the segment that pays. A price increase that upsets free users and retains paying ones worked.

## Free-to-Paid Conversion Levers

In order of usual impact: reaching the activation event (`product-led.md`) → the upgrade trigger arriving at a moment of success rather than failure → clarity of the tier comparison page → payment friction (currencies, methods, taxes shown honestly) → time-limited offers, last.

Never make cancellation harder to protect the conversion number; in several jurisdictions cancellation must be as easy as signup, and everywhere it converts a quiet churn into a public complaint (`compliance.md`).

## Promotions Without Damage

- Time-box everything and state the end date up front. A permanent sale is a price.
- Never discount the same segment on a predictable calendar — buyers learn the calendar and stop paying full price.
- Prefer value-adds (extra seats, a service credit, an extended term) to headline price cuts: they cost margin points instead of resetting the price anchor.
- Measure a promotion on incremental revenue, not on promoted revenue — the customers who would have bought anyway are the majority of most promo results (`measurement.md`). Retail and DTC calendars in `ecommerce.md`.

## Pricing Page

The page is a positioning artifact: it says who you are for. Rules — show a price or a starting price; make tier differences scannable in one column-by-column pass; put the value metric in the headline of each tier; answer the top five objections as an FAQ below (taxes, contracts, overage, downgrade, data export); keep it reachable in one click from the homepage (`positioning.md`).
