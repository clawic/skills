# International — Entry Sequencing, Localization, Regional Channels

Read this when `markets` is set to anything beyond the home market, or when someone proposes "going international" as a growth plan. The usual failure is not a bad market; it is entering four at once with a translated website and no owner.

## Entry Sequencing

Score candidate markets on five axes before committing budget:

1. **Existing pull** — inbound signups, traffic, and support requests already arriving from there. Free evidence; weight it highest.
2. **Willingness and ability to pay** — local price levels for the category, not GDP.
3. **Competitive density** — an entrenched local incumbent with local integrations is a harder opponent than a global brand.
4. **Cost to serve** — language support hours, payment methods, tax registration, data-residency demands, returns logistics.
5. **Regulatory load** — consent regimes, sector rules, contract language requirements (`compliance.md`).

Sequence one market at a time and define what "working" means before entering: a revenue threshold and a date. Two markets entered simultaneously make it impossible to learn which decisions worked.

Minimum viable entry: localized pricing and payment methods, a landing experience in the local language, one local channel test, and a named owner in the timezone. Missing any one of the four turns the test into a null result.

## Translation Is Not Localization

| Layer | Translated | Localized |
|-------|-----------|-----------|
| Copy | Same message in another language | The claim that lands in that culture, in native idiom |
| Proof | Home-market logos | Local customers and local case data |
| Pricing | Converted at spot rate | Local price points, local rounding, local payment methods |
| Search | Home keywords translated | Keywords researched natively — volumes and phrasings rarely map |
| Compliance | Ignored | Local consent, invoicing, and claim rules |
| Support | English only | Local-hours coverage, or an honest stated window |

Machine translation of marketing copy is acceptable for internal comprehension and unacceptable for anything a buyer reads: an off-register headline signals "not for you" more strongly than English would in many markets. Budget native review, and keep one owner per language for consistency of terminology.

Keyword volumes never transfer. A term with high volume at home may not exist locally, and the local phrase for the same problem may sit in a different category entirely — re-run keyword research natively before writing anything (`content.md`).

## Regional Channel Map

Defaults differ far more than most plans assume:

- **China** — the open web is largely irrelevant to acquisition; discovery runs through WeChat, Weibo, Xiaohongshu and Douyin, and search means Baidu. Requires a local entity, ICP filing for hosting, and local partners. Treat as a separate company-level decision, not a marketing expansion.
- **Japan and Korea** — LINE and KakaoTalk are primary messaging surfaces; trust signals, formality and documentation depth matter more than speed; local search behaviour favours long-form detail.
- **Germany and the DACH region** — privacy expectations are high and enforced; imprint (Impressum) requirements are legal, not stylistic; buyers read specifications.
- **Latin America** — WhatsApp is a primary sales and support channel, and local payment methods (instalments, bank transfer schemes) decide checkout completion.
- **India and Southeast Asia** — mobile-first, price-sensitive tiering, and messaging apps as the service channel.
- **US from elsewhere** — the largest market and the most competitive auction; expect CAC several times the home-market figure and budget accordingly.

Verify current platform and regulatory specifics before committing spend — this map changes, and the cost of an outdated assumption falls entirely on you.

## Organizational Model

| Model | Fits | Fails when |
|-------|------|-----------|
| Centralized, translated | Early entry, one or two markets, uniform product | Local nuance never reaches the team; local team becomes a translation queue |
| Hub with local marketers | Several markets at scale | Unclear ownership of the brand and the message |
| Fully local teams | Markets with distinct products or regulation | Brand fragments; every market invents its own positioning |

Whatever the model: the message pillars are global, the expression is local (`positioning.md`). Publish a terminology glossary per language, or the same product acquires three names in one market.

## Money and Measurement

- Report in one reporting currency for comparison and in local currency for local decisions; state the conversion basis (`currency` in Configuration) so quarter-to-quarter comparisons are not accidentally an FX story.
- Local price points, not converted ones. Rounding conventions and psychological price points differ, and a converted price with three decimals reads as an import.
- Set market-level kill lines exactly as for a channel test (SKILL.md Rule 6), with a longer window: entry costs are front-loaded and pipeline lags.
- Exiting a market is a legitimate outcome, not a failure. Plan the exit conditions on the way in — customer commitments, contract terms, and the migration path.
