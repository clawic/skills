# Paid Media — Pacing, Creative, Waste, and Who Buys It

Paid media is the only channel where you can spend the whole budget in a week with nothing to show. It is also the fastest instrument you own for testing a message (`positioning.md`). Treat it as a rented, metered utility — never as a growth strategy on its own.

## What Actually Drives Performance, in Order

1. **Audience/targeting** — reaching the wrong people cannot be fixed downstream.
2. **Offer** — what the click gets (`demand.md`, Offers).
3. **Creative** — on most modern auction platforms, creative is the largest remaining controllable lever, because targeting is increasingly delegated to the platform's optimizer.
4. **Landing experience** — message match, load speed, one action.
5. **Bidding and structure** — real, but the smallest of the five once the first four are right.

Teams invert this list and spend their week in the bid settings.

## Account Structure Defaults

- Separate campaigns by *intent*, not by product: brand terms, competitor terms, category terms, and retargeting each behave differently and deserve their own budget and read.
- Give the platform's optimizer enough conversions to learn: if a campaign produces only a handful of conversions per week, optimize toward a reliable upper-funnel event (qualified lead form, trial start) and judge the money event separately.
- Feed the platform your *qualified* conversions, not raw form fills — otherwise you buy more of exactly the lead sales rejects (`diagnose.md`).
- One change at a time, with a read window of at least one full sales cycle for anything downstream of the click.

## Brand Terms: the Most Common Fake ROAS

Bidding on your own brand name usually reports the best ROAS in the account and often creates the least incremental revenue — those buyers were arriving anyway. It is still defensible in three cases: a competitor is bidding on your name, the SERP pushes your organic result below the fold, or you need to control the message on a live crisis or launch. Settle it with evidence, not opinion: pause brand terms in a matched set of geos for two weeks and read total (paid + organic) branded sessions and pipeline (`measurement.md`). A large share of the paused clicks usually returns through the organic result; measure the share in your own account rather than quoting an industry figure.

## Creative Testing

- Test *concepts*, not colours: a different problem framing, a different proof, a different format. Button-colour tests belong to `cro`, not to media buying.
- Diagnose fatigue with two numbers together: frequency rising while CTR falls at stable CPM = fatigue. CPM rising alone = auction competition, not your creative.
- Refresh cadence follows spend velocity, not the calendar: the faster you spend against a fixed audience, the faster you exhaust it. A small audience with heavy spend can fatigue in days.
- Keep a control running. An account with all-new creative every cycle has no baseline and cannot tell fatigue from a bad month.
- Winning ad copy is a positioning asset: whatever wins in the auction goes into the homepage headline and the sales deck.

## Budget Pacing

- Pace to the sales cycle, not to the month: front-loading spend into a quarter's last weeks buys pipeline that closes in the next quarter.
- A hard monthly cap with no daily pacing produces a dark last week — the platform stops learning and the following month restarts from zero.
- Reserve a standing test budget (a fixed share of the paid line, set once and defended) so that experimentation does not compete with the pipeline number every month.
- Any pause longer than two weeks re-enters a learning phase; budget the re-learning cost before pausing for a holiday.

## The Waste Audit (run quarterly, 60 minutes)

| Check | Common finding |
|-------|----------------|
| Search terms report vs. keyword list | Broad match paying for jobs, tutorials, and "free" queries |
| Placement report | Spend on app inventory and made-for-advertising sites |
| Geo report | Budget in countries you do not sell to or cannot support |
| Device split | Mobile clicks landing on a page that only converts on desktop |
| Conversion definitions | Duplicate or double-counted conversions inflating ROAS |
| Audience overlap | Retargeting and prospecting bidding against each other for the same person |
| Landing pages | Live ads pointing at 404s, expired offers, or last year's pricing |
| Exclusion lists | Existing customers and current opportunities being paid for again |

Excluding current customers and open opportunities from prospecting is the single fastest saving in most accounts — and it also stops the sales team from seeing "their" deals in a cold ad.

## Reading the Numbers Honestly

- Platform-reported conversions are the platform's own homework. Reconcile against the CRM monthly; a persistent gap is a modeling difference, a sudden gap is a tracking break.
- Optimize the click-level metrics in-platform, allocate budget between channels from a source that can see everything (`measurement.md`).
- ROAS is a ratio, not a profit: at 60% gross margin, a 2× ROAS loses money on every order. Judge paid on contribution margin (`ecommerce.md`) or on CAC payback (`operations.md`).
- A "cheap" channel with a 4% lead-to-opportunity rate can cost more per opportunity than an expensive one at 25%. Always carry the metric to cost per opportunity, not cost per lead.

## In-House vs. Agency vs. Freelance

| Setup | Fits | Fails when |
|-------|------|-----------|
| In-house | Spend large enough to occupy a person; creative iteration is the bottleneck | The single specialist leaves and the account has no documentation |
| Agency | Multi-platform scale, sudden capacity, technical audits | Paid a % of ad spend — the incentive is to grow spend, not efficiency (`operations.md`) |
| Freelancer | Single-platform, sub-scale budgets, an interim after a departure | Nobody owns strategy and the freelancer optimizes to their own report |

Non-negotiables in every arrangement: you own the ad accounts, the pixel, the audiences, and the creative files. Handing those to an agency converts a supplier into a hostage-taker.

## Retargeting Discipline

Retargeting reports well and is easy to over-fund: it re-reaches people already in your funnel, so its conversions are the most likely to have happened anyway. Cap it as a share of paid spend, exclude converters and recent customers, cap frequency, and set a membership window matched to the buying cycle — a 180-day window in a 14-day cycle just annoys people. When in doubt, holdout-test it before scaling (`measurement.md`).
