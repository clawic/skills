# Content Strategy

## Two Jobs, Mapped to 95:5

Content either captures existing demand or creates future demand (95:5 → SKILL.md Rule 4):
- **Capture content**: pricing, "X vs Y", "X alternatives", integration pages — low volume, highest conversion intent.
- **Creation content**: point of view, teaching, original research — builds memory in the 95% not buying yet.

Most teams over-produce top-funnel volume and under-produce the dozen-odd bottom-funnel pages that actually convert.

## BOFU-First SEO Order

Build in this order — the reverse of volume-chasing:
1. Pricing, comparison, alternatives, and integration pages.
2. Problem pages phrased in the ICP's own words — mined from sales call recordings, not keyword tools alone.
3. Pillar/cluster top-funnel pages for compounding traffic — only after 1 and 2 exist.

Rankings take months, not weeks. That lag is why SEO gets killed at exactly the wrong time — set its kill line (SKILL.md Rule 6) in quarters, not weeks.

Comparison pages you did not write are being written about you by review sites and competitors. Owning the "you vs. them" page is not arrogance; it is the only version of that page where the facts are yours and current.

## Content-Market Fit Signal

You have it when: prospects mention specific pieces on sales calls; inbound messages cite content; subscribers match the ICP, not just traffic. Traffic growth with none of these = writing for the wrong audience or the wrong funnel stage (`diagnose.md`).

## The Portfolio, Not the Calendar

Budget content like an investment portfolio rather than a publishing schedule:

| Type | Share of effort | Payoff | Read after |
|------|-----------------|--------|-----------|
| Money pages (BOFU) | Small, permanent | Direct conversion | Weeks |
| Compounding assets (guides, tools, calculators) | Largest single block | Traffic and links that grow | Quarters |
| Original research and data | Occasional, expensive | Links, press, authority (`comms.md`) | Quarters, then years |
| Point-of-view and opinion | Steady, cheap | Memory in the 95%, recruiting, sales trust | Never attributable; judge by citation |
| Refresh of past winners | Continuous, cheapest | Usually the highest ROI line in the plan | Weeks |

A plan that is 90% new top-funnel posts is a content factory, not a strategy.

## Original Research

The most reliable link and press magnet in B2B: run a survey or mine your own product data for a statistic nobody else can publish. Requirements: a defensible method (sample size and question wording published), a number that is genuinely new, and a single citable chart. Repeat annually — the second edition earns more than the first because it becomes a series people wait for. Never publish a proprietary-data statistic without checking customer contracts and privacy commitments (`compliance.md`).

## Visibility in AI Answer Engines

Assistants and AI search answer the buyer's question without a click, then cite a handful of sources. What travels into those answers is *quotable, attributable* material: clear definitions, named numbers with a method, comparison tables, and pages whose claims are stated plainly rather than buried in narrative. Practical consequences:

- Structure pages so a single paragraph answers the question completely, then elaborate below.
- Keep pricing, capability, and comparison facts current on the page — stale facts get quoted back at you in a sales call.
- Track brand mentions in answers as a visibility metric alongside rankings; treat "cited but not clicked" as brand impression, not as a loss.
- Expect this to move: verify current behaviour of the specific engine before making a big bet on it.

## Repurposing

Create once at the highest-fidelity format you can sustain, then derive downward:

```
Webinar / long video
    → blog post → social clips → quote graphics → email series → sales deck
```

Deriving upward (blog → webinar) costs nearly as much as starting over — pick the apex format first.

## Cadence

Commit only to a cadence you can hold for a year: algorithms and audiences both punish gaps more than they reward bursts. Cut frequency before cutting quality — a marginal mediocre post subtracts trust; it doesn't add reach.

Staffing check before committing: one editorially strong piece per week is roughly a full-time load once research, interviews, editing, design, and distribution are counted. Plans that assume otherwise fail in month three, which is exactly when SEO would have started to work.

## Gated vs. Ungated

Gate only what a stranger would trade an email for: tools, templates, original research. Everything that builds positioning stays ungated — the 95% will not fill a form to learn who you are. A gated download signals curiosity, not buying intent: route to nurture, never straight to an SDR call list.

Middle path when sales demands names: publish ungated and put an optional email-for-the-PDF/summary offer at the end. You keep the reach and collect the subset that self-identifies.

## Editorial Standards That Prevent Rework

- One owner per piece, one reviewer, one deadline. Committee review is where content goes to become average.
- Every piece names its funnel stage and its next step before drafting; a piece with no next step is a brand asset and must be justified as one.
- Subject-matter interviews beat solo drafting: 30 minutes with an engineer or a customer produces material no research pass can invent.
- Kill criteria for the archive: pages with no traffic, no links, and no sales use after two refresh attempts get merged or removed — thin archives dilute the pages you want ranked.

## Distribution Checklist

After publishing (SKILL.md Rule 8 — hours distributing ≥ hours creating):
- [ ] Owned first: newsletter segment, nurture sequences, in-product surfaces
- [ ] Sales: share with active deals it's relevant to — the highest-converting distribution channel nobody uses
- [ ] Social: native-format excerpts per platform, not bare links (platforms throttle outbound links)
- [ ] Communities: contribute the insight itself; link only where norms allow
- [ ] Syndication: pitch trade publications and partner newsletters
- [ ] Paid amplification on the pieces that already earned organic engagement — never on a cold one
- [ ] Refresh calendar: update and re-promote past top performers — old winners usually out-earn new posts
