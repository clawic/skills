# B2B and Enterprise — ABM, Events, Sales Interlock, Partners

Applies when `motion` is `b2b-saas` or `enterprise`. The defining constraints: a buying committee rather than a buyer, a cycle measured in months, and a small number of accounts that matter enormously.

## The Buying Committee

Enterprise deals are decided by a group, and marketing's job is to give each role the thing they need to say yes:

| Role | Wants | Asset that works |
|------|-------|------------------|
| Champion | To look right internally | A one-page business case they can forward without you |
| Economic buyer | Return, risk, and a defensible number | ROI model with your own assumptions labelled |
| End user | To not have their day made worse | Demo, trial, migration story |
| Security / IT | Compliance evidence, architecture | Trust page, certifications, DPA, questionnaire answers pre-written |
| Procurement | Terms, price benchmark, alternatives | Clean pricing, references, contract templates |
| Legal | Liability and data terms | Standard terms published, redline history known |

The most under-produced asset in B2B is the internal-champion kit: the slide the champion presents when you are not in the room. Write it from the champion's own explanation of the purchase (`positioning.md`, win/loss).

## Account-Based Marketing

ABM is a targeting discipline, not a software category. Tier by economics:

| Tier | Accounts | Approach |
|------|----------|----------|
| 1:1 | A handful, strategic | Bespoke research, custom content, exec-to-exec, named plans per account |
| 1:few | Tens, clustered by industry or use case | Segment-specific content, industry events, tailored sequences |
| 1:many | Hundreds to thousands, ICP-matched | Programmatic targeting, intent data, generic-but-relevant nurture |

Prerequisites, in order: an account list sales agrees with, contact coverage across the committee, and a joint plan per Tier-1 account. Buying an ABM platform before those three exist automates a list nobody believes in.

- **Intent data** tells you an account is researching a topic; it does not tell you who, or that they will buy. Use it to prioritize outreach order, never as a lead. Verify it against your own first-party signals before spending on it.
- Measure ABM on account engagement breadth (how many roles in the account are engaged) and pipeline per target account — never on MQLs, which is the metric ABM exists to replace.
- ABM works when the ACV justifies the cost per account. Below that, you have expensive demand gen with a fashionable name.

## Sales Interlock

- **Shared definitions in writing**: what counts as an opportunity, when a stage advances, what a rejected lead requires (`demand.md`, two-way SLA).
- **Shared number**: marketing owns pipeline created and its conversion to opportunity; sales owns opportunity to close (SKILL.md Where Experts Disagree).
- **Weekly pipeline review with marketing in the room**, listening to deal-level language. This is where message drift is caught first.
- **Enablement is a product, not an email**: pitch, objection handling, competitive battlecards, and the assets to send at each stage. Battlecards go stale in a quarter — assign an owner and a refresh date.
- **The feedback loop that matters**: every lost deal returns a reason code; every won deal returns the sentence the champion used internally.

## Events and Field Marketing

Events are the highest-cost, highest-trust channel in B2B and the easiest to run as theatre.

- Decide the goal before the booth: meetings booked, an existing-pipeline acceleration, brand presence in a category, or recruiting. Each implies a different spend.
- The economics that matter: **cost per qualified meeting**, and pipeline from meetings held — not badge scans (SKILL.md Traps). Book meetings *before* the event with the target account list; walk-up traffic is a bonus, not a plan.
- Own the follow-up window: hours, not the following week. A same-day, personalized message referencing the conversation outperforms any sequence.
- Small owned dinners and roundtables usually beat a large sponsorship at equal cost for high-ACV segments: 12 qualified people in a room for two hours is more contact time than a conference floor gives in three days.
- Track cancellations and no-shows honestly in webinar and event forecasting; registration is not attendance and attendance is not interest.

## Partner and Channel Marketing

| Partner type | What you are buying | Watch for |
|--------------|--------------------|-----------|
| Technology / integration | Access to their user base, a product reason to be mentioned | A listing with no co-marketing is a directory entry |
| Reseller / channel | Distribution and local presence | Margin stacking, and losing the customer relationship |
| Referral / affiliate | Cheap incremental leads | Paying for customers who were already arriving (`measurement.md`) |
| Agency / SI | Implementation capacity that unblocks deals | Their incentive is billable hours, not your renewal |

Rules: co-marketing works when both audiences gain something, so lead with a joint customer story rather than a joint logo; enable partners like you enable sales (they will not read a PDF — give them the deck, the demo, and the objection list); and set a single attribution and deal-registration rule in writing before the first lead, because partner attribution fights are the ugliest kind.

## Long-Cycle Realities

- Anything you start today shows up in pipeline after most of a sales cycle. Judge new programs at cycle length + reporting lag, and say the read date out loud when you launch them.
- Multi-year contracts mean this year's win rate reflects last year's positioning. Diagnose with cohorts by creation date (`diagnose.md`).
- Deal sizes are lumpy: a single enterprise deal can make a quarter look successful while the underlying funnel deteriorates. Report the median deal and the count alongside the total.
