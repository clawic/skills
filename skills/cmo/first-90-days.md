# First 90 Days — Taking Over or Auditing a Marketing Function

Marketing leadership has the shortest average tenure in the C-suite (Spencer Stuart's annual CMO tenure study puts it in the low single-digit years, consistently below other functions). The two reliable causes are inheriting an unfixable expectation and spending the first quarter on a rebrand instead of on the funnel. This file is the antidote to both.

## Week 0 — Before Accepting or Committing

Ask these before the job starts, because the answers determine whether the role is possible:

1. What is the revenue number, and who else is accountable for it?
2. What is the runway, and what is the current burn? (A twelve-month runway makes brand investment a fantasy.)
3. What has been tried and killed already, and why?
4. Who reports where — does marketing own the website, the product marketing function, the SDR team?
5. What would make this role a success in the CEO's own words, and what would make it a failure?
6. Is the product retaining customers? A retention problem cannot be out-marketed, and taking the job means owning the symptom.

Mismatch between the CEO's answer to (5) and the budget available is the most common structural trap. Name it in week one, in writing, or own it without recourse for a year.

## Days 1-30 — Listen and Instrument

- **Twenty customer conversations**: ten recent buyers, five evaluators who chose otherwise, five churned. The questions are in `diagnose.md`.
- **Listen to twenty sales calls** end to end, unedited. Nothing in a dashboard teaches faster.
- **Interview the team individually**: what is working, what is broken, what would you fix with no constraints, and what are you not allowed to say.
- **Reconcile the funnel** with the pipeline math (SKILL.md Rule 1) using the company's own trailing rates. Expect the numbers not to agree between systems; that disagreement is finding number one.
- **Instrument the cheap things immediately**: the self-reported "how did you hear about us?" field (`measurement.md`), the experiment log, the disposition reason on rejected leads.
- Change nothing structural yet, with one exception: stop spend that is provably wasted (`paid.md`, waste audit) — an early, quantified saving buys the credibility for the rest.

## Days 31-60 — Diagnose and Choose

- Write the **positioning as it exists today**, in the company's own words, and run the swap test (`positioning.md`). Circulate it. The reaction tells you whether the problem is positioning or expression.
- Produce the **one-page diagnosis**: which funnel stage is broken, what the evidence is, and what it costs. One page, three numbers, no adjectives.
- Pick **three priorities and name what dies** to fund them. A first-quarter plan with seven priorities is a resignation letter with a longer timeline.
- Rebuild the **budget from the pipeline target** (`operations.md`), not from last year's plan plus a percentage.
- Renegotiate the **MQL contract and the two-way SLA with sales** with example leads attached (`demand.md`). Do it now, while you are new and the goodwill is free.
- Set the **reporting cadence and the metric definitions** you will keep for the year (`measurement.md`). Changing definitions later reads as hiding.

## Days 61-90 — Ship and Prove

- Ship one visible improvement that the whole company feels: a homepage that passes the five-second test, a fixed lead-routing SLA, a launch that lands (`launch.md`).
- Start one channel test with a written kill line (SKILL.md Rule 6) — chosen from the Bullseye shortlist, not from what the last leader was excited about.
- Present the plan with its assumptions separated from its numbers, and name the read dates.
- Make the first team decision you have been avoiding. Delaying an obvious personnel call past 90 days makes it your decision instead of the inherited one, and costs the team's trust either way.

## The Audit Checklist (also use when evaluating a function before hiring)

| Area | What to pull | Failure signature |
|------|--------------|-------------------|
| Funnel | Stage-to-stage rates and durations, 8 quarters | Nobody can produce them from one source |
| Positioning | Homepage, deck, and three reps' pitches | Three different companies described |
| Demand | Spend and pipeline by channel, fully loaded | Only platform-reported numbers exist |
| Content | Top 20 pages by traffic and by pipeline influence | Two lists with no overlap |
| Lifecycle | Deliverability, complaint rate, list engagement (`lifecycle.md`) | No sunset policy; a large disengaged list |
| Data | Source field completeness, UTM taxonomy, CRM stage definitions | A channel called "other" carrying the largest share |
| Brand | Asset consistency, share of search trend | A refresh within the last year with no reason recorded |
| Team | Who owns which number | Roles defined by activity, not by outcome |
| Contracts | Agency terms, tool renewals, account ownership | The agency owns the ad account or the domain |
| Compliance | Consent records, claims file, disclosures (`compliance.md`) | Nobody knows where the lists came from |

## What Not To Do in the First Quarter

- No rebrand. It is the most visible way to spend a quarter and the least likely to be the problem (`brand.md`).
- No new martech purchase before the data model is understood (`operations.md`).
- No public promise of a number you have not reconciled yourself.
- No wholesale team change before you have seen each person work a real problem.
- No abandoning a channel mid-test because it was the predecessor's idea; read the kill line as written, then decide.
