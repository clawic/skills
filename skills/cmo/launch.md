# Launch — Tiering, Sequencing, Launch Day, Post-Mortem

A launch is an allocation decision before it is a communications plan: you are spending finite audience attention, and the audience does not refill on your schedule.

## Tier the Launch First

| Tier | What it is | Spend | Channels |
|------|-----------|-------|----------|
| **1 — Company moment** | New product line, category bet, rebrand, major partnership | Weeks of team time, paid amplification, press push | Everything, coordinated to a date |
| **2 — Notable release** | A capability that changes who can buy or what they pay | Days of team time, owned channels + sales enablement | Email, blog, in-product, social, sales deck update |
| **3 — Continuous** | Improvements, fixes, small features | Hours; automated | Changelog, in-product note, release digest |

Most organizations run everything at Tier 1 and exhaust the list, the press, and the team so that the real Tier 1 gets no lift (SKILL.md Traps). One to two Tier-1 moments a year is a healthy budget for most companies; the rest is Tier 2 and 3.

## Decide Before Building Anything

Five questions, answered in writing, before a single asset:
1. **Who is it for, and what do they do today instead?** (`positioning.md`)
2. **What decision are we asking for?** Try it, upgrade, book a call, switch, or merely remember.
3. **What is the number?** Signups, pipeline, upgrades — one primary metric with a target and a read date.
4. **Is the product ready for the traffic?** Onboarding, docs, pricing page, support coverage, and the ability to say yes at volume.
5. **What happens on day 30?** A launch without a sustaining plan is a spike followed by a flat line.

## Sequencing (Tier 1, working backwards from date D)

| When | Work |
|------|------|
| D−8 to D−6 weeks | Positioning and message locked; pricing and packaging final; internal naming settled |
| D−6 to D−4 weeks | Asset build: page, demo, docs, pricing update, sales deck, FAQ, objection handling |
| D−4 weeks | Design partners and beta customers briefed; quotes and case data collected with written permission |
| D−3 weeks | Press and analyst briefings under embargo (`comms.md`); creator and partner outreach |
| D−2 weeks | Internal enablement: sales, support, and success trained on the pitch and the objections |
| D−1 week | Dry run: every link, every form, every tracking parameter, the pricing page, the trial path |
| **D** | Publish in one coordinated window; owned channels first, then earned, then paid |
| D+1 to D+7 | Respond to everything; capture objections verbatim; fix the top drop-off |
| D+14 | Second wave to non-openers and non-attendees with a different angle |
| D+30 | Post-mortem with the number, the surprises, and the reusable assets |

Compress the whole table for Tier 2; skip to the changelog for Tier 3.

## Internal Launch Before External Launch

The most common cause of a flat launch is a sales team that heard about it on the website. Enablement minimum: a one-page pitch, the three qualifying questions, the top five objections with answers, a demo path, pricing changes with the discount policy, and the list of accounts to call on day one. Support needs the same, plus the known limitations — the fastest trust loss is a support agent surprised by their own product.

## Launch Day Checklist

- [ ] Page live, correct price, correct claims, substantiation on file (`compliance.md`)
- [ ] Tracking verified end to end — UTMs, conversion events, CRM source field (`measurement.md`)
- [ ] Owned first: email to the segment that will care most, in-product announcement, community post
- [ ] Sales notified with the account list and the talk track
- [ ] Press embargo lifted at the agreed hour; spokesperson available for follow-ups
- [ ] Social and community accounts staffed for the day — an unanswered question under a launch post is public
- [ ] Paid amplification pointed at the pieces already earning organic engagement, not at everything
- [ ] A named owner for the day, with the authority to pause the whole thing

## Launch Post-Mortem

Within two weeks, while the data and the memories are both fresh. Four sections: the number vs. the target; what the audience actually asked about (their questions are the real message test); what was reusable and what was thrown away; what to do differently at the next tier of the same size. Feed the objections into the FAQ and the message pillars (`positioning.md`).

## Special Cases

- **Launching on a community platform** (Product Hunt, Hacker News, subreddits, Show-and-tell channels): norms punish orchestration. Contribute a genuinely interesting artifact, be present all day answering, and never buy or solicit votes — the rule violations are visible and the reputational cost outlasts the traffic.
- **Sunsetting a product or feature**: same rigor, opposite emotion. Announce once with a firm date, a migration path, and a named contact; over-communicate to the accounts affected before anyone else hears it; never let a customer learn from a changelog that their workflow is ending.
- **Re-launching something that flopped**: change the audience or the framing, not the announcement volume. A second Tier-1 push at the same audience with the same message costs list health and buys nothing.
- **A competitor launches into your space on your date**: hold your date, drop the comparative content, and let the market do the comparing — reactive comparison posts published in the same 48 hours read as rattled.
