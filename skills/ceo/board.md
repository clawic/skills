# Board and Investor Relations

## Pre-Wiring (the real board meeting)

Consequential board decisions are made in 1:1 calls before the meeting; the meeting ratifies them. A director surprised in the room converts a business problem into a governance problem — blindsided directors start managing you instead of the plan.

Procedure:
- 3-7 days out: 15-30 min call with each director.
- Lead with the contentious item. Hear objections while you can still adapt the proposal or line up support.
- If a director will oppose, you want to know it on the call, not discover it in the room.
- 5-7 days before: full deck and materials sent (the packet lead time, canonical below). Day of: zero new information.

## Board Meeting Structure

| Section | Time | Purpose |
|---------|------|---------|
| Highlights / lowlights | 5 min | Context; confirms no surprises |
| Key metrics | 10 min | Performance vs plan |
| Strategic topics | 30 min | The 2-3 questions you need the board's brain on |
| Operations update | 10 min | Hiring, product, sales |
| Financial review | 10 min | Cash, burn, runway |
| Asks | 5 min | Specific help, named |

Rules of the room:
- Assume the deck was read; never narrate slides. If directors arrive unread, shorten the deck, don't read it to them.
- Present problems WITH your proposed response — a problem without a plan invites the board to write one for you.
- End every meeting with a closed session (directors without CEO). Make it routine so it carries no signal, then call the lead director afterward for the feedback.

## Investor Updates

**Monthly, especially when it's hard.** The first believers in your next round are the investors who watched you report bad months honestly; silence reads as failure plus concealment.

Template: one-liner on company state · the same five metrics with trend (the CEO page — → SKILL.md Quick Reference: metrics) · wins · challenges (honest) · asks (specific: intros, references, expertise) · what to watch next month.

## Difficult Board Conversations

Bad news travels by phone before it travels by deck — no director learns about a crisis from pre-read materials.

**Cash running low**: raise it while options exist — no later than `runway_alarm_months` (default 12), the band boundary defined in the canonical zone table (→ SKILL.md Quick Reference: finance). Show cut scenarios ("cut X, runway becomes Y"), propose one plan, and ask directly about bridge appetite.

**Missing plan**: own the miss without hedging, show the root cause, present a recovery plan with dated milestones. Trust is rebuilt by hitting the next three milestones, not by the apology.

**Pivot**: frame as a conclusion from data, not a confession. Show what was falsified, present the new direction with conviction, ask for explicit endorsement — a half-endorsed pivot dies in the next hard quarter.

## Board Composition

| Stage | Ideal board |
|-------|-------------|
| Seed | 3 seats: 2 founders, 1 investor or independent |
| Series A | 5 seats: 2 founders, 2 investors, 1 independent |
| Series B+ | Add independents to balance investors |
| Pre-IPO | Majority independent, audit committee ready |

Board structure is negotiated at the term sheet, never after (→ SKILL.md Quick Reference: fundraising). The independent seat is your tiebreaker — recruit it yourself, deliberately, before you need the vote.

## Managing Investors

- Updates monthly, calls quarterly minimum; relationships built before you need them.
- Know each investor's thesis, their fund's age, and remaining reserves — fund mechanics, not goodwill, determine who can bridge you. Ask before you need it.
- Ask for specific help; "any thoughts?" produces none.
- Don't over-rotate on the loudest voice — one director's pet metric is not the board's position.
- Observers have information rights, not votes or duties. Write down what they receive; an undefined observer becomes a shadow director with none of the accountability (→ SKILL.md Quick Reference: governance).

## The Board Packet

Sent 5-7 days ahead — the canonical lead time for any board or committee material — and read before the room. What belongs in it: a one-page CEO letter with the honest headline, the same five metrics every time with identical definitions (→ SKILL.md Quick Reference: metrics), financials against plan with variance explanations, an updated cash and runway view, progress on the three priorities including the one that is behind, a risk page (concentration, key-person, legal, covenant), and the two or three strategic questions you want their brains on — framed as questions with your recommendation attached.

What does not belong: new numbers on the day, unlabeled definition changes, or a metric that appears once because it was good this quarter. A board that catches one undisclosed definition change treats every subsequent number as suspect.

## A Difficult Director

Diagnose before reacting; the treatments differ:

| Pattern | What is usually happening | Move |
|---|---|---|
| Relitigates settled decisions | They lost the argument and never felt heard | Acknowledge the disagreement explicitly, restate the falsifier that would reopen it (SKILL.md rule 9) |
| Operates in your company's day-to-day | Fund pressure, or a genuine capability gap they see | Give them a scoped, real assignment; ambiguity invites freelancing |
| Consistently unprepared | The company is not a priority in their portfolio | Shorter deck, direct ask, and stop spending pre-wire time proportional to their influence |
| Pushes their fund's outcome over the company's | A real conflict of interest | Name it privately, in the language of duty, and involve the independent (→ SKILL.md Quick Reference: governance) |
| Undermines you with other directors | Loss of confidence, usually not new | Direct conversation first; then the chair or lead investor. Do not let it be discovered at a vote |

Escalation path when it does not resolve: the lead director or chair, then a board-level conversation with the independent as broker. A director cannot usually be removed except by whoever holds the right to that seat — which is why who appoints the independent is negotiated at the term sheet, not after (→ SKILL.md Quick Reference: fundraising).

## Board Health

- Ask each director annually what would make the board more useful to you, and what you should do differently. Two questions, individually, off the record.
- The board evaluates the CEO at least annually with written criteria agreed in advance. A CEO who does not ask for this ends up being evaluated informally, in a closed session, against criteria they never saw (→ SKILL.md Quick Reference: succession).
- Between meetings, use written consents for routine approvals so option grants and hires do not wait a quarter (→ SKILL.md Quick Reference: governance).
- The board is at its most useful on three things: the top team, capital allocation, and the hard call you are avoiding. Bring those and the meeting justifies itself.
