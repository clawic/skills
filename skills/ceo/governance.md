# Governance and Corporate Hygiene

Boring until it is fatal. Every item below is cheap to maintain and expensive to fix retroactively — and they get discovered at the worst possible moment: during diligence for a round or a sale, when you have no leverage and a clock.

## Fiduciary Duties in Practice

As a director you owe duty of care (be informed, deliberate properly) and duty of loyalty (the company's interest over your own). What that means operationally:

- Conflicts get disclosed and the conflicted party leaves the vote. This includes you: your own compensation, a company you also invest in, a contract with a relative.
- Decisions on major matters need a documented record — materials circulated in advance, deliberation, alternatives considered. The record is the defense; its absence is the exposure.
- The board's duty runs to the corporation and its stockholders, not to the investor who appointed a director. An investor-director advocating solely for their fund's outcome is a governance issue you should name.
- Once a sale of control becomes inevitable, the duty shifts toward maximizing value for stockholders (the Delaware Revlon line of cases) — process matters more here than in any other decision (→ SKILL.md Quick Reference: exit).
- On insolvency, creditors gain standing to bring derivative claims. Decisions made near the line get scrutinized after the fact; document reasoning contemporaneously (→ SKILL.md Quick Reference: shutdown).

## The Records That Must Exist

| Record | Cadence | Consequence of neglect |
|---|---|---|
| Board minutes | Every meeting, drafted within 2 weeks, approved at the next | No evidence of process; option grants of uncertain validity |
| Written consents for grants, hires, financings | Per action | Options granted without board approval are voidable — a routine diligence finding |
| Signed stock and option ledger, single source of truth | Continuous | Cap table disputes at the worst moment (→ SKILL.md Quick Reference: exit) |
| IP assignment from every founder, employee, contractor | At start of engagement, no exceptions | The most common small-deal blocker: a contractor owns part of your product |
| Executed employment and contractor agreements | At start | Misclassification exposure, unclear notice terms |
| Corporate filings, franchise tax, registered agent | Annually | Administrative dissolution — which does happen, quietly |
| Insurance: D&O, E&O, cyber, employment practices | Annual review | D&O before your first outside director; late notice on any policy voids coverage (→ SKILL.md Quick Reference: crisis) |

## Equity Administration

- **409A valuation** (US): refresh at least every 12 months and on any material event — a new priced round, an acquisition offer, a major change in results. Grants priced off a stale 409A create tax exposure for your employees, which is your problem, not theirs.
- **Board approval before communication.** The grant date is the approval date; promising a number in an offer letter and approving it three months later is a repricing problem.
- **83(b) elections**: 30 days from grant, no extensions, and this catches founders and early employees with restricted stock (→ SKILL.md Quick Reference: cofounders).
- **Option exercise window**: the standard 90 days post-termination means a departing employee must find cash and pay tax to keep what they earned. Extending it (to 5-10 years) is a real, one-time governance decision with real tax consequences for the employee (ISO status converts) — decide it deliberately and apply it consistently.
- **The pool**: sized against an 18-month hiring plan; a pool created pre-money dilutes only existing holders (→ SKILL.md Quick Reference: fundraising).
- **QSBS eligibility** (US, §1202) depends on entity type, gross assets at issuance, and holding period, and the rules were amended in 2025. Before any restructuring, reincorporation, or redemption, ask counsel what it does to §1202 — the mistake is unrecoverable.

## Employment and Compliance

- **Contractor versus employee** is decided by how the work happens, not by the contract's label. Full-time, directed, integrated workers who were paid on invoices generate back taxes, penalties, and benefits claims.
- **Multi-state and multi-country employment**: one remote hire creates payroll registration, tax nexus, and sometimes sales-tax obligations in that jurisdiction. Know where you have employees before finance discovers it (→ SKILL.md Quick Reference: international).
- **Compliance regimes** worth planning a quarter ahead of the deal that requires them: SOC 2 Type II requires an observation window (commonly 3-12 months) — you cannot start it the week enterprise procurement asks; GDPR and similar regimes need a lawful basis, DPAs with subprocessors, and a breach process (→ SKILL.md Quick Reference: crisis); HIPAA, PCI, and sector rules gate entire market segments.
- **Data retention and deletion policy**: adopt one and follow it. Selective deletion after a dispute begins is spoliation; a consistently applied policy is a defense.

## Board Operations

- Committees when the board reaches ~5 members or a raise requires them: audit (financial oversight, auditor relationship) and compensation (exec pay, equity grants — and the CEO does not sit on their own comp decision).
- Independent directors: recruit them yourself, deliberately, before you need the tiebreaking vote. Who selects the independent is the negotiation (→ SKILL.md Quick Reference: board).
- Meeting materials circulated 5-7 days in advance, the same lead time as the board packet (→ SKILL.md Quick Reference: board); decisions requiring a vote flagged as such in the agenda so nobody claims surprise.
- Written consent for routine approvals between meetings keeps grants and hires from waiting a quarter — but consents still need to be signed, filed, and reflected in the ledger.
- Observers are not directors: they have no vote and no duty, and information rights should be written, not assumed.

## The Diligence-Readiness Drill

Once a year, spend a day assembling what a buyer or lead investor would ask for: cap table with every instrument, all board minutes and consents, IP assignments for everyone who ever wrote code, executed customer and vendor contracts with change-of-control clauses flagged, financials that tie to the bank, tax filings, insurance, and a list of every jurisdiction you have employees or customers in. Whatever is missing takes weeks to reconstruct — find out on a calm day, not during a signed LOI with an outside date (→ SKILL.md Quick Reference: exit).
