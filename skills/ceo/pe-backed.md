# Sponsor-Backed CEO — PE Boards, Covenants, and the Clock

A private-equity-backed CEO is graded against a different scoreboard than a venture CEO, on a shorter clock, with a board that owns control rather than a minority stake. Advice that transfers from venture will get you fired in year two.

## What Actually Differs

| Dimension | Venture-backed | Sponsor-backed |
|---|---|---|
| Scoreboard | Growth rate against the next round's bar | EBITDA growth and deleveraging against the value-creation plan |
| Board | Minority investors, advisory posture | Control owner; the board can replace you at will |
| Failure mode | Running out of cash | Breaching a covenant |
| Time horizon | Next round, 18-24 months | Hold period, typically 3-7 years, with an exit already imagined |
| Your equity | Founder shares | A management incentive plan, usually vesting on time plus a return hurdle |
| Reporting | Monthly update, quarterly board | Monthly pack on a deadline, weekly flash in the first 100 days, plus ad-hoc requests |

The single mental shift: the sponsor is not evaluating your company, they are evaluating their return on it. Every proposal lands better when it is expressed as its effect on EBITDA, cash conversion, or exit multiple.

## The First 100 Days With a New Sponsor

- The value-creation plan exists before you were hired or re-signed. Get it in writing, with the underwriting assumptions — the model that justified the purchase price is the document you are actually being measured against.
- Reconcile it to reality immediately. If an assumption is wrong, say so in month one, when it is their model's error; in month nine it becomes your miss.
- Expect a 100-day plan with workstreams, owners, and weekly reporting. Staff it rather than resisting it; the reporting burden is real and permanent, and a CFO who can produce the pack on time is your most important hire (→ SKILL.md Quick Reference: people).
- Establish which decisions need sponsor consent versus notification. Get the delegation of authority in writing early; discovering a consent right mid-deal is how CEOs lose credibility (→ SKILL.md Quick Reference: governance).

## Debt Is the Real Governor

Leveraged structures mean the balance sheet, not the market, sets the pace.

- Know your covenants precisely: the leverage ratio (net debt / EBITDA), fixed-charge or interest coverage, minimum liquidity, and the exact definition of "EBITDA" in the credit agreement — it is a negotiated definition, not the accounting one.
- Model headroom monthly on a rolling forward basis, not on the last-reported quarter. Covenant tests hit on trailing twelve-month figures, so a weak quarter enters the calculation and stays for a year.
- Approaching a breach: go to the lender early with a plan. Lenders forgive bad news delivered ahead of time and price late news punitively — waivers, amendments, and fees are available to a CEO who called in advance, and rarely to one who did not (→ SKILL.md Quick Reference: crisis).
- Growth investments compete with debt service in a way they never did with venture money. Every spend proposal needs a payback period the sponsor recognizes, typically inside the hold.

## Add-On M&A

Most sponsor theses include acquisitions to build scale and arbitrage the multiple (buy small at a lower multiple, exit combined at a higher one). You will be asked to source and integrate them.

- Integration capacity is the constraint, not deal flow. Two acquisitions a year is a lot for a company that has never done one (→ SKILL.md Quick Reference: acquisitions).
- Quality of earnings work on the target is done by the sponsor's diligence provider; your job is the operational read they cannot do — whether the customers stay, whether the systems merge, whether the team is real.
- Synergy numbers in the model become your targets. Negotiate them before the deal closes, because you will be reported against them for the rest of the hold.

## Your Own Economics

- The management incentive plan (MIP) is a pool of equity or options for the leadership team, commonly in the high single digits to low teens as a share of equity, allocated by the sponsor with CEO input. Ask three questions before you sign: what percentage is mine, what does it vest on (time, MOIC or IRR hurdles, or both), and what happens on a sale, a recapitalization, or my termination without cause.
- Understand leverage's effect on your outcome: in a levered structure the equity value is highly sensitive to the exit multiple and to debt paydown, so a modest EBITDA improvement can be worth a great deal — and a modest miss can wipe the equity out entirely.
- Rolling equity from a prior sale into the new deal is a real investment decision, not a loyalty gesture. Evaluate it as one.
- Get the terms reviewed by counsel who does MIPs. Terms differ enormously between sponsors and are rarely negotiable after signing.

## Managing the Sponsor Relationship

- No surprises, at a higher frequency than a venture board: the deal partner should hear bad news by phone the day you know it (SKILL.md rule 6).
- Learn who actually decides. The deal partner, the operating partner, and the investment committee have different concerns; a proposal that survives all three is written for all three.
- Operating partners are a resource, not an inspection. Give them a real problem to own and the relationship improves permanently.
- The exit clock is always running. From roughly 18 months before the intended exit, everything you do is read through what it looks like in a data room: clean financials, contracted recurring revenue, low customer concentration (→ SKILL.md Quick Reference: customers), documented processes, and a management team that is not just you (→ SKILL.md Quick Reference: exit).
- If your read is that the plan cannot be hit, say it early with an alternative. Sponsors replace CEOs for missing quietly, far more often than for missing.
