# Pivot — Persevere, Change, or Stop

Most companies do not die from picking the wrong direction; they die from taking eighteen months to admit it and then having no runway to act on the admission.

## Decide Whether You Have Evidence or Fatigue

Pivot on falsified assumptions, not on a bad quarter or a founder's boredom. Write the assumption that has been falsified and the observation that falsified it. If you cannot write the sentence, you have fatigue, and the answer is a plan change, not a pivot.

| Signal | Reads as | Test |
|---|---|---|
| Retention curve decays to zero in every cohort | No value delivered; nothing to build on | Do any cohorts flatten? A flattening cohort is a segment, and a segment is a business |
| Growth only where you personally sell | Founder charisma, not product pull | Hand three deals to someone else; do they close? |
| Customers renew but nobody expands | A feature, not a platform | Is the ceiling the segment, the pricing, or the product (→ SKILL.md Quick Reference: pricing)? |
| Sales cycle keeps stretching quarter over quarter | Budget owner does not feel the pain | Ask who gets fired if this problem persists. If nobody, there is no budget |
| Usage strong, willingness to pay zero | Value real, capture wrong | Try the price change before the pivot — it is reversible and takes weeks |
| The team keeps proposing new markets | You are already pivoting, informally | Force the choice into the open before it fragments the roadmap |
| Nobody can name what you learned this quarter | Not iterating — waiting | Set a pre-committed decision date, now |

**Pre-commit the criteria before the experiment**, not after: the metric, the threshold, and the date. "If paid conversion is under 3% by March 31 with 200 signups, we change segments." Criteria written after the data is in are always met.

## The Pivot Types (Ries)

Naming the type prevents the most expensive version — changing everything at once and learning nothing.

| Type | You keep | You change |
|---|---|---|
| Zoom-in | One feature everyone uses | The rest of the product becomes the product |
| Zoom-out | The whole product | It becomes a feature of something larger |
| Customer segment | The product | Who you sell it to |
| Customer need | The customer | The problem you solve for them |
| Platform | The technology | App becomes platform, or the reverse |
| Business architecture | Capability | High-margin/low-volume ↔ low-margin/high-volume |
| Value capture | Everything | How you monetize (→ SKILL.md Quick Reference: pricing) |
| Engine of growth | Product | Viral, paid, or sticky loop |
| Channel | Product | How it reaches the buyer |
| Technology | Problem and customer | How you solve it — usually cost or performance |

Rule: change one axis at a time where the runway allows it. Two axes at once means a failure teaches you nothing about which one was wrong. The exception is a genuine restart, where pretending otherwise wastes months.

## Can You Afford It

- Budget 6-12 months from decision to a new signal, and more for enterprise sales cycles. Enter with at least months-to-signal + 6 of runway at the post-pivot burn, or the pivot is really a fundraising plan with a story attached (SKILL.md rule 4).
- If runway is short, the sequence inverts: cut first to buy the time, then pivot (→ SKILL.md Quick Reference: layoffs). Pivoting at 5 months of cash produces a half-executed idea and a desperate raise.
- Cash and team are the two assets that survive a pivot. Protect both in the sizing; a pivot that loses the three people who could execute it is a shutdown with extra steps (→ SKILL.md Quick Reference: shutdown).

## Executing It

1. Decide with a pre-mortem and one named decision-maker (→ SKILL.md Quick Reference: decisions). A pivot decided by consensus gets relitigated at the first bad month.
2. Pre-wire the board individually before any deck exists. You want the objections while the plan is still editable (→ SKILL.md Quick Reference: board).
3. Write the memo: what we believed, what we observed, what we now believe, what we will do for the next two quarters, what would prove us wrong. Two pages, no hedging.
4. Announce internally in one meeting, with the memo. Frame it as a conclusion drawn from data, not a confession — conviction is what the team is deciding whether to follow (→ SKILL.md Quick Reference: communication).
5. Kill the old thing on a date, in writing. A pivot with the old product still on life support splits the team's attention and both efforts starve.
6. Expect and accept departures: people joined the old mission. Talk to your key people individually within 48 hours, before they hear it in a group.
7. Existing customers get a support commitment with an end date, an honest migration story, or a refund. What you do here is the reference call for your next company.
8. Set the next decision date the same day you announce. A pivot with no review date is how a company arrives at the same problem twice.

## Anti-Patterns

- **The zombie pivot**: new pitch, same product, same customers. Only the deck changed.
- **The serial pivot**: three directions in a year. The team stops believing any of them, and investors read velocity as panic — after the second, the market's read is that the founders cannot choose.
- **Pivoting away from the hard part**: switching to a market where you have no advantage because the current one is difficult. Where-to-play needs a how-to-win (→ SKILL.md Quick Reference: strategy).
- **The quiet pivot**: strategy changes but nobody tells the team, so half the roadmap still serves the old thesis.
- **Pivoting on the loudest customer**: one prospect's promise is not a segment (→ SKILL.md Quick Reference: customers).
- **Sunk-cost persistence**: "we've spent two years on this" is an argument for stopping, not continuing (→ SKILL.md Quick Reference: decisions).

## When The Answer Is Stop, Not Pivot

No falsifiable new thesis, no cash to test one, and no team that wants to — that is not a pivot candidate. Returning remaining capital beats spending it on a direction nobody believes in, and it is the version of this that investors remember well (→ SKILL.md Quick Reference: shutdown).
