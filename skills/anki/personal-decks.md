# Personal Decks — Anki Outside School

Most Anki writing assumes a student. The non-academic decks are smaller, longer-lived, and fail differently: nobody grades you, so a badly designed personal deck is quietly abandoned instead of loudly failed. Design them to survive indifference.

## What Works Off-Campus

| Deck | Card shape | The detail that makes it work |
|---|---|---|
| Names and faces | Photo → name; name → role/context | Two cards per person; add the context you will meet them in ("standup, backend team"), or the name recalls nothing useful |
| Colleagues, clients, org charts | Person → their remit, their manager, their project | Suspend when they leave; this deck rots faster than any other |
| Poetry, scripture, speeches | Progressive cloze over consecutive lines | Blank one line at a time with the previous line visible; whole-stanza cards test nothing |
| Music theory | Key signature → accidentals; interval → sound name; chord → notes | Audio on the answer turns theory into recognition |
| Chess openings | Position image → the move | Cards store the move, the board teaches the plan; without play they become trivia |
| Birds, plants, mushrooms, wine | Image → species/name; and feature → species | The distinguishing feature card is the one that transfers to the field |
| Geography | Image Occlusion over a map | Occlude one region per card, keep the neighbours visible for orientation |
| Professional facts (dosages, codes, part numbers, regulations) | Fact → value, and value → what it applies to | Tag by revision cycle: regulated facts change and must be re-carded (`volatile::`) |
| Recipes and techniques | Ratio → dish, technique → its one constraint | Card the ratios and temperatures, not the procedures |
| Trivia and general knowledge | Anything, as long as intake stays capped | The only deck where fun is the criterion — enforce a low `new_per_day` |

## Names And Faces (the highest-payoff personal deck)

1. Capture the photo and the context on the same day you meet the person; memory of the context evaporates within a week.
2. Two cards: photo → name (recognition, the one you actually need) and name → who they are (production, for the meeting you are walking into).
3. Add a distinguishing feature to the answer field — not to the question, or you will recognize the description instead of the face.
4. Suspend rather than delete when someone leaves your life; unsuspending later costs nothing.
5. Privacy: photos of real people live in your collection and sync to AnkiWeb. Decide that deliberately before building the deck.

## Memorizing Long Text

Progressive cloze, one line per cloze number, previous line visible:

```
{{c1::Whose woods these are I think I know.}}
{{c2::His house is in the village though;}}
{{c3::He will not see me stopping here}}
```

The chain is learned as a chain, which is how recitation actually works. Add a card for the first line alone — starting is the step people freeze on. Delivery, timing, and breath are performance and belong to rehearsal, not to Anki (SKILL.md, When Anki Is The Wrong Tool).

## Keeping A Personal Deck Alive

- **Cap intake hard.** 5-10 new/day is plenty; a personal deck with 40 new/day becomes a chore in three weeks and dies (SKILL.md rule 5).
- **Attach it to an existing routine** rather than a reminder: the review happens where the phone is already in your hand.
- **Delete without guilt.** No exam is coming; a card that stopped being useful is pure cost (SKILL.md rule 8).
- **One collection, separate decks.** Splitting personal material into a second profile means a second sync, a second backup, and one of them will be forgotten.
- **Expect long intervals** — that is the point. A personal deck at year two shows a handful of cards a day, which is what a maintained memory costs.

## Traps

| Trap | Why it fails | Do instead |
|---|---|---|
| A "life facts" deck of everything | Unbounded intake with no deadline forcing discipline | Pick the decks above with a purpose you can name |
| Photos of people without deciding about sync | Personal images replicate to a cloud account | Decide first, then build |
| Carding a hobby you have just started | You do not yet know what matters; most cards get deleted | Practise for a month, then card what you keep looking up |
| Chess/music cards instead of playing | Recall without performance transfers poorly | Cards as a supplement to practice, never a replacement |
| Trivia deck at 30 new/day | Crowds out decks with a purpose | Low cap, and it is the first deck to cut when workload bites |
