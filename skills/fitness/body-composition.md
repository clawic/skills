# Body Composition — Fat-Loss and Muscle-Gain Phases

Training-side management of cutting, gaining, and reading the scale. Calorie targets, macro math, and meal planning route to `calories`; protein defaults are canonical in SKILL.md (1.6-2.2 g/kg/day, Morton).

## Rate Targets

| Phase | Rate | Signal it's too fast |
|---|---|---|
| Fat loss | 0.5-1% bodyweight/week (weekly averages) | Lifts dropping across the board, sleep degrading — leaner and smaller people use the low end (Helms) |
| Muscle gain | 0.25-0.5% bodyweight/month; beginners up to ~1% | Waist growing faster than lifts — surplus feeding fat, trim it |
| Recomp | No scale target; e1RM up + waist flat is the win condition | — |

Recomp (gaining muscle and losing fat at once) is realistic for beginners, returners (muscle memory, SKILL.md Recovery and Readiness), and higher body-fat trainees — for lean intermediates, pick a phase instead of chasing both.

## Training in a Deficit

- Intensity is the muscle-retention signal: keep loads heavy, cut volume. Maintenance needs as little as one-third of building volume when intensity stays (Bickel dose-response maintenance work; older trainees need the upper end of that fraction).
- Practical: hold the Rule 4 floor (~10 hard sets/muscle/week) in a mild deficit; in an aggressive cut, drop toward maintenance volume before dropping load.
- Expect double progression to slow or pause in a deficit — a flat e1RM while losing weight is relative strength going UP, and gets reported as progress, not a stall. An active deficit is already step 2 of the stall diagnostic (SKILL.md).
- Cardio for the deficit: Zone 2 and steps, not extra HIIT — hard intervals compete with lifting recovery precisely when recovery capacity is lowest.

## Training in a Surplus

- The surplus is the time to push volume: progress within the Rule 4 corridor and run accumulation blocks while recovery is subsidized.
- Rate check monthly, not weekly — muscle-gain rates are inside scale noise on any shorter window.

## Reading the Scale

- Daily weigh-in under consistent conditions (morning, post-bathroom), compared as **weekly averages**. Day-to-day swings of 1-2% bodyweight are water, glycogen, and gut content — never adjust anything on a single day.
- New deficit week 1 overstates loss (glycogen water); first surplus week overstates gain. Judge from week 2.
- Adjustment trigger: two consecutive weekly averages flat against the phase target → adjust intake (route to `calories`) or activity, one lever at a time. The lift log arbitrates which: lifts strong + scale flat in a cut = intake lever; lifts fading = recovery problem first.
- Complementary cheap metrics: waist measurement weekly, same conditions; progress photos monthly. Bodyweight + waist + e1RM trend together beat any body-fat-percentage gadget reading.

## Phase Duration and Exits

- Cuts: cap continuous deficits around 12-16 weeks, then hold maintenance 2-4 weeks before cutting again — adherence and training quality decay on longer runs; the break is a default, not a law.
- Exit any phase into 1-2 weeks at maintenance, not straight into the opposite phase; post-cut, expect 1-2 kg of water/glycogen regain that is not fat — pre-warn or it reads as failure.
- Gaining phases end by waist trigger or by calendar (the user's stated tolerance), whichever comes first.

## Escalation

Rapid unexplained weight loss without a deficit, signs of disordered eating patterns (compensatory over-exercise, fear-driven restriction), or a BMI already low with a requested cut → outside this skill's lane: advise clinician or registered dietitian, keep training guidance at maintenance meanwhile.
