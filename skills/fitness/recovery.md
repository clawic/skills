# Recovery — Deloads, Readiness, Overtraining

Managing fatigue so training keeps working. Canonical thresholds in SKILL.md: readiness check and DOMS-vs-injury test (Recovery and Readiness), deload trigger and protocol (Rule 7), the overtraining Red Flags row.

## Running a Deload Week

Rule 7 defines it (1 week at 50% of normal set count, same loads). Execution details that get botched:

- Keep the loads. Halving weight AND sets makes the week a vacation; the point is maintaining the movement pattern and intensity signal while slashing fatigue. Sets stay at RIR 3-4.
- Keep the schedule: same days, shorter sessions. Skipping the week entirely restarts habit friction and reads as a gap in the log.
- Cardio deloads in parallel: volume −30%, no hard sessions.
- Exit: resume at pre-deload loads (or 90% of a stall load when the deload was plateau-driven — SKILL.md diagnostic order). Expect the first session back to feel oddly heavy; that is normal and gone by session two.

## Readiness Decision Tree

The three-item check (resting HR within 5 bpm, sleep 6h+, appetite normal) is canonical in SKILL.md. Decisions:

| Result | Action |
|---|---|
| 3/3 pass | Train as planned |
| 2/3 pass | Train as planned; autoregulate load −5% if the first work set feels heavy |
| 1/3 pass | Convert to Zone 2 cardio or technique work (canonical) |
| 0/3, or fever/illness signs | Rest day; below-neck symptoms (fever, chest, GI) mean full rest until recovered |

One converted session is free. The pattern to escalate: 3+ conversions in a week → treat as a Rule 7 trigger regardless of lift numbers.

## The Overtraining Ladder

Fatigue states are a continuum; the response differs by rung:

| Rung | Signals | Timescale to recover | Response |
|---|---|---|---|
| Functional overreaching | Planned hard block ends with performance dip, mood intact | Days | Normal — the Rule 7 deload absorbs it |
| Non-functional overreaching | Performance down 2+ weeks, sleep quality falling, motivation down, RHR elevated | Weeks | Deload, then halve volume for 2 weeks; audit life stress and calories |
| Overtraining syndrome | Months of decline despite rest; diagnosis of exclusion | Months | Red Flags row: suspend hard training, clinician if unresolved in 2 weeks |

The ladder is climbed by ignoring the previous rung, not by any single session. Genuine OTS is rare in recreational trainees; persistent symptoms more often mean illness, anemia, or thyroid issues — hence the clinician routing.

## Stress-Adjusted Training

- High life stress (work crunch, new baby, exam period): cut volume ~30-50%, keep frequency and intensity. Three short sessions that happen beat one perfect plan that doesn't; the habit is the asset being protected.
- Never stack a planned hard block on top of a foreseeable life crunch — move the accumulation block, don't fight the calendar.

## Sleep

- The stall-diagnosis threshold (average under 7h) is canonical in SKILL.md. Chronic short sleep also shifts body-composition results toward muscle loss in a deficit — it silently taxes both goals.
- Fix behavioral basics inside this skill's lane (consistent schedule, training earlier when late sessions wreck sleep onset); persistent insomnia or suspected apnea routes to the `sleep` skill.

## What Recovery Modalities Are Worth

- Load-bearing: sleep, food (protein per SKILL.md defaults), and dosage control. Nothing below substitutes for them.
- Cheap and fine: walking, Zone 1 spins, light mobility on off days — they cost nothing and help subjective soreness.
- Cold plunges after lifting: post-exercise cold water immersion blunts hypertrophy signaling (Roberts) — keep cold exposure away from the hours after resistance work if muscle is the goal; fine after pure cardio.
- Massage guns, compression, saunas: comfort tools; use them for feel, never count them as recovery capacity in programming decisions.
