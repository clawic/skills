# Electrolytes — Sodium, ORS, Sports Drinks

Frame: water follows sodium. Replacing large losses with plain water dilutes serum sodium — the mechanism behind exercise hyponatremia (exercise.md) and the reason GI illness needs ORS, not water (illness.md). Everyday life needs none of this: food sodium covers normal sweat.

## When Sodium Enters the Picture

| Situation | Play |
|---|---|
| Session over 90 min, or heavy sweating | Drink with 400-700 mg sodium per liter during (canonical band in exercise.md) |
| Heavy losses, no meal afterward | Sodium option from the ranking below, inside the 125-150% replacement (exercise.md) |
| Vomiting or diarrhea | ORS — sports drinks are under-salted for GI losses (illness.md; ranking below) |
| Fast over 24 h | Sodium supplementation required; protocol → fasting skill |
| First 1-2 weeks of keto or low-carb | Falling insulin dumps sodium and water — salt food deliberately; the rapid scale drop is water, not fat |
| Desk life, sessions under an hour | Nothing — a normal diet covers it; "electrolyte water" here is taste, not therapy |

## Reading a Salty Sweater

- Signals: white crust on dark clothing or skin after sweat dries, sweat that stings the eyes, cramping despite adequate volume.
- Sweat sodium spans roughly 500-1500 mg per liter across people and is largely individual — two athletes on the same run can need double each other's salt.
- Acclimatization lowers sweat sodium while raising sweat volume (environment.md): week-one-in-the-heat salt needs are the peak, not the baseline.

## Oral Rehydration Solution

- Gold standard for GI losses: WHO reduced-osmolarity ORS, ~75 mmol sodium per liter (~1700 mg) — roughly 4× a sports drink. Pharmacy sachets are cheap; stock them before they're needed.
- Home recipe (WHO): 1 L safe water + 6 level teaspoons sugar + 1/2 level teaspoon salt. Taste check: like tears, not like soup. Level spoons matter — oversalted ORS is dangerous for children.
- The sugar is not for energy: glucose is what carries sodium (and water with it) across the gut wall. Sugar-free "ORS" is a contradiction.
- Delivery when nauseated: 5-10 ml sips every 5-10 minutes (illness.md).

## Ranking the Options for Heavy Losses

| Drink | Sodium per liter | Verdict |
|---|---|---|
| ORS | ~1700 mg | GI illness; overkill for workouts |
| Salted broth / bouillon | Varies; a cube per cup lands near ORS strength | Fever and GI comfort option that also feeds (illness.md) |
| Sports drink (Gatorade class) | ~450 mg | In-session for 90-min+ efforts — low end of the 400-700 band; under-salted for GI losses |
| DIY: 1 L water + 1/4 tsp salt + ~60 g sugar or juice | ~575 mg | Cheapest in-band session drink; the 1/4 tsp is the whole trick |
| Milk | ~400 mg, plus protein and lactose | Best-retained post-session drink without a meal (index below) |
| Flat soda | ~30 mg | Stomach-bug folklore: all sugar, no salt — skip |
| Plain water | 0 | Everyday default; wrong for every row above it |

## Retention — the Beverage Hydration Index

- Maughan's beverage hydration index: 2 h after drinking, milk and ORS leave measurably more of the fluid in the body than still water (~50% better retention); the drivers are sodium and energy content slowing gastric emptying.
- Practical use: after heavy one-shot losses with no meal coming, pick milk or ORS over plain water (SKILL.md Where Experts Disagree). With a meal, plain water ties — food is the electrolyte source.
- Coffee and tea within the 400 mg caffeine ceiling retain like water (Rule 2); the index is why "coffee dehydrates" stays folklore.

## Traps

- Salt tablets swallowed dry or with a sip: concentrated salt pulls water INTO the gut — nausea, not hydration. Dissolve them, or take with a full glass.
- Chasing potassium or magnesium for sweat: losses are small and food covers them; the magnesium-for-cramps evidence is weak. Cramp play = salt + fluid + rest; repeated cramping → sweat-rate test (exercise.md).
- Dosing sodium per day instead of per liter: in-drink concentration (mg/L) is what governs absorption and palatability; daily totals belong to diet, and on a clinician-set sodium restriction (conditions.md) the day total is the clinician's call — flag the conflict, don't resolve it.
