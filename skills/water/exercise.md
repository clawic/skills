# Exercise — Sweat Rate, Replacement, and the Overdrinking Line

Frame: on training days hydration is a timing problem before it is a volume problem — the gut absorbs about 1 L per hour sustained (Rule 6), so a large deficit cannot be fixed inside a session, and a large surplus is the dangerous direction.

## Before

- ACSM pre-hydration: 5-7 ml per kg in the ~4 h before a hard or hot session (70 kg → 350-490 ml). Urine still dark 2 h out → add another 3-5 ml per kg.
- Do not pre-load beyond that: extra water pre-session is urinated out mid-workout, not stored.

## During

| Session | Drink |
|---|---|
| Under 60 min, moderate | Nothing required; thirst is fine |
| 60-90 min or heavy sweat | Water on a schedule — 150-250 ml every 15-20 min lands inside the +500-1000 ml per sweaty hour band |
| Over 90 min, or a salty sweater | Switch to a drink with 400-700 mg sodium per liter (electrolytes.md) |
| Over 4 h (ultra, century ride, all-day hike) | Drink to thirst plus scheduled sodium — overdrinking is the bigger risk (below) |

## The Sweat-Rate Test (regular trainers, once per sport and season)

sweat rate (L/h) = (pre-weight kg − post-weight kg + fluid drunk L) / hours

Example: 72.0 kg before, 71.2 after a 1 h run while drinking 0.5 L → (0.8 + 0.5)/1 = 1.3 L/h.

- Weigh in the same dry state both times: towel off sweat, empty bladder first — wet clothing and a full bladder are the two errors that flip results.
- Store the result in memory.md Baseline per sport and season; a summer rate can be double the winter rate, so one number is not enough.
- A measured rate replaces the generic +500-1000 ml per sweaty hour band for that user — that band is the fallback, not the ceiling.

## After

- Replace 125-150% of the net mass lost over the next 2-4 hours (0.8 kg lost → 1000-1200 ml), not all at once (Shirreffs) — the overshoot covers urine losses that continue during rehydration.
- With a meal, plain water works: food supplies the sodium. Without a meal after heavy losses, pick a sodium option from electrolytes.md.
- No scale available: pale-straw urine within ~4 h of finishing says done (Rule 4).

## Overdrinking — the Direction That Kills

- Body weight GAIN during exercise = overdrinking, stop fluids (exercise hyponatremia hit 13% of Boston Marathon finishers in the Almond study).
- Risk profile: slower finishers drinking at every station, small body mass, events over 4 h, plain water only, "more is better" beliefs.
- Headache, nausea, bloating, or confusion during or after a long event with heavy drinking → Red Flags (SKILL.md): emergency care, do NOT give more water.
- Drink-to-thirst is the hyponatremia-consensus defense (Hew-Butler) for recreational endurance athletes; programmed drinking belongs to short hard efforts in heat, not six-hour marathons (SKILL.md Where Experts Disagree).

## Special Cases

- Gym and strength sessions: sweat rates sit at the low end; the bottom half of the +500-1000 ml per sweaty hour band covers a typical hour of lifting — no sports drink needed.
- Hot yoga and sauna suits: the scale loss is water, not fat (SKILL.md Traps); replace per the 125-150% rule and expect the weight back.
- Two-a-days: replacement between sessions is the limiter. Weigh before session two — still down more than 2% of morning weight (Rule 5's performance line) → shorten or ease the session.
- Kids' sports: children heat up faster and under-drink; scheduled breaks every 15-20 min with the bottle in hand; illness or vulnerability signs → Red Flags.
- Weight-class athletes cutting water: deliberate dehydration protocols are out of scope — past the 2% performance line the health cost climbs; route to their coach and clinician.
