# Environment — Heat, Cold, Altitude, Flights

The Rule 1 target assumes temperate indoor life. Environments shift it through one mechanism worth naming to the user: unnoticed losses — sweat that evaporates instantly, breath moisture into dry air — so thirst under-reports exactly when losses rise.

## Heat

- Above 30 C ambient or direct sun: +250-500 ml per day of exposure (canonical row in SKILL.md Target Adjustments). Low end for office-plus-commute days, high end for hours outdoors.
- Hard labor in heat sweats 1-2 L per hour — treat as exercise, not weather: the exercise.md replacement rules and sodium thresholds apply.
- Humid heat: sweat drips instead of evaporating, so cooling fails while losses continue — losses are equal or higher than in dry heat despite feeling "just sticky, not thirsty".
- Acclimatization: over roughly 1-2 weeks of repeated heat exposure, sweat volume rises while its sodium content drops — the adapted body needs MORE water and LESS salt than in week one. Regular trainers: re-run the sweat-rate test (exercise.md) after acclimatizing.
- `climate: hot` in config applies the heat adjustment as a standing baseline — no daily weather questions.

## Sauna

- A typical 15-20 min round costs roughly 0.3-0.5 kg of sweat. Replace after the last round per the exercise 125-150% rule; drinking between rounds is comfort, not obligation.
- Multiple rounds, or sauna stacked after training, compound losses — log them as exercise-equivalent.
- Alcohol around sauna both dehydrates and blunts the body's heat response: count it 0 ml (Rule 2) and note the combination.
- Scale readings after sauna are water, not progress (SKILL.md Traps).

## Cold

- Cold-induced diuresis: vasoconstriction pushes urine out while thirst is simultaneously blunted — winter users lose water without ever feeling dry.
- Layered clothing during winter sport traps real sweat: log winter training with the full exercise adjustment, not a discount.
- Heated indoor air is dry all winter — a mild, steady respiratory loss; the fix is environmental (bottle at the desk, habits.md), not reminders.

## Altitude

- Above 2500 m: faster breathing of dry air plus altitude diuresis raise losses, most in the first days. Expect a higher target; urine color (Rule 4) still governs.
- Overhydration does not prevent altitude sickness (Wilderness Medical Society) — drink for the losses, not as an AMS charm; forcing liters on summit days adds hyponatremia risk instead.
- A headache at altitude is AMS until proven otherwise, not merely dehydration — route to mountain-medicine guidance, do not answer it with a water target.

## Flights

- Cabin humidity runs 10-20% versus 30-60% indoors; breath and skin losses accumulate over hours.
- +250 ml per hour in the air (estimate; canonical row in SKILL.md Target Adjustments). A 10 h long-haul is a ~2.5 L addition spread over the day — board with a filled bottle, security allows empties.
- The real trap is voluntary dehydration: passengers skip drinking to avoid climbing over seatmates. Name it and front-load before boarding rather than pretending sipping will happen mid-flight.
- Onboard drinks follow Rule 2: coffee and juice count at 100%, alcohol at 0 ml. Jet-lagged mornings mute thirst cues — schedule the first 500 ml for landing-day morning.

Illness in any of these environments (fever in heat, GI trouble while traveling): illness.md, and Red Flags first.
