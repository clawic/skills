# Quality — Tap, Bottled, Filters, Travel Safety

Frame: which water, not how much. In most developed-country municipal systems, tap water is tested more often than bottled — the honest drivers are taste and context, not fear. Nothing here changes targets; SKILL.md rules govern amounts.

## Tap vs Bottled

- Municipal tap in developed countries: regulated, frequently tested, the default. Bottled wins only on taste preference, an unreliable local supply, or an active advisory.
- Chlorine-taste complaint: fill a pitcher and refrigerate — chlorine off-gasses within hours — or use a carbon filter. Either beats a bottled-water habit by orders of magnitude on cost.
- Sparkling hydrates like still (Rule 2). Mildly acidic for enamel but nowhere near soda; on a sodium restriction, check labels — some European sparkling brands carry real sodium (conditions.md).
- Mineral and hard water contribute some calcium and magnesium, but food dwarfs it — choose by taste; limescale is a kettle problem, not a health problem.
- RO or distilled water is fine to drink on a normal diet; food supplies the minerals.

## Household Pipes

- Never drink or cook from the hot tap: heat leaches metals from pipes and the heater tank. Hot beverages start from cold water.
- After stagnation (overnight, vacation): flush the cold tap 30-60 s before drinking (EPA lead guidance). Buildings old enough for lead service lines → certified filter (table below); a lab test settles the question for good.
- Infant formula: cold tap or certified-filtered water, never the hot tap; any local advisory overrides everything here.

## Filters — Match the Tool to the Problem

| Method | Handles | Does not handle |
|---|---|---|
| Activated carbon (pitcher, faucet) | Chlorine taste, some organics; lead ONLY if NSF/ANSI 53 certified | Microbes, nitrates |
| Reverse osmosis | Nearly everything: lead, nitrates, PFAS (certified units) | Wastes several liters per liter produced; strips taste minerals |
| UV | Microbes | Chemicals, metals, taste |
| Boiling | Microbes — 1 min rolling boil; 3 min above 2000 m (CDC) | Chemicals (slightly concentrates them) |

- Certification beats marketing: NSF/ANSI 42 = taste, 53 = health contaminants (lead), 58 = RO. No certification number on the box = a claim without evidence.
- An unmaintained filter is worse than none: saturated carbon sheds what it caught and grows bacteria — calendar the cartridge change.

## Travel

- The real question: is the tap treated AND the building plumbing trustworthy? Uncertain → sealed bottled (check the cap), boiled, or purified — and remember ice, salads, and tooth-brushing count as exposure too.
- Field purification: rolling boil 1 min (3 min above 2000 m — CDC), chemical tablets, or a filter-plus-purifier combo for trekking.
- Once symptoms start, this stops being a quality question: illness.md and ORS (electrolytes.md).
- Dry-cabin flight losses belong to environment.md; this file only covers what to refill the bottle with.

## Emergency Storage

- FEMA baseline: 1 gallon (3.8 L) per person per day, 3-day minimum — half for drinking, half for hygiene; commercially bottled, rotated by date.
- Under a boil-water advisory: boil everything for drinking, cooking, ice, and brushing until it lifts; coffee makers do not reach a sustained boil.
