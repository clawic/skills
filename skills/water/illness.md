# Illness — Fever, GI Losses, Hangover, Vulnerable People

Sickness losses are water PLUS electrolytes; plain water alone dilutes what remains. Run the Red Flags table (SKILL.md) before any protocol here — everything below assumes no row matched.

## Fever

- Losses rise with temperature through skin and breath. Offer small amounts hourly rather than big glasses — compliance beats arithmetic when someone feels terrible.
- Judge by urine color (Rule 4) and voiding frequency, not by the computed target: appetite-suppressed sick days rarely hit formulas, and that's fine as long as urine stays mid-range.
- "Sweating it out" is folklore; the drenching sweat when a fever breaks is a real loss to replace, not a therapy to induce.
- Warm fluids (broth, tea) count at 100% (Rule 2) and go down easier — broth adds the sodium a fever diet is missing.

## Vomiting and Diarrhea

- Oral rehydration solution, not plain water — composition and home recipe in electrolytes.md.
- Method beats volume: 5-10 ml sips every 5-10 minutes stay down when a full glass comes straight back up; scale up as tolerance returns.
- Sports drinks are under-salted for GI losses (electrolytes.md ranks the options); salted broth or ORS come first.
- Track two numbers in the log: hours since fluids last stayed down, hours since last urination — they drive the vulnerable-person rows in Red Flags.
- Keep feeding alongside ORS once vomiting settles; starving a stomach flu is outdated advice.

## Children and Elderly

- Both decompensate fast: vomiting or diarrhea beyond 24 h in a child or elderly person, or fluids not staying down → same-day care; oral rehydration solution meanwhile (Red Flags row, verbatim).
- Earlier signals in small children: no wet diaper for 6-8 h, crying without tears, sunken eyes, unusual drowsiness — any of these upgrades to same-day care now.
- Adults over 65: blunted thirst (Rule 5) plus common diuretic use multiply illness risk — during any illness, schedule fluids for them by default instead of waiting for requests.

## Hangover

- Mechanism: alcohol suppresses vasopressin, so more is urinated than was drunk. The morning deficit is real but modest — water helps the dehydration share of the misery; it does not cure a hangover.
- Play: 300-500 ml before bed when evening alcohol was logged, normal drinking on waking; ORS or a salty breakfast speeds the rebound; coffee is fine (Rule 2).
- Do not prescribe preventive liters at 2 a.m.: the 1 L per hour cap (Rule 6) and sleep both argue against it — this is the night version of the deficit-chugging trap (SKILL.md Traps).

## When Fluids Won't Fix It

- Dizziness on standing that persists after drinking, no urination for 8+ h, or confusion → Red Flags severe-dehydration row.
- Extreme thirst with high urine output over a week → Red Flags diabetes row; illness is not always dehydration.
- This skill logs and suggests oral fluids. IV rehydration, antiemetics, and prescription decisions are clinical territory — say so and route.
