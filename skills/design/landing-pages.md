# Landing Pages — Marketing Pages and CTAs

Brand positioning and messaging strategy live in the `branding` skill; this file executes the page once the message exists.

## Hero (above the fold)

- One headline stating the outcome for the visitor (not the product's category), one subline, one CTA. Every extra element above the fold splits the single rank-1.
- Headline at the top of the type ladder (39px+ desktop, one step down mobile), max 2 lines; a 3-line headline is a subline in disguise — cut it.
- The CTA is the highest-contrast element on the page (accent fill, 60-30-10). "Get started free" beats "Submit"; label = the action's outcome.
- Hero media (screenshot, product shot) supports the claim; if it's decorative stock, the text-only version is stronger. Faces and motion vectors point INTO the headline (`layout.md`).
- Navigation stays minimal on conversion pages: every nav link is an exit ramp; landing pages for campaigns often drop nav entirely except the logo.

## Section Rhythm

- Page = argument: hero claim → proof (how it works, 3 steps max) → evidence (social proof, numbers) → objection handling (FAQ, pricing clarity) → repeated CTA. Each section answers the objection the previous one raised.
- Consistent section padding (96px desktop / 48px mobile) so scrolling has a beat; alternate background tint at most every other section (`layout.md`).
- One idea per section, one heading on the scale. Sections with two messages split into two sections or lose one.
- Feature lists: 3 or 6 items in a grid — icon + 3-5 word claim + one supporting sentence. Paragraph-length feature descriptions never get read on a scroll.
- Repeat the CTA after each major proof block and at page end; same label every time — synonym CTAs ("Start now" / "Try it" / "Go") read as different actions.

## Social Proof

- Place proof adjacent to the claim it supports: logos near the hero, testimonial near pricing, numbers near the feature they measure.
- Specific beats superlative: "4,200 teams" and a named quote with face + role beat "loved by thousands". An unattributed quote is decoration, not proof.
- Logo strips: grayscale all logos at equal optical size — full-color logos at mixed sizes turn proof into clutter.

## Pricing Sections

- 3 tiers maximum, recommended tier visually promoted (accent border + "most popular" tag) — one rank-1 among the cards.
- Price is the largest number on the card; billing period small and adjacent; feature list as checkmarks, differences bolded, shared features collapsed.
- The free/cheap tier's CTA stays ghost-style; only the recommended tier gets the filled accent button.

## Conversion Hygiene

- Above-the-fold test at 360px width: headline + CTA visible without scrolling on mobile — most traffic sees this frame first.
- Forms on landing pages: ask the minimum (email alone converts best); every added field must earn its cost.
- Carousels and auto-rotating banners: users treat them as ads (banner blindness) and rarely see slide 2 — replace with a static section per message.
- Page weight is design: hero images compressed and sized to their container; a slow hero forfeits the reader before any typography matters.

## Traps

| Trap | Why it fails | Do instead |
|---|---|---|
| Two CTAs of equal weight in the hero ("Buy" + "Learn more") | Splits rank-1; squint test fails | Filled primary + text-link secondary |
| Headline describes the product category | Visitor must translate feature → benefit themselves | Lead with the visitor's outcome |
| Wall of features to seem complete | Nothing ranks; page reads as a spec sheet | 3-6 features tied to outcomes; rest to a docs link |
| Testimonial carousel with 10 quotes | One rotating slot = one quote seen | 2-3 static quotes placed next to relevant claims |
| Fake urgency banners and countdown timers | Erodes trust; reads as template spam | Real scarcity only, or none |
