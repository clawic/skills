# Slides — Decks and Presentations

## The Three Floors

- 24pt text floor (SKILL.md Quick Reference) — anything smaller is unreadable from the back of a room and signals the slide holds too much.
- One idea per slide. The slide count is free; audience attention is not. Splitting a crowded slide into three costs nothing and rescues all three.
- Max 6 lines of text per slide. Beyond that the audience reads instead of listening — and they read faster than you speak.

## Slide Anatomy

- Title states the takeaway, not the topic: "Churn concentrates in month 2" beats "Churn analysis". A reader flipping only titles should get the whole argument (assertion-evidence structure).
- Body = evidence for the title: one chart, one image, or 3-6 bullets — never two competing evidence blocks.
- Consistent title position and size on every slide; a jumping title line makes transitions feel broken.
- 16:9 canvas; margins ~5% of slide width on all sides; nothing touches the edge except intentional full-bleed images.

## Deck-Level Hierarchy

- Section divider slides (big number/title, distinct background) every 5-8 content slides give the audience a progress bar.
- One type scale for the whole deck: title ~32-40pt, body 24-28pt. The 24pt floor applies to everything the room must read; only source footnotes may drop to 20pt. Same ladder logic as Core Rule 4, coarser steps.
- One accent color for emphasis across the deck; the accent marks THE datum or phrase per slide, not decorative shapes.
- Repeatable layouts: title-only, title+chart, title+bullets, comparison (2-up), quote, divider. Six masters cover a whole deck; per-slide freeform layout is where alignment dies.

## Charts on Slides

- One chart per slide, title = the finding. Strip to the single series that proves the point; the full analysis lives in the appendix.
- Direct-label the key data point on the slide (callout + accent); the audience has ~10 seconds, not time to decode a legend (`data-viz.md`).
- Rebuild spreadsheet-default charts: default gridlines, 3D effects, and legends at 10pt do not survive projection.

## Projection Reality

- Projectors compress contrast: mid-gray text on white washes out. Keep text at near-black on white, or white on a dark slide — nothing between.
- Dark decks look premium in a dark room, light decks win in daylight offices; pick per venue, don't mix within a deck.
- Thin fonts and hairline rules vanish at distance: 400 weight minimum for body, 2px+ rule lines (`fonts.md` — one sans, 400/700).
- Test at thumbnail size: if the slide sorter view communicates each slide's point, the room will read it too.

## Delivery Formats

- Presented deck ≠ sent deck. A deck that reads alone needs the speaker notes promoted onto the slides — make two exports rather than one deck that does both badly.
- Slide numbers on content slides (skip title/dividers) for Q&A referencing.
- Embed fonts or export PDF; a deck opened on the podium machine with substituted fonts reflows every line.

## Traps

| Trap | Why it fails | Do instead |
|---|---|---|
| Paragraphs pasted from the doc | Audience reads, stops listening; 24pt floor breaks | One claim per line, max 6 lines, rest to notes |
| Topic titles ("Q3 Results") | Slide demands decoding; takeaway lost | Assertion titles ("Q3 revenue up 18% on renewals") |
| Template clip-art and decorative stock | Competes with the one idea for attention | Content-bearing visuals only, or whitespace |
| 10-item bullet lists compressed to fit | Violates the 24pt floor to keep one slide | Split by grouping into 2-3 slides |
| Animations on every transition | Motion becomes noise; timing stumbles live | Fade or none; animate only progressive disclosure of a complex diagram |
