# Focus — Procrastination, Distraction, Burnout

Most "focus problems" are entry problems: the session never starts, or starts on the wrong first action. Fix entry before optimizing the environment.

## Starting

- Shrink the entry: the first action is concrete and small — "answer yesterday's 5 missed questions", never "study chapter 3". Session Protocol 1 doubles as an on-ramp: opening with recall removes the where-do-I-begin decision entirely.
- 5-minute contract: commit to five minutes with permission to quit after. The contract works because stopping mid-task is rarer than never starting.
- Aversive subject: pomodoro it (25/5, Cirillo) for entry; drop the timer once engaged. The timer serves starting, not learning (SKILL.md Where Experts Disagree).
- Procrastination that survives all of the above is task ambiguity: the next action was never defined. Close every session by writing the next session's first three actions.

## Phone and Environment

- Phone in another room. Ward's brain-drain experiments measured attention costs from the phone's mere presence on the desk — even off, even face-down.
- One surface open: the material plus the question log. OS-level notifications off during blocks.
- A consistent study spot builds a start cue. Before an exam, run one or two sessions somewhere else — varied context aided later recall in Smith and Bjork's experiments, and the exam room is a new context anyway.
- Music: lyrics compete with verbal encoding — silence or instrumental for reading and memorization; instrumental tolerable for routine problem sets. Log what this student proves into memory.md (environment area); their data beats the default.

## Breaks

- A break is off-screen: walk, water, window. Feed-scrolling is task-switching that carries attention residue back into the block.
- Short breaks (5-10 min) between blocks. A meal-length break resets the entry problem — write the next block's first action before leaving the desk.

## Burnout and Motivation

- Burnout signals: hit rates falling across days despite adherence; dread disproportionate to the task; sessions running longer while producing less.
- Response: cut the number of sessions, never their quality — Rule 1 (retrieval share) and Rule 6 (sleep) are the floors that stay.
- Distant exam, no pull: switch to process goals (sessions completed, questions answered) tracked visibly; outcome goals don't pay out for months and motivation runs on payout frequency.
- Streaks: track consecutive study days in memory.md. A broken streak restarts without ceremony — the penalty mindset around one missed day is what causes the week-long abandonment.
- Persistent dread plus physical symptoms (sleep loss, appetite, panic) → SKILL.md Red Flags: route, don't optimize.

## Fragmented Time (commutes, gaps between classes)

| Fragment | Use it for |
|---|---|
| Under 15 min | Flashcard reviews only (`memorization.md` queue) |
| 15-30 min | One question-list pass, or one full problem with the error log |
| 30+ min | A real block: run the Session Protocol at reduced scale |

- Audio (recorded lectures, notes read aloud) is pre-processing only — it counts toward Rule 7's one pass, never toward the retrieval share. Retrieval needs a pen, a keyboard, or a card app.
