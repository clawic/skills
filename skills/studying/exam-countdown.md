# Exam Countdown — Protocols by Horizon

The core T-4-weeks → T-1-day schedule lives in SKILL.md (Exam Countdown). This file covers the horizons around it and the execution detail.

## T-8+ Weeks (certifications, comprehensives, finals season)

- Rule 2's ratio drifts down toward 5-10%: weekly relearning slots, not daily grind.
- Build the topic inventory from the syllabus or vendor blueprint; studying alongside a job → `certifications.md`.
- Front-load fact-heavy tracks — Rule 3 needs 3+ sessions each, and they are the first casualty of a late start.
- Add a midpoint diagnostic before the standard T-2-weeks test: a full timed set whose job is allocation (feed the `scheduling.md` formula), not prediction.

## Multiple Exams in One Week

- Order prep by exam date first, then by weakness × weight within each day.
- Each subject's last touch is the evening before its exam, retrieval-only.
- Never open exam B's new material the morning of exam A — it buys nothing for B and costs calm for A.
- 10-minute post-mortem after each exam BEFORE switching subjects; the details evaporate by evening.
- No answer-key rumination between exams in the same week — it changes nothing and taxes the next one.

## The Cram Protocol (<48h, unprepared)

Honest framing first: cramming buys recognition that survives days, not weeks. It is the right tool only because the exam is in days — say so, then run it properly.

1. Hour 1: one past paper or self-test, untimed if necessary. Its only job is the gap map.
2. Triage: rank topics by syllabus weight × miss rate. Keep the top slice coverable with ~3 retrieval passes each; explicitly drop the rest. Permission to drop is what makes the passes possible.
3. Retrieval-only loop: question lists, flashcards, cover-and-solve. Zero fresh reading beyond looking up missed answers.
4. Sleep stays (Rule 6). The all-nighter trade is strictly bad: it degrades exam-day recall of everything, including the material that was already solid.
5. Logistics done the night before; morning is warm-up only (below).

## Exam Morning

- Usual wake time; no new material.
- 10-15 min warm-up recall of the STRONGEST material — priming and confidence, not learning. Warming up on weak topics manufactures panic.
- Arrive early enough to be bored; the buffer absorbs the transit surprise that otherwise becomes the day's story.
- Once seated → `test-day.md` (brain dump, pacing, MCQ, blanking).

## Post-Mortem (10 min, same day)

Log to memory.md → Exams, while details persist:

- What the exam tested vs what the plan predicted (topic weights, question formats).
- Format surprises and time pressure — did the per-question budget hold?
- Which prep hours paid and which didn't.
- One sentence: what the next countdown does differently. This log is what makes the third countdown better than the first.
