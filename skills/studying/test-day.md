# Test Day — Execution Under Time

Preparation ends the night before (`exam-countdown.md`); this file is what happens once seated. Points are lost on execution that practice already earned.

## Before the First Question

- Brain dump (where allowed): first 2 minutes, write the fragile items on scratch paper — formulas, date lists, mnemonics — before the questions overwrite them.
- Read the scoring rules, not just the instructions: negative marking changes guessing strategy (below); uneven point values change time allocation.
- Per-question budget = total minutes ÷ question count, weighted by points when uneven. Worked example: a 20-point essay in a 100-point, 60-minute exam gets ~12 minutes, whatever its position in the paper.

## Two-Pass Pacing

1. Pass 1: answer everything solvable at sight; flag and skip anything stalling past ~2× its budget. Easy points first is variance insurance — the hard question can't eat the easy ones' time.
2. Pass 2: flagged questions with the remaining time. Later questions often cue the parked ones.
3. Final 5 minutes: format/bubble check; every question has an answer unless negative marking says otherwise.

## MCQ

- Answer in your head BEFORE reading the options — options are engineered to sound right.
- Eliminate, then choose. With formula scoring (a penalty of 1/(k−1) per wrong answer), guessing is break-even blind and positive-expected-value after eliminating even one option. With no penalty, never leave a blank.
- Changing answers: the "always trust your first instinct" rule is folklore — in Kruger's answer-changing studies, most changes went wrong→right. Change when there is a concrete reason (misread the stem, recalled the fact); the bigger measured error is fear of changing, not changing.

## Essays In-Exam

- Outline before writing, ~10% of the question's time: thesis, point-per-paragraph, evidence tags.
- Allocate by marks; when behind, cut the lowest-value section — an unpolished last paragraph loses fewer points than an unwritten question.
- Answer the verb: "evaluate" is not "describe". Underline the command verb and the scope words (during, except, primary) before outlining.

## Blanking and Panic

- Park it: flag, move on — adjacent questions are retrieval cues for each other.
- Approach sideways: free-associate on scratch paper around the topic (which lecture, which chapter, what sat next to it in the notes); the direct path being blocked doesn't mean the memory is gone.
- One minute of slow, exhale-weighted breathing, then re-read the question fresh — re-reading mid-panic just re-reads the panic.
- Blanking recurring across exams despite matched practice conditions → SKILL.md Red Flags: route to counseling, don't train harder.

## After

- Same-day 10-minute post-mortem while details persist (`exam-countdown.md`).
- No answer-key rumination before remaining exams in the same week — it changes nothing and taxes the next exam.
