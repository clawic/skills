# Equity And Comp Math

The numbers that decide whether "one more year" or "this offer" is real money. All figures pre-tax; tax treatment varies by jurisdiction and bracket — flag it, don't compute it, unless the user supplies their situation.

## RSUs (Public Company)

- Value the grant at current price and say the caveat once: a 4-year grant carries the stock's 4-year variance; no haircut formula pretends precision it doesn't have.
- Cliff math: a standard 4-year vest with 1-year cliff means leaving at month 11 forfeits the entire first year. Include cliff dates in any departure or "one more year" calculation.
- Vest cadence matters: monthly-after-cliff vs annual vesting changes optimal departure timing by up to a year of grant value — read the schedule, not the summary.
- Front- and back-loaded schedules (e.g., 5/15/40/40) move real annual comp: compute per-year vest value, never grant ÷ 4.
- Refreshers are the year-3 question: an initial grant fully vested by year 4 with no refresher policy is a built-in pay cut — this is the mechanism behind the year-4 exodus pattern at grant-heavy companies.

## Options (Startup / Private)

- Paper value = (preferred price − strike) × shares is the OPTIMISTIC read; the honest read prices in dilution, liquidation preferences, and the chance of no exit.
- The five questions to ask any startup: strike price; latest 409A fair market value; latest preferred (investor) price; total fully-diluted shares (your % = your shares ÷ fully diluted); liquidation preference stack. 1× non-participating preference is standard; participating or >1× eats common shareholders in modest exits.
- A company that refuses to state fully-diluted share count is answering the question.
- ISO vs NSO: ISOs keep tax-advantaged status only if exercised within 90 days of termination (statutory). Exercising costs strike × shares plus possible AMT, so leaving can mean forfeiting or writing a large check — ask about extended post-termination exercise windows before joining, not after resigning.
- Early exercise + 83(b): the election must be filed within 30 days of exercise (IRS deadline, no extensions). Missing it converts future appreciation into ordinary income at each vest.
- Options expire — 10 years from grant is standard even if you stay. Old grants near expiry are a forced decision, not a keepsake.

## "One More Year" Math

- Cost of leaving = value vesting between departure date and the next cliff/vest date, minus what the new offer replaces (sign-on, initial grant). Only that forward-looking number is the price of leaving; sunk years are not an asset (SKILL.md trap).
- Make-whole is a standard ask: employers buy out documented forfeited equity with sign-on or a matching grant. Bring the vest schedule and the arithmetic, not a feeling.
- Change-of-control: double-trigger acceleration (acquisition + termination) is the standard form; single-trigger (acceleration on acquisition alone) is rare. If a retention case rests on an acquisition rumor, read which one the grant actually has.

## Bonus Reality

- Expected bonus = target % × historical payout rate. A 20% target that paid 60% of target the last two cycles is a 12% bonus — ask for the payout history in writing.
- Guaranteed vs formulaic vs discretionary: get the classification in writing. "Discretionary" means it can be zero precisely in the year the company most needs it to be.

## Cash vs Equity Weighting

- Weight by the user's comp philosophy (Configuration preference area) and runway: short runway or dependents prices private equity near zero; long runway and conviction can weight it higher.
- Stability check: compute the comparison twice — equity at face value and equity at zero. If the decision flips between the two, the decision is really about risk posture, not about the offer; surface that explicitly before recommending.
