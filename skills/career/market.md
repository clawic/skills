# Market Position — Benchmarking And Staying Liquid

The base rate behind this file: switchers outprice stayers by roughly 1-2 percentage points of annual wage growth (SKILL.md rule 3, Atlanta Fed). Knowing your number and keeping options warm is how you capture that without job-hopping.

## Triangulating Your Band

- Three sources minimum; no single source trusted: comp aggregators (levels.fyi-class — dense for large tech, thin elsewhere), recruiter-stated ranges (they know the real budget), and peer conversations (trade your number to get numbers; the taboo is expensive).
- Posted ranges: pay-transparency laws in several US states and EU jurisdictions force ranges into job postings — treat midpoint-to-top as the live zone; bottoms are often legal-band edges, not offers.
- Normalize by LEVEL, not title (titles inflate across companies; map through aggregator level equivalencies) and by location/remote policy.
- Output: a band written into `profile.md` with sources and date. Refresh it at every market check — a band older than the last check is a memory, not data.

## The 18-24 Month Market Check

- Cadence even when happy: every 18-24 months, take 2-3 recruiter conversations to reprice the band (SKILL.md Quick Reference).
- Minimum viable check: two screens plus one band question each. Full check: one loop to a real offer — offers are the only ground truth; screens only estimate.
- Interviewing with low intent is legitimate market research; repeatedly running full onsites with zero intent burns recruiter goodwill. Cap deep loops to genuine "would move for the right number" cases.
- Calendar it like a dental visit: the trigger is elapsed time, not unhappiness. Waiting for unhappiness means negotiating desperate (SKILL.md trap).

## Handling Inbound Recruiter Pings

- Every inbound is free band data: "Not looking right now, but for calibration — what's the comp band on this?"
- Know who you're talking to: internal recruiters work for the employer and know the real band; contingency recruiters are paid a placement fee that scales with your comp (typically 15-25% of first-year salary) — their incentive to close is also an incentive to close HIGH, use that alignment; retained search is the executive game (`executive.md`).
- Keep a warm list: reply to good recruiters even when declining. The recruiter answered politely in March is the BATNA generator in November (SKILL.md rule 1).

## Underpaid Diagnosis

| Signal | Read |
|---|---|
| New hires at your level land above your comp | The band moved; you didn't. Compression is fixable with data |
| You don't know your position in band | Ask your manager where you sit — banded companies will usually state position even when they won't state numbers |
| Sub-inflation raises two consecutive years while performing | A real-terms cut; treat as a repricing trigger, not an insult |
| Recruiter bands consistently well above current comp | Confirmed gap — proceed to the fix order below |

Fix order: internal ask armed with band data (`promotion.md`, Internal Raises) → market test to a live offer → move. Skipping the internal ask forfeits the cheapest fix; skipping the market test makes the internal ask a bluff.

## Staying Findable

- BATNA decays without maintenance. Former colleagues are the referral source that beats cold applications on both response and offer quality — keep 2-3 touchpoints per quarter with the ones you'd want to work with again.
- One visible artifact per year outside your employer's walls (talk, post, open-source contribution, credential): it is what recruiters and hiring managers find when they look, and it survives any single employer's opinion of you.
- Log every data point in `profile.md` as it arrives — inbound bands, peer numbers, offer history. The file, not recall, is what makes the next negotiation start from evidence.
