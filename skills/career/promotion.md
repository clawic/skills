# Promotion — Levels, Calibration, Internal Raises

Scope: getting promoted, raised, or re-scoped where you already work. External offers → `offers.md`; deciding whether to stop trying → SKILL.md, The Decision Razor.

## How Calibration Actually Works

- Beyond small-company scale, promotions are decided in a calibration meeting your manager attends and you don't. Your manager is your advocate, not the decider: the packet must work when read by people who have never met you.
- The packet is 3-5 artifacts with your name on them that someone outside your team has already seen. Start collecting the day you decide to push, not review week.
- Promotion is a lagging indicator: you get it for having already done the next level's job for 6-12 months with witnesses outside your team (SKILL.md rule 5).

## Sponsor Cultivation

- Mentor vs sponsor: a mentor talks WITH you; a sponsor talks ABOUT you in rooms you are not in. A case without a sponsor above your manager is a coin flip at best.
- Build sponsorship by making work legible to skip-levels: demos, written updates that name outcomes, cross-team projects where another org's leader benefits from your work.
- Sponsor test: who, above your manager, could describe two things you shipped? If the answer is nobody, that is the packet's real gap — fix it before resubmitting anything.

## Timing And The One Question

- Raise the topic 2 review cycles before you want the outcome, and ask exactly one question: "What specifically is missing from my packet?"
- A specific answer is a checklist: do it visibly and confirm completion in writing — written confirmation converts a moving target into a contract.
- A vague answer ("more impact", "keep doing what you're doing") means no sponsor or no headcount. Probe which: "If I deliver X and Y by [cycle], will you sponsor the case?" A yes names a sponsor; a dodge names the constraint.

## Diagnosing A Stall

| Signal | Constraint | Move |
|---|---|---|
| "Not enough scope" every cycle, no new scope offered | Team has no next-level work | Internal transfer to a team that does, or go external |
| Packet praised, outcome "next cycle" twice | No sponsor in the room | Cultivate skip-level visibility before resubmitting |
| Level ratios or headcount cited | Structural ceiling | SKILL.md rule 3: the level you cannot get internally is usually purchasable externally as a hiring level |
| Concrete gap named once, consistently | Real gap | Close it with witnesses — the cheapest path in the table |
| New hires arriving at your target level | Band has space; your case lacks a champion | Ask what those hires showed that your packet doesn't |

## Internal Raises And Retention

- Internal negotiation is a different game from offers: your performance is already known, internal peer equity matters more than market rate, and you need a champion in the room, not just a request.
- Ask with band data (`market.md`), one number, and a business frame ("my position in band vs my scope"), never a threat.
- Never wave an external offer you are unwilling to take. Waved and matched, you stay flagged — the counteroffer default in `offers.md` now applies to you.
- Out-of-cycle asks work only when something changed: scope grew, a departed peer's work landed on you, or the market repriced the role (bring recruiter-band evidence). No change, no case — wait for the cycle.
- Title-for-scope trade: take scope, budget, or reports over an inflated title; an inflated title with unchanged scope fails the next interview loop (SKILL.md trap).

## Internal Transfers

- Transfer before quitting when the diagnosis is manager or team, not company (Decision Razor question a).
- Move on strength: transfer windows close during PIPs and open after strong reviews. If a PIP is likely, transfer FIRST or accept that the option is gone (`layoffs.md`).
- Diligence the target internally the way you would externally: interview the receiving manager, and ask what happened to the last person in the seat.
