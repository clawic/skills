# Executive Moves — Director And Above

Different mechanics, not just bigger numbers: a smaller market, longer cycles, and negotiated contracts instead of offer letters. `career_stage: executive` routes here; staff-plus ICs share about half of this file (retained search, contract terms, make-wholes).

## The Market Is Small And Has Memory

- Exec hiring runs on retained search and back-channels. The searcher is paid by the company (retained fees commonly run about a third of first-year cash comp), but the relationship with a search partner is a career-long asset — respond to every credible searcher, even when not looking.
- References run both directions and start before you know: assume back-channel checks with anyone you ever shared an employer with. The burn-no-bridges math is strict — the person you undercut this year sits on next year's board.
- Cycles run in months, not weeks: staged meetings, comp-committee sign-off, board approval. If leaving first, scale the runway formula (SKILL.md, The Decision Razor): executive searches commonly run 6-12 months, so 2× means holding 12-24 months or not resigning first.

## The Offer Is A Contract, Not A Letter

Negotiate at entry — leverage is never higher than the day before you sign:

- Severance agreement AT HIRE: 6-12 months of salary plus bonus treatment and vesting continuation is a normal executive ask. Negotiating severance after bad news is negotiating with none.
- Year-1 guaranteed bonus (you are walking away from an accrued one) and make-whole grants for forfeited unvested equity — document the forfeit with vest schedules, not estimates (`equity.md`).
- Change-of-control terms: double-trigger acceleration is standard, single-trigger rare (`equity.md`) — read which one is actually in the grant.
- Title, reporting line, and scope in writing: "CTO reporting to the COO" is a different job than "CTO reporting to the CEO". The org chart is comp.
- Counsel on every contract containing non-compete, clawback, or severance terms: their side drafted it; flat-fee employment counsel is how symmetry gets restored.

## Non-Competes And Garden Leave

- At this level non-competes are actually litigated and garden leave actually paid. Read duration, geography, and the definition of "competitor" twice: before signing, and again before resigning.
- Negotiate at entry: narrow "competitor" to a named list, convert restriction periods into PAID garden leave, and carve out board seats and personal investing.
- Enforceability is jurisdictional (California voids most employee non-competes); the contract's governing law, not your desk location, controls — get counsel's read, not folklore.

## Exiting As An Executive

- Resignations are negotiated events: transition period, announcement language, and vesting treatment are all on the table — the company wants a clean story as much as you do.
- Agree the external narrative in writing (announcement line, reference language) BEFORE the announcement; after it, you have nothing left to trade.
- Handled gracefully, exits compound: they convert into board seats, advisory roles, and the call from the next retained search. The exit is part of the career, not the end of one.
