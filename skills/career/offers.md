# Offers — Evaluating, Comparing, Countering

Scope: external offers. Internal raises and retention → `promotion.md`; equity mechanics and valuation → `equity.md`; director-and-above contracts → `executive.md`.

## Intake (Before Any Judgment)

1. Get every component in writing: base, bonus target AND payout history, equity grant with vest schedule and cliff, sign-on with clawback terms, refresher policy, review timing.
2. Compute annualized total comp yourself; recruiters quote best case. "Bonus target 20%" is not "bonus 20%": ask what percent of employees hit target last cycle (`equity.md` Bonus Reality).
3. Ask for the LEVEL, not just the title — comp bands, refreshers, and promotion math all hang off the level.
4. Never respond in the call: "I'm excited — send the full details and I'll come back within 48 hours" costs nothing and prevents peak-emotion acceptance at their anchor.

## Setting The Target

- Target comes from the market band (`market.md`), never from current comp. "Current plus 15%" is an anchor, not a target.
- Write the target down BEFORE the first comp conversation. A target set after seeing their number is their number wearing your handwriting.
- Record target and offer history in `profile.md` — next negotiation starts from data, not memory.

## The Expectations Question

- Deflect once: "I'd rather understand the role first — what's the budgeted band for this position?" Many answer when asked directly; pay-transparency laws in a growing set of US states and EU jurisdictions force ranges into postings anyway — check the posting first.
- If forced, give a range whose BOTTOM is your target: they hear the bottom; you still anchored above it.
- Application forms that require a number: enter the top of the researched band, not your target — forms filter, humans negotiate.

## Countering

- Counter once at 2 × target − offer (SKILL.md rule 2), justified with one sentence of market data, never personal need. Need justifies nothing; band data justifies everything.
- Delivery frame: "Based on [source] for this level and market, I was expecting closer to X — can we close that gap?" Collaborative wording, firm number.
- If base is capped, walk the flexibility ladder in order (SKILL.md rule 6): sign-on → equity → start date and vacation → title and review timing → base. A 10-20k sign-on closes most sub-10% gaps and is a one-time cost to them; an earlier 6-month review is a cheap early raise window.
- Sign-on clawbacks: 12-month repayment terms are standard; ask whether repayment is prorated or full. Full repayment at month 11 is a retention trap — negotiate prorated.
- No BATNA? Counter anyway. The script contains no tell of desperation unless you add one; the risk profile of a polite, single, data-backed counter does not change with your alternatives.
- Rescind fear check: professional counters almost never kill offers. A company that rescinds over one data-backed counter revealed its management culture at zero cost to you.

## Comparing Multiple Offers

- Normalize to 4-year total value per year: base + expected (not target) bonus + risk-adjusted equity (`equity.md`) + sign-on amortized over its clawback period.
- Compare trajectory, not just year 1: refresher policy and bonus payout history change year 3 more than sign-on changes year 1.
- Price the non-comp items that have cash value: healthcare premium delta, retirement match, commute or remote flexibility (hours saved × your hourly comp).
- The spreadsheet ranks; stored values decide. Score finalists against the values ranking in `profile.md` — a 10% comp edge does not outrank a top-ranked value violation.

## Deadlines And Sequencing

- Exploding deadline (under 1 week): request one week in writing; treat refusal as data about the employer.
- Tell slower processes: "I have an offer with a deadline of X." It compresses their loop or reveals you were not their front-runner — both useful. Never invent a deadline; it gets called.
- Accept-then-renege: exhaust extensions first. Recruiting networks are small and reneges are remembered (`executive.md` small-market math applies below exec level too).

## The Current-Employer Counteroffer

- Decline by default: the root cause persists and flight risk is now on record.
- The narrow exception: the counter fixes the DIAGNOSED problem (scope, manager, level — not just money) and you would have stayed had it come unprompted a month earlier. Run The Decision Razor (SKILL.md) again before accepting.
- Never resign to trigger one (SKILL.md trap).

## Verbal, Written, Rescinded

- Verbal ≠ offer. Resign only on a written offer with contingencies (background check, references) cleared — SKILL.md rule 8.
- Offer rescinded before start: treat as a layoff-lite — ask for feedback, a recruiter referral, and any promised sign-on honoring in writing; then back to the pipeline (`layoffs.md` narrative section for the story).
