# Audiences — Calibrating for the Reader

The same material briefs differently per reader. Calibration starts with one question: **who is the decider** — the single person whose action this brief serves. Their needs set depth and jargon; everyone else adapts.

## By Audience

| Audience | Optimize for | Depth and jargon | Classic failure |
|----------|--------------|------------------|-----------------|
| Your boss | Ammunition to relay upward; zero surprises | Shared team jargon fine; include what THEY will be asked about | They hear it from someone else first |
| Exec / leadership | The decision and your confidence in it | No project jargon; money, time, and risk units only | Mistaking detail for diligence |
| Board / investors | Trajectory vs plan; the asks | Company metrics, market language | Burying lowlights (`templates.md` → Board) |
| External client | Commitments and status against them | Their vocabulary, formal register, no internal names | Internal candor leaking ("the team is struggling") |
| Technical peers | Changes that touch their work; interfaces | Full jargon; link depth instead of inlining it | Exec-summarizing people who wanted specifics |
| Your future self | Resumable state | Any shorthand that survives 3 months | Writing to impress an audience of nobody |
| Mixed room | The decider | Least-technical decider sets the jargon ceiling | Writing for the loudest reader instead |

## Rules

- **Seniority compresses.** The more senior the reader, the shorter the brief and the earlier the ask. An exec version is never the team version plus polish — it is the team version minus everything below the decision.
- **Derive downward by deletion.** Two audiences for one material: write the deepest version first, then cut it down for the senior reader. Writing the short version first and inflating it produces padding, not depth.
- **No-surprises rule (upward).** Anything your boss could be asked about by THEIR boss goes in, even if it makes the brief less flattering. A boss ambushed once reads all your future briefs adversarially.
- **Interfaces, not internals (sideways).** Peer teams need what changed at the boundary — API, timeline, dependency — not your team's internal journey to it.
- **External register is one-way.** Candor removed for a client version never comes back by accident; build the external version from the internal one, and keep them as separate artifacts.
- **The spectator trap.** Non-deciders on the distribution list get the same brief as the decider — not a longer one. Readers who want more ask; readers who wanted less already stopped.

## When the Audience Is Unknown

Default to the exec calibration (shortest, plainest) and say what depth exists: "Detail on the vendor comparison available." Under-briefing a specialist costs one follow-up question; over-briefing an exec costs the read entirely.

Record revealed audience patterns under the **Audience mix** preference area (SKILL.md Configuration) — a user who always briefs their board should get board calibration by default.
