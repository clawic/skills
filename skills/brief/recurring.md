# Recurring Briefs — Running a Series

A one-off brief informs; a series builds (or burns) trust. The rules change when edition #2 exists.

## Delta Discipline

- Report what changed since the last edition; unchanged sections collapse to one line: "Hiring: no change (3rd week)."
- The "(Nth week)" counter matters — a long-unchanged item is itself a signal (stalled? deprioritized?) that a bare "no change" hides.
- Full restates train readers to skim, and skimming readers miss the one edition where something changed. That edition is the entire reason the series exists.

## The Baseline

The skill stores no brief content (SKILL.md Scope), so the delta needs a baseline from the user:
- Ask for the previous edition or the tracker it was built from before writing edition #2+.
- No baseline available → mark the edition "full status — no delta baseline" once, and ask the user to keep a series source (a doc, a thread) for the next one. Never fake a delta from memory.

## The No-Change Edition

Send it. Three lines: status, "no material change", next milestone date. Skipping a period breaks the cadence contract — readers either assume the worst or stop expecting the brief at all, and both cost more than three lines. Same day, same time, every edition (`delivery.md`).

## Status Flips

- A status change is the headline of that edition, not a table cell — bottom line states the flip and why.
- Green-to-red with no prior at-risk edition: include one line on why the early-warning failed ("vendor breakage surfaced only at integration"). Owning the miss is what keeps the next "On track" believable (SKILL.md Rule 7).
- Recovery flips (red-to-green) name what actually changed, or the reader assumes the red was theater.

## Metric Continuity

- Same metrics, same order, same definitions, every edition. Readers navigate a series spatially; reordering costs them the comparison that is the series' whole value.
- Adding or dropping a metric is announced in the edition that does it: "Dropping NPS (survey retired); adding weekly actives."
- A metric redefined mid-series gets both numbers once: old definition and new, side by side, then the new one only.

## Series Hygiene

- Title carries name + date + edition number: "Platform Status — Jul 21 — #14". Late joiners and forwarded copies need to know where in the stream they are.
- Keep a stable section skeleton even when a section is empty ("Blockers: none") — absence of the section and absence of blockers must not look identical.
- Audience drift check every few editions: people join and leave distributions; a series calibrated for last quarter's readers slowly misses this quarter's (`audiences.md`).

## Killing a Series

A series nobody reads costs credibility on everything else you send. Signals: no reactions or questions for several editions while decisions in its domain happen through other channels. Move: propose merging it into an existing forum or killing it — "this brief or the Monday sync, not both." Proposing the kill reads as judgment; quietly degrading the quality reads as neglect.

## When the Series Outgrows This Skill

Fixed data sources on a schedule with configured formatting → `report`. Curated external sources (news, competitors) on a cadence → `digest`. This skill covers series the user feeds by hand.
