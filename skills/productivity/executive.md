# Executive — Thinking Time Inside a Calendar You Do Not Own

The executive constraint: accountability for judgment, with a calendar filled by other people. The scarce resource is not hours, it is uninterrupted hours in which the thinking that justifies the role can happen. Set `calendar_owned: false` if scheduling is not actually theirs — half the plays change.

**Before advising**, read `## Constraints`, `## Commitments` and `## Due` in `~/Clawic/data/productivity/memory.md`, and the shared `~/Clawic/data/projects/`. An executive's dropped commitment costs more than their lost hour, because several people were waiting on it.

## What Fails Here

- **Calendar tetris.** Optimizing the packing produces a fuller calendar, not a better one. Fifteen-minute gaps are not usable for judgment work; the packing optimization is what removed the blocks.
- **Always available.** Instant response trains the organization to route every decision upward, and the volume grows to match the responsiveness. The pattern is self-reinforcing and self-inflicted.
- **Delegation as dumping.** Handing off a task without criteria and without decision rights produces boomerang work at a worse quality than doing it (`delegation.md`).
- **Strategy on the weekend.** Thinking scheduled into unpaid, unprotected time is thinking that does not happen; and when it does, it happens with no access to the people who hold the facts.
- **Inbox as the job.** Each reply generates further replies; processing volume rises to fill whatever time is allocated (`messages.md`).
- **Individual-contributor reflexes.** Doing the analysis yourself because you are good at it costs the org the multiplier and costs the analyst the growth.

## What Works

- **One sacred block, weekly, defended like a board meeting.** 2-3 hours, same slot, named after its output ("Q3 plan"), never traded. One protected block that survives beats five that get moved.
- **Decision criteria instead of decisions.** "If X, do Y; escalate only if Z." Every criterion published removes a recurring stream of escalations. This is the highest-leverage writing an executive does, and it belongs in `artifacts/` so it survives the person who wrote it.
- **The first hour before the organization wakes.** The only reliably uncontested block in most executive calendars.
- **Batch the recurring load.** All 1:1s on one or two days, keeping the rest structurally whole (`meetings.md`).
- **A written operating manual.** How you decide, what you want escalated, response times, what "done" looks like. It removes a standing tax of guessing across the whole team and takes about two hours to write once (`artifacts/operating-manual.md`).
- **Someone owns the calendar** — an assistant, a chief of staff, or an explicit set of rules the executive enforces. Unowned calendars fill by default.

## The Leverage Question

Every hour has an alternative use with a different multiplier. Before accepting anything into the calendar: **what does this hour multiply?**

| Activity | Multiplier |
|---|---|
| Setting direction, deciding a criterion, hiring | Compounds across the whole org, for quarters |
| Unblocking a stuck team | Multiplies by the size of the team, immediately |
| Reviewing work | Multiplies by one, plus a development effect if the feedback teaches |
| Doing the work yourself | Multiplies by one, minus the opportunity cost of everything above |

An executive doing IC work is not lazy or heroic; they are paying the org's most expensive rate for its cheapest output. The defensible exceptions are narrow: a genuine emergency, and deliberately staying close to the craft on a bounded schedule.

## Decision Load

Decision quality degrades across a long day of decisions. Three mitigations:

- **Batch similar decisions.** All hiring calls together, all budget approvals together. Switching decision types is where most of the cost is.
- **Set a decision speed by reversibility.** Reversible decisions get made fast and revisited; irreversible ones get the slot, the data and the sleep. Treating every decision as irreversible is the most common executive slowdown.
- **Make the important ones early**, in the peak window (`energy.md`). Anything decided at 18:30 after a full day is decided by a depleted instrument.

## Information Diet

- Ask for one page, not a deck. If a recommendation cannot survive one page, the thinking is not finished.
- Require the recommendation up front, followed by the reasoning. Reading a build-up to a conclusion costs your time to save the author's.
- Escalations arrive with the options and a recommended option, or they go back once with that request. This is training, not rudeness, and it takes about three rounds.
- Pick a small number of numbers you look at weekly and ignore the rest of the dashboard. Dashboards expand to fill attention.

## The Real Issue

Executive productivity problems are usually organizational problems that appear on one person's calendar: unclear strategy makes everything urgent, weak delegation systems route everything upward, and an unfilled role becomes a standing overload with a name on it. Treat the calendar as a symptom and read it as diagnostic data about the organization — then fix the organization, which is the actual job.

## What to Write Down

- Standing meetings, board and reporting cycles, travel patterns go to `## Constraints`.
- Commitments made to the board, peers or reports go to `## Commitments`, people to `~/Clawic/data/contacts/contacts.md` with name, role and channel only — never a private assessment of them.
- Decision criteria, the operating manual, and delegation briefs go to `~/Clawic/data/productivity/artifacts/` with their `## Boxes` lines. These are the artifacts with the longest half-life in this role.
- The meeting audit (`meetings.md`) goes to `artifacts/meeting-audit-<date>.md`; the quarterly re-audit is a `## Due` row.
- Strategic outcomes go to `## Goals`, each with a project file in the shared `~/Clawic/data/projects/`.
