# Parent — Planning Around Time You Do Not Control

The parent constraint: interruption is not a failure mode, it is the environment. Available hours are fewer, more fragmented, and less predictable than any standard productivity model assumes, and sleep — the input everything else depends on — is the one most often unavailable.

**Before advising**, read `## Constraints`, `## Energy Patterns` and `## Commitments` in `~/Clawic/data/productivity/memory.md`, plus the long-form file named by `constraints_file` if one exists. School hours, nap windows, handovers and care arrangements are hard constraints, not preferences.

## What Fails Here

- **"Wake up before the kids."** Works until the baby wakes earlier, or until the 5am start produces a 2pm collapse. Borrowing from a sleep account that is already overdrawn is a losing trade (`energy.md`).
- **The two-hour block.** Planning around blocks that do not exist produces a plan that fails daily and a person who concludes they cannot focus any more.
- **Comparison to the pre-child self.** That person had eight hours of sleep and no dependents. The comparison is not a motivation; it is a recurring grief with no action attached.
- **Both-places guilt.** Working time feels stolen from the children, child time feels stolen from work. The result is two halves of divided attention and no recovery in either.
- **Waiting for it to calm down.** Newborn chaos becomes toddler chaos becomes school logistics becomes teenage logistics. There is no phase where the schedule becomes yours again by itself.

## What Works

- **Micro-sessions are real work.** Fifteen focused minutes with a defined target produces more than two hours of half-attention. The requirement is that the target is written before the session starts, so no runway is spent deciding (`focus.md`).
- **One most-important task per available window.** When they sleep, that task — not laundry, not email. The window is short and it will not be re-offered.
- **Lower the bar deliberately, in both directions.** B-tier work plus B-tier housekeeping is what makes a sustainable week; A-tier at both is what produces the collapse.
- **Tag-team windows with a partner**, written down: "you have 19:00-21:00, I have 21:00-23:00." Vague fairness produces resentment; a schedule produces time.
- **Budget for interruption.** Apply the calibration ratio honestly: a parent's ratio is often near 2 during care hours, and planning at 1.2 guarantees a failed week every week.
- **Prepared starts.** Leave the file open and the next action written at the end of every session. When you get 15 minutes, none of it should go to remembering where you were.
- **Visible work.** Working where children can see you, with a stated start and end, teaches focus and buys legitimacy for the boundary.

## Two Capacities, Not One

Plan the week as two different resources, because they behave differently:

| Resource | Typical shape | Suited to |
|---|---|---|
| Care hours (children present) | 5-20 minute fragments, unpredictable | Admin, replies, laundry-shaped tasks, thinking while walking |
| Clear hours (childcare, school, sleep) | 1-3 hours, semi-predictable | The one demanding task, and nothing else |

The most common planning error is spending clear hours on work that could have been done in care hours. Clear hours are the scarcest resource in this situation; guard them like an executive guards a board slot, and put admin in the fragments where it belongs.

## Sleep Deprivation Is a Capacity Fact

- Chronic short sleep degrades attention and increases error rates before the person notices (`energy.md`). During those months, plans built for a rested version of the user will fail and the failure will be read as a character problem.
- Adjust the numbers instead of the ambition: reduce `focus_hours_target` to 1-2 while the nights are broken, and say why. A plan that fits is worth more than a plan that flatters.
- Whoever can sleep, sleeps: a split-shift arrangement with a partner beats both people being averagely destroyed.
- The recovery is measured in months for anyone under a year of broken nights. That is a fact about the situation, not a personal failing.

## Logistics as Real Work

School forms, appointments, birthdays, kit and paperwork are unpaid, invisible, and usually unevenly distributed. They are also genuine load and belong in the system:

- Capture them in the same one inbox as everything else (`capture.md`); a separate mental list for family logistics is a second inbox and it leaks.
- Recurring items — term dates, vaccination reminders, renewals — become `## Due` rows once, not a monthly rediscovery.
- Make the split with a partner explicit and visible. Unwritten division of invisible work is where most of the resentment lives, and writing it down usually redistributes it without an argument.

## The Real Issue

Parent productivity problems are capacity problems, not system problems: fewer hours, less sleep, less predictability. Better systems help at the margin; radical prioritization and lowered expectations do the actual work. The most useful thing to say is often "this is the correct amount of output for your current situation" — which is not encouragement, it is arithmetic.

## What to Write Down

- School hours, care arrangements, nap windows, handovers, on-call weeks go to `## Constraints`; long or complex arrangements to the file named by `constraints_file`.
- The reduced `focus_hours_target` while the constraint lasts is a declaration: `config.yaml`, with a date to revisit.
- Family logistics with dates become `## Due` rows or `## Tasks` items — the same system as work, never a separate one.
- Commitments to a partner or to school go to `## Commitments`; people to `~/Clawic/data/contacts/contacts.md` with name and role only, and nothing about children beyond what a schedule needs.
- Energy windows in the current phase go to `## Energy Patterns`, dated — they change every few months, and a stale window silently misplaces every block.
