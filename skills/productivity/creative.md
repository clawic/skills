# Creative — Output That Cannot Be Scheduled Like Assembly

The creative constraint: the work has phases with genuinely different requirements, quality is not proportional to hours, and the standard productivity apparatus — quotas, timers, uniform blocks — is calibrated for work whose value scales with volume. Applies to writing, design, composition, research and any craft where the first version is not the deliverable.

**Before advising**, read `## Energy Patterns`, `## Friction` and `## Tasks` in `~/Clawic/data/productivity/memory.md`. Creative peak windows are unusually personal and unusually stable, and the plan is worth almost nothing if it ignores them.

## What Fails Here

- **The 25-minute timer applied to flow.** Excellent for starting, disruptive once the work is moving: the interruption costs more than the break returns (`focus.md`).
- **Daily quotas as the only metric.** 3,000 words of unusable draft counts, one sentence that fixes the structure does not. Quotas optimize what they measure.
- **Waiting for inspiration.** Inspiration arrives during work more often than before it; the belief is a reliable way to not start (`procrastination.md`).
- **Imported morning routines.** A significant minority of creative workers peak late; the 5am prescription is a schedule preference presented as a law.
- **Mixing modes in one session.** Generating and editing use opposite postures — one needs permissiveness, the other needs judgment. Running them together produces a slow first draft and shallow editing.
- **Reviewing before finishing.** Editing the first paragraph twelve times is the most socially acceptable way to avoid writing the twelfth.

## The Four Modes

Treat these as different kinds of work with different requirements, and schedule them separately.

| Mode | Needs | Fits |
|---|---|---|
| Input | Novelty, unrelated material, rest | Trough hours, walks, deliberately unproductive time |
| Ideation | Permission, volume, no judgment | Medium energy; a notebook, low stakes, quantity targets are legitimate here |
| Drafting | Long uninterrupted runway, low standards | The peak window, longest available block |
| Editing | Judgment, distance, fresh eyes | Medium energy, and at least one night after drafting |

Two consequences: editing on the same day as drafting is systematically worse, because distance is the input editing requires; and blocked drafting is often an ideation problem — there is nothing to draft yet, and the fix is upstream.

## What Works

- **Start in the middle.** Write the section you can see clearly; beginnings are the hardest part and rarely survive editing anyway.
- **Minimum viable touch.** "Open the file and read yesterday's last paragraph" is enough of a first action to break the stall (`procrastination.md`).
- **Long sessions when it is working.** When a session is producing, cancel what can be cancelled. Four productive hours are worth more than eight fragmented ones, and the state is not summonable on demand.
- **Stop at a known next move.** Hemingway's habit, and the most reliable one here: end mid-sentence, or with one line about what comes next. Tomorrow's start costs seconds.
- **Deliberate volume in ideation.** Twenty bad titles produce a good one; three careful titles produce three careful titles.
- **Separate the reader from the work.** Show a draft to one safe reader before a real audience — the fear of judgment is the mechanism, and one low-stakes exposure discharges most of it.
- **Track sessions, not words.** Did the session happen, and did it end at a landing point? That measure survives days where the right output was one deleted chapter.

## Deadlines and Commissioned Work

- **External deadlines help creative work more than most practitioners admit** — they end the infinite revision loop. The cost is a missing revision pass, so the schedule should place the deadline before the real one (`planning.md`).
- **Client and editor feedback rounds are a phase, with a count**: two rounds in the scope, extras billed. Unlimited revisions convert a fixed price into an open-ended one (`freelancer.md`).
- **Estimate creative work with a separate ratio.** Drafting and editing diverge sharply; a blended ratio makes both plans wrong. Keep both in `## Calibration` once each has five pairs.
- **Define done in advance.** "Good enough" set before starting is a decision; set during the work it is a negotiation with fear, and fear does not concede.

## Blocked

Diagnose before treating. Four causes, distinguishable in one question each.

| Cause | Question that reveals it | Move |
|---|---|---|
| Nothing to say yet | "Could you explain it out loud to someone?" No → | Back to input and ideation |
| Structure unclear | "Do you know what comes after this section?" No → | Outline, not prose; the block is a planning problem |
| Fear of the result | "If it were finished and bad, what happens?" | Lower the tier, show one safe reader |
| Depletion | "Is anything producing energy right now?" | Rest; creative work is the first casualty of depletion (`energy.md`, `burnout.md`) |

Free-writing for ten minutes with no requirement to keep any of it addresses the first two and does nothing for the last two — which is why it sometimes seems to work and often does not.

## The Real Issue

Creative productivity problems are usually fear problems: fear that the work will not be good, that finishing invites judgment, that the last good piece was the last one. The technique layer — modes, blocks, landing points — makes the work start; it does not resolve the fear, and pretending otherwise is why generic advice bounces off creative practitioners. Say the fear out loud, then make the next action small enough that fear has nothing to grip.

## What to Write Down

- Peak window and the mode that fits each part of the day go to `## Energy Patterns`. This is the highest-value observation in this situation and it is stable for years.
- Session records — planned vs actual minutes, which mode, whether it ended at a landing point — go to `sessions/<year>.md`; estimate pairs, split by mode, go to `## Calibration`.
- A body of work with phases and deadlines becomes a project file in the shared `~/Clawic/data/projects/`, with the decisions taken and rejected recorded there.
- Editors, clients and readers go to `~/Clawic/data/contacts/contacts.md`; feedback commitments to `## Commitments`.
- A routine that reliably unblocks this person — a specific walk, a warm-up, a first-line ritual — goes to `~/Clawic/data/productivity/artifacts/<name>.md` with its `## Boxes` line. It is worth more than any general technique in this file.
