# ADHD — Systems That Work With the Brain You Have

The constraint: standard productivity advice assumes reliable access to initiation, time perception and working memory. Where that access is intermittent, the advice does not merely fail, it produces evidence for a shame narrative that has usually been accumulating since school. Everything here works for anyone with executive-function difficulty, diagnosed or not — diagnosis is a clinician's call and never yours (SKILL.md Red Flags).

**Before advising**, read `## Friction`, `## Energy Patterns` and `## How They Work` in `~/Clawic/data/productivity/memory.md`, plus the shared `~/Clawic/data/health/profile.md` if it exists — medication timing changes when the usable hours are.

## What Fails Here

- **The silent planner.** A notebook that must be remembered to be checked cannot solve a remembering problem. Anything that relies on you thinking of it is not a system.
- **"Just focus" and "try harder".** Executive function is not effort; effort is what is being spent already, which is why the exhaustion is real and invisible.
- **Willpower-dependent systems.** Any design that needs consistent daily initiation will fail on the days it is most needed.
- **Distant deadlines.** A date two months out has little motivational reality; the work arrives with the urgency, not with the plan.
- **Rigid identical routines.** Sameness stops registering as a cue. The routine that worked for six weeks disappearing is a normal feature, not a relapse.
- **Advice built on shame.** Guilt reliably produces avoidance of the topic, which means avoidance of the system, which produces more guilt. It is the single most damaging thing to add.

## What Works

- **Externalize everything.** Alarms, visible timers, notes where the action happens, a whiteboard by the door. Working memory is the bottleneck; anything held in it is at risk.
- **Body doubling.** Working alongside someone, in person or on a call, supplies external structure that internal systems cannot. It is the highest-yield intervention here and the most underused, because it feels like it should not work.
- **Manufactured urgency.** Artificial deadlines with a witness, a scheduled session with someone else, a public commitment. Urgency is what converts intention into action, so it has to be supplied rather than waited for.
- **Extreme decomposition.** "Open the document" is a task; "work on the report" is not. Smaller than feels reasonable — the granularity that works is usually two steps below what a neurotypical planner would write (`procrastination.md`).
- **Timers, always visible.** Time blindness is real: 20 minutes and 2 hours feel similar from inside a task. A visible countdown restores the sense that the plan is a physical fact.
- **Novelty as fuel.** New place, new music, new tool, standing instead of sitting. Rotating the environment is a legitimate strategy rather than an admission of failure.
- **Ride hyperfocus, then pay it back.** When it arrives, clear the calendar and use it — and put recovery afterwards, because the crash is part of the transaction.
- **Forgive fast, restart smaller.** The gap does not matter; the restart does. The minimum version exists precisely for this (`habit-building.md`).

## Designing Around Time Blindness

- **Anchor to events, not to clock time.** "After lunch" beats "at 14:00" for anything that is not a meeting, because clock time requires monitoring and events do not.
- **Alarms for transitions, not just for starts.** The hard part is leaving the current task; a five-minute warning before the transition costs nothing and prevents the lateness.
- **Make duration physical.** An analog timer, a playlist that is exactly one block long, a candle. Abstract time does not constrain behavior; visible time does.
- **Estimate multipliers run higher here.** Use the measured ratio from `## Calibration` without softening it. A ratio of 2 or 3 on some task types is information, not an indictment, and planning at 1.2 guarantees a failed week.
- **Buffer between everything.** Transitions cost more than the tasks admit; a schedule with no gaps is a schedule that fails at the first transition.

## Interest, Not Importance

Attention here follows interest, novelty, urgency and challenge more than it follows stated importance. Fighting that is a losing strategy; designing around it is not.

- **Pair the boring with the stimulating**: music, a walking call, a coworking session, a timer race. Genuinely dull work needs an added source of stimulation, not more resolve.
- **Reorder rather than force.** If three tasks are open and one is interesting today, doing it now and the others tomorrow beats doing none of them today in the correct order.
- **Front-load the deposit.** Sign up, pay, tell someone, book the slot — the parts that can be done in a burst of interest, so that the later work has an external commitment attached.
- **Bundle admin into one loud session** with a body double, rather than distributing it across a week where it will be avoided daily.

## Emotional Reality

- Rejection sensitivity is common and it makes feedback disproportionately expensive; a shame spiral costs far more days than the original missed task.
- Self-compassion is functional here, not sentimental: the faster the restart, the smaller the total loss.
- Years of "you have so much potential" produce a specific injury — the belief that the problem is character. Naming the mechanism as neurological is often the most useful sentence in the entire session.
- Medication, where a clinician has prescribed it, changes which hours are usable; it is not a moral question, and it is not yours to raise unprompted or to evaluate.

## The Real Issue

ADHD productivity problems are neurological, not moral. The intervention is not more discipline; it is externalizing memory, manufacturing urgency, shrinking the first action, and removing shame from the loop — because shame is the component that turns one missed day into three lost weeks.

## What to Write Down

- What actually works for this person — body doubling, timer style, best hours, which task types stall — goes to `## How They Work`. It is the most valuable section in the file for this situation, because generic advice has already been tried and discarded.
- Recurring stall patterns and what unblocked them go to `## Friction`.
- Medication timing or a diagnosis the user states goes to the shared `~/Clawic/data/health/profile.md` as one dated line. Never infer it, never diagnose, never record a symptom you observed.
- Estimate pairs go to `## Calibration` unsoftened — the honest ratio is what makes the plans survivable.
- A working routine (a specific body-doubling arrangement, a launch sequence for hard tasks) goes to `~/Clawic/data/productivity/artifacts/<name>.md` with its `## Boxes` line, because rebuilding it from scratch after a bad month is exactly what will not happen.
