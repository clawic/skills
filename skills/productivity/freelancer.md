# Freelancer — Structure Nobody Else Will Build

The freelancer constraint: total control of the schedule, no external structure, and unpaid work (sales, admin, invoicing) competing with paid work for the same hours. Everything that an employer normally supplies — the day's shape, the boundary, the pipeline — has to be built by the person doing the billable work.

**Before advising**, read `## Constraints`, `## Commitments` and `## Due` in `~/Clawic/data/productivity/memory.md`, plus the shared `~/Clawic/data/projects/` and `~/Clawic/data/contacts/contacts.md`. Client deadlines and who is waiting are the real constraints here.

## What Fails Here

- **No shape to the day.** Without a start signal, "I'll begin after lunch" reliably becomes "I'll begin Monday". The absence of structure is the entire problem, and willpower is not a structure.
- **Availability as a service.** Answering instantly feels like good client service; it sets a response expectation that then makes deep work impossible, for the whole relationship (`messages.md`).
- **Feast or famine.** Busy means no time to sell; quiet means panic selling into a pipeline with a lag. The cycle is generated by treating sales as something to do when there is room.
- **Home as office.** Work everywhere means recovery nowhere. The couch becomes a place with obligations attached.
- **Scope creep accepted in the moment.** "Just one small change" costs the margin. Unbilled changes accumulate into an effective hourly rate nobody would have accepted up front.
- **Isolation.** No colleagues means no calibration and no casual feedback; it drains energy in a way that reads as a motivation problem.

## What Works

- **A fake commute.** Walk around the block to start, and again to end. A physical boundary is cheaper and more reliable than a mental one.
- **Published office hours.** "I respond 9-17, Mon-Fri" in the signature and the proposal. Almost nothing is a genuine emergency, and the ones that are get a phone number.
- **One admin day a week.** Invoicing, marketing, email, bookkeeping, proposals — batched, so the other days stay whole. Admin scattered across five days consumes more than a whole day of them.
- **Sell while busy.** One pipeline action per week, always, including in the busiest month. It is the only thing that breaks the cycle, and it is the first thing dropped.
- **Track billable ratio, honestly.** `billable ratio = billed hours ÷ hours worked`. If a rate is quoted as if every hour were billable, the real income is that number multiplied by the ratio — which is often between half and three quarters for solo work.
- **Coworking, even occasionally.** One day a week outside the flat buys structure and a social baseline for the price of a desk.

## Pricing the Hour Honestly

Effective hourly rate = `(project fee) ÷ (estimated hours × calibration ratio + admin + revisions + unbilled calls)`. A 2,000 fee on an estimate of 20 hours reads as 100/hour; at a ratio of 1.5 plus 4 hours of admin and calls, it is 2,000 ÷ 34 ≈ 59/hour. Every quote should be checked against that number before it is sent, and every ratio should come from `## Calibration` rather than from optimism.

Two consequences: a fixed price without a scope boundary is a variable price with fixed revenue; and the cheapest capacity a freelancer can buy is usually someone else doing the admin, because it converts trough hours into peak hours.

## Client Boundaries That Hold

- **Scope in writing, always** — deliverables, revision count, what is out of scope, and the rate for extras. The rate for extras is what makes "just one more thing" a decision instead of a favour.
- **Trade, do not absorb**: "Happy to add that — it moves delivery to <date>, or it replaces <item>. Which?" (`delegation.md`).
- **Deposit before starting**, staged payments on anything long. Payment terms are a boundary; the client who negotiates them hardest is usually the one who pays late.
- **Chase invoices on a schedule**, not on a feeling — a `## Due` row for the weekly overdue check. Discomfort is what makes freelancers wait three weeks to send a one-line reminder.
- **Fire the loss-making client.** Compute the effective rate per client once a quarter; the one that generates the most email is usually the one paying least per hour.

## Feast and Famine

- **Pipeline as a `## Due` row.** One outreach action weekly, regardless of load. Sales has a lag of weeks to months; work done today lands when you need it.
- **Keep a bench project** — a portfolio piece, a template, a rewrite of the website — for the quiet weeks, so the quiet is not spent on anxiety.
- **Name the minimum viable month**: the number that covers fixed costs. Knowing it converts a quiet month from panic into arithmetic, and it makes it possible to say no in a busy one.
- **Overbooking is a decision with a price**: accepting overlapping projects buys revenue and spends quality and sleep. Make it deliberately, with a date when it ends.

## The Real Issue

Freelancer productivity problems are boundary problems — with clients, with space, with hours, and mostly with yourself. Freedom without structure is not freedom; it is a job with no start time, no end time, and no colleagues. The structure has to be manufactured deliberately, and it holds only when it is visible to clients as well as to you.

## What to Write Down

- Client work becomes a project file in the shared `~/Clawic/data/projects/` — scope, fee, deadline, decisions; clients go to `~/Clawic/data/contacts/contacts.md` with name, role and channel, never a private opinion.
- Estimate and actual per project go to `## Calibration`. For a freelancer this ratio is a pricing input, not a curiosity.
- Admin day, invoicing, pipeline action and the overdue-invoice check become `## Due` rows.
- Scope templates, proposal boilerplate, and the wording that ended a scope-creep conversation go to `~/Clawic/data/productivity/artifacts/` with their `## Boxes` lines.
- Money detail beyond the project fee — accounts, subscriptions, tax reserves — is out of this skill's scope and is not written anywhere by it; keep only the fee and the deadline in the project file.
