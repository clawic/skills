# Bangkok Festivals & Annual Calendar

What each event means operationally — when the city shuts, when prices spike, when alcohol disappears, and when the party is worth planning a trip around. Dates for lunar events shift yearly; verify the current year's dates.

## The Year at a Glance

| When | Event | Operational impact |
|------|-------|--------------------|
| Jan/Feb (lunar) | Chinese New Year | Yaowarat closes to traffic — lion dances, the year's best Chinatown night; Chinese-Thai businesses close 2-3 days |
| Feb (full moon) | Makha Bucha | Alcohol ban day |
| Apr 13-15 (really 12-16) | **Songkran** | See below — the big one |
| May (full moon) | Visakha Bucha | Alcohol ban day |
| Jul (full moon, x2 days) | Asahna Bucha + Khao Phansa | Alcohol ban days, back to back |
| Jul 28 | King's Birthday | Government holiday; no alcohol ban |
| Sep/Oct (9 days, lunar) | Vegetarian Festival | Yellow flags everywhere = strict vegan (jay) street food — the best vegan eating of the year (`food-practical.md`) |
| Oct (full moon) | Wan Ok Phansa | Alcohol ban day |
| Nov (full moon) | **Loy Krathong** | Riverside and park lakes packed; magical, low-hassle |
| Dec 5 | Father's Day (late King Rama IX) | Government holiday |
| Dec 31 | New Year countdown | CentralWorld = Bangkok's Times Square; river fireworks; roads gridlocked from 8 PM |

## Songkran — Survival & Enjoyment

Thai New Year, officially Apr 13-15; in practice water flies Apr 12-16 in party zones.

- **The non-negotiable**: on any street, you are a target — monks and the visibly elderly are exempt, you are not. Phone and passport in a dry bag (฿50-100 from any 7-Eleven) every time you step out, all week.
- **Epicenters**: Silom (closed to traffic, the biggest), Khao San (backpacker chaos), Ari (family-friendlier). Join one deliberately or route around them entirely — there's no half-in.
- **The city empties**: Bangkok residents go home upcountry. Many small restaurants and laundries close for up to a week; malls, chains, and tourist zones stay open. Quietest traffic of the year outside the water zones.
- **Government closes** ~Apr 12-16 including immigration: never let a visa deadline land in Songkran week (`visas.md`).
- **"Seven dangerous days"**: the national road-death peak; drunk driving spikes. Don't ride motorbikes, don't do intercity road trips (`driving.md`).
- Hotels in party zones book out weeks ahead at premium; book by February or stay outside the epicenters.

## Loy Krathong — Where to Float

- Full moon of the 12th lunar month (November). Float a krathong (฿30-100 from vendors on-site) to release the year's bad luck.
- Best Bangkok spots: Chao Phraya piers and riverside hotels (most atmospheric, most crowded), Lumphini and Benjakitti park lakes (calmer, families), Rot Fai Park.
- Buy the banana-trunk or bread versions, not styrofoam — the river cleanup the next morning is the argument.
- Not the flying-lantern festival: that mass sky-lantern imagery is Yi Peng in Chiang Mai, same week. Lanterns are restricted in Bangkok (flight paths).

## Alcohol Ban Days — the Mechanics

- Five Buddhist holy days (table above) + election days: **no retail or bar sales**, 24 hours. 7-Eleven physically curtains the alcohol shelves; bars close or serve soft drinks; clubs go dark.
- Some hotels quietly serve guests; a 2025 relaxation for hotels/airports was floated — verify current practice, don't promise it.
- Everyday baseline (non-festival): retail alcohol sells only 11:00-14:00 and 17:00-24:00 — the 3 PM beer run at 7-Eleven fails every day, not just holidays (`food-practical.md`).
- Play: check the holy-day calendar when planning a night out or a party; stock up the day before.

## Planning Around the Calendar

- **Best festival to fly in for**: Songkran if you want the party; Loy Krathong if you want the postcard; Chinese New Year if you're a food person.
- **Worst weeks to handle bureaucracy**: Songkran week and the Dec 31-Jan 2 stretch — offices closed, everything slow.
- **Price spikes**: Songkran and New Year for hotels; cool-season December is peak season anyway (`climate.md`).
- Long-weekend clusters (many holidays bridge Mondays): Thais travel domestically — Hua Hin, Khao Yai and trains sell out; book day-trip transport ahead (`visitor-tips.md`).
