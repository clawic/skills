# Retiring in Bangkok

Visa numbers (the ฿800,000 / ฿65,000-month tests) live in `visas.md` — this file is the retirement life around them: route choice, insurance reality, where to live, and the compliance rhythm that keeps the extension alive.

## Route Choice: Non-O vs O-A

| | Non-O (convert in-country) | O-A (apply from home country) |
|---|---|---|
| Where obtained | Enter visa-exempt → convert at immigration → annual extensions | Thai embassy at home before flying |
| Health insurance mandate | None | Required (coverage floor has moved — ฿3M/$100k class; verify current figure) |
| Practical verdict | **Default choice** — same result, no insurance mandate, no home-country paperwork | Only worth it if you want a year stamped before arrival |

- Income-letter method (฿65,000/month): only embassies still issuing them work — the US, UK, and Australian embassies stopped in 2019, so their citizens use the bank method or the combination method.
- Seasoning discipline (canonical figures in `visas.md`): ฿800,000 in place 2 months before and 3 months after each extension, never below ฿400,000 the rest of the year. One dip below the floor = denied extension at renewal. Keep this account untouched and separate from spending money.

## Health Insurance — the Real Gate

The visa is easy; insurability is the actual long-term constraint.

- New policies get hard to start around age 70-75; most insurers won't onboard past that. **Decide your insurance strategy before 65, not at 74.**
- Ballpark premiums: a 60-year-old pays roughly ฿60,000-120,000/year for ฿3-5M inpatient cover; premiums step up each age band and pre-existing conditions are excluded, not rated.
- Self-insure math if uninsurable: hold ฿1-2M liquid earmarked for one major hospitalization (`healthcare.md` for what private care costs), use public hospitals for the catastrophic tail, and price in medical evacuation ending the Thailand experiment.
- Hospital proximity is a legitimate neighborhood criterion at this life stage — it's why retirees cluster where they do (below).

## Where Retirees Actually Live

| Base | Why | Trade-off |
|------|-----|-----------|
| Phrom Phong / Asok | Samitivej + Bumrungrad within 10 min, Emporium comforts, flat walkable sois | Sukhumvit prices (`neighborhoods-sukhumvit.md`) |
| Sathorn / Silom | BNH and St Louis hospitals, Lumphini Park for morning walks | Office-district energy (`neighborhoods-silom.md`) |
| Riverside | Calm, views, cooler breeze | Everything needs a boat or BTS ride |
| Outside Bangkok: Hua Hin | Beach town 2.5 h away, huge retiree infrastructure, cheaper | Hospital ceiling lower than Bangkok's top tier |
| Outside Bangkok: Chiang Mai / Pattaya | Cheaper still, big expat scenes | Burning season (Feb-Apr) in Chiang Mai is brutal on older lungs |

Budget: comfortable retired single in Bangkok ฿50,000-80,000/month, couple ฿70,000-120,000 — the delta over the general single budget in `cost.md` is mostly insurance, better housing, and more air-conditioned living.

## The Compliance Rhythm

Annual cycle that keeps everything valid — calendar all of it:

1. **Annual extension** at Chaengwattana: bank letter + statements same-day fresh, photos, TM7, ฿1,900. Arrive 7-8 AM (`visas.md` for office mechanics).
2. **Re-entry permit** before ANY trip abroad: single ฿1,000, multiple ฿3,800. Leaving without one voids the extension — the most expensive ฿1,000 ever saved.
3. **90-day reports** (฿2,000 fine missed) and **TM30** address filing after moves.
4. Bank balance check monthly against the ฿400,000 floor.

Agents (฿5,000-15,000/year) legitimately handle queueing and paperwork. An agent offering extensions "without money in the bank" is describing bribery that attaches to your passport — `visas.md`.

## Estate and Admin

- **Thai will for Thai assets** (bank accounts, condo, vehicle): ฿10,000-30,000 at a law firm, in Thai and English. Without one, Thai intestacy law + a consular process measured in years. Home-country wills don't reach Thai assets cleanly.
- Register your embassy's residency notification; it speeds up everything consular.
- Tell your Thai bank you're tax-resident status honestly — and read `taxes.md`: pension remittances are assessable for residents unless your tax treaty says otherwise (many protect government and social-security pensions).

## Traps

| Trap | Why it fails | Do instead |
|------|--------------|------------|
| Arriving at 72 planning to buy insurance "later" | Insurers won't start cover; the window closed | Sort insurance in your 60s or commit to the self-insure number |
| Spending the ฿800k between extensions | Balance dip = extension denied, restart from scratch | Separate untouchable account; live off a different one |
| Flying home for an emergency without a re-entry permit | Extension void; the whole visa history resets | Multiple re-entry permit every year, automatically |
| Marriage extension to save money (฿400k vs ฿800k) | Higher scrutiny, home visits, and divorce = visa dies with it | Retirement extension if you qualify — money buys simplicity |
| Buying a condo in month two | See `property.md`'s 12-month rule; retirees are the top demographic for this mistake | Rent a year; the condo will still be for sale (cheaper) |
