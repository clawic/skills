# Thai Taxes for Foreigners

The single most expensive surprise for long-stayers. The folk wisdom "Thailand doesn't tax foreign income" died on 1 Jan 2024. Rules below held as of early 2026 and are actively in flux — verify with the Revenue Department or a Thai tax advisor before structuring anything.

## Tax Residency — the 180-Day Rule

- **≥180 days physically in Thailand in a calendar year = Thai tax resident.** Days count per calendar year (Jan-Dec), not rolling; entry and exit days both count.
- Residency is automatic — no registration needed, no visa type exempts you from it. A tourist chaining exemptions past 180 days is a tax resident.
- Non-resident (<180 days): taxed only on Thai-source income.

## The Remittance Rules (what changed in 2024)

| Period | Rule |
|--------|------|
| Income earned before 1 Jan 2024 | Grandfathered: remit anytime tax-free — keep year-end 2023 statements as proof of the balance |
| Income earned from 1 Jan 2024 (Por. 161/2566) | Assessable when remitted to Thailand, in whatever year, if you were tax resident in the year it was earned |
| 2025 draft softening | Revenue Department floated exempting income remitted in the year earned or the following year; a worldwide-taxation bill was also floated. Neither is confirmed law — check current status before advising |

- "Remittance" = money entering Thailand: bank transfers, and ATM withdrawals from a foreign account. Foreign credit-card spending in Thailand is a gray zone the RD has signaled it may treat as remittance — unenforced so far, don't build a strategy on it.
- Money kept offshore is not taxed (under the remittance regime). The planning lever is what you bring in, and when.

## Rates and a Worked Example

Progressive personal income tax on assessable income:

| Bracket (THB/year) | Rate |
|--------------------|------|
| 0 – 150,000 | 0% |
| 150,001 – 300,000 | 5% |
| 300,001 – 500,000 | 10% |
| 500,001 – 750,000 | 15% |
| 750,001 – 1,000,000 | 20% |
| 1,000,001 – 2,000,000 | 25% |
| 2,000,001 – 5,000,000 | 30% |
| over 5,000,000 | 35% |

Worked example — resident remits ฿1,000,000 of post-2024 foreign earnings, only the ฿60,000 personal allowance applies: assessable ฿940,000 → 5% band ฿7,500 + 10% band ฿20,000 + 15% band ฿37,500 + 20% on the remaining ฿190,000 = ฿38,000 → **฿103,000 (~$2,940, effective ~10%)**. Employment income also gets a 50% expense deduction capped at ฿100,000; pension/dividend/rent income each have their own deduction rules — advisor territory.

## Per-Visa Exposure

| Visa | Tax position |
|------|-------------|
| Tourist / exemption chains | Same 180-day rule as everyone — no shelter |
| DTV | **No tax privilege.** Resident + remitting = assessable; the visa only solves immigration |
| LTR (Wealthy, Pensioner, Work-from-Thailand categories) | Foreign-source income exempt by Royal Decree 743 — the one genuine shelter; LTR-HSP instead gets a 17% flat rate on Thai employment |
| Non-B + work permit | Thai salary taxed at source via monthly withholding; employer files |
| Retirement (Non-O) | Pension remittances assessable unless a treaty assigns taxing rights elsewhere (many treaties protect government/social-security pensions — check yours) |

## Double Tax Agreements

- Thailand has DTAs with 61 countries. Effect: tax paid at home is credited against Thai tax on the same income, not stacked — you pay roughly the higher of the two, not both.
- US citizens: the US taxes worldwide income regardless; FEIE (~$120k+/year, physical-presence test) plus the US-Thai treaty usually zeroes double payment, not filing obligations.
- Treaty relief is claimed at filing with proof of foreign tax paid — keep home-country returns.

## Filing Mechanics

- Tax year = calendar year. File PND 90/91 by **31 March** paper, ~8 April online, for the previous year.
- Tax ID: local Revenue Department office, passport + lease/TM30; 10-15 minutes. Needed to file and increasingly requested by Thai banks.
- No tax due = no penalty for not filing in practice, but a filed return is becoming useful evidence (visa extensions, LTR applications). Tax due + no return = back tax, 1.5%/month surcharge, fines.
- Thailand joined CRS in 2023: foreign banks report Thai-resident accounts to the RD. The "they can't see my accounts" assumption is dead.

## Traps

| Trap | Why it fails | Do instead |
|------|--------------|------------|
| Planning around pre-2024 folk wisdom ("season it offshore a year") | That exemption ended for income earned from 2024 | Grandfathered pre-2024 savings first; current income needs real planning |
| Counting only long stays toward 180 days | Every day counts, across any number of entries | Tally all days per calendar year before assuming non-residence |
| Remitting a lump sum for a condo purchase without checking source | A ฿5M remittance of post-2024 income can trigger a ~฿1.2M tax bill | Remit grandfathered funds or structure with an advisor first (`property.md`) |
| Treating the DTV as a tax exemption | It is immigration paper only | LTR is the exemption route for high earners |
| Ignoring home-country exit rules | Some countries keep taxing until you formally break residency | Break home residency properly or expect two claims on the same income |
