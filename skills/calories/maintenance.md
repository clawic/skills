# Ending a Diet — Maintenance, Reverse Dieting, Recomp, and Phase Changes

Most tracking advice covers the cut and abandons the user at the finish line — which is where most regain happens. This file covers everything after "I hit my goal" and everything between goals.

## Exiting a Deficit

- Default: go straight to calculated maintenance — measured TDEE at the new bodyweight (`calibration.md`), which already reflects the smaller body and most adaptation.
- Expect +1-2 kg in week one: glycogen and water refilling, the mirror image of the first-week drop (`trend.md`). Say this BEFORE it happens — the week-one regain is the single biggest trigger for panic re-cutting.
- Reverse dieting (adding ~100 kcal/week until maintenance): evidence does not show it outperforms going straight up (SKILL.md, Where Experts Disagree). Offer it for post-show athletes or users genuinely anxious about regain — the value is psychological ramp, not metabolic repair.
- Adaptation partially resolves at maintenance over weeks-to-months; recalibrate monthly and expect measured TDEE to drift UP slightly — pleasant news worth delivering.

## Maintenance as a Practice (not a number)

- Define a band: settled weight ±1-1.5 kg. Inside the band, no action, no commentary.
- Act only when the 7-day average sits outside the band for 2+ weeks — then a small correction (±200-300 kcal/day) until back inside. This is the whole system; its virtue is that it is boring.
- Tracking can loosen: many users graduate to hand portions (`estimation.md`) + weekly weigh-ins, with full logging reserved for two calibration weeks after anything big changes (new job, injury, new training block). Tracking is scaffolding, not a life sentence — offer the exit ramp; never prescribe indefinite gram-logging.

## Diet Breaks and Refeeds (during long cuts)

- Diet break: 1-2 weeks at maintenance every 8-12 weeks of continuous cutting. The MATADOR trial (2-week diet / 2-week maintenance blocks) found better loss efficiency, and breaks reliably rescue adherence. Frame as part of the plan, never as failure.
- Refeed: 1-2 days at maintenance with the extra calories mostly carbs. Solid psychological tool; modest metabolic one. It refills glycogen — warn about the scale blip (`trend.md`) the day you schedule it.
- Neither is "cheating": both are planned maintenance. The word "cheat" itself feeds the moralizing trap (SKILL.md Traps).

## Recomposition (muscle up, fat down, scale flat)

- Who it works for: novices, returners after a layoff, and higher body-fat trainees — at maintenance or a small deficit (200-300 kcal) with protein at the top of rule 7's band and progressive training (`gym`).
- The scale is the wrong instrument by design: track waist at navel monthly, photos every 4 weeks, and gym performance. A flat scale + shrinking waist + rising lifts IS the win condition; say so explicitly or the user quits a working plan.
- Advanced lean trainees recomp barely or not at all: for them, sequenced bulk/cut phases beat chasing both at once.

## Switching Phases (cut ↔ bulk)

- Insert 2-4 weeks of deliberate maintenance between phases: it re-baselines measured TDEE, settles water, and separates the psychological gears — jumping cut-to-surplus overnight reliably overshoots.
- Bulk end condition is the user's stated comfort/goal, not a universal body-fat number; when they ask "should I cut now?", surface the tradeoff (longer bulk = more muscle AND more fat to cut later) and let them pick.
- Each new phase gets fresh targets from `targets.md` seeded with the latest measured TDEE — never reuse last year's numbers at a new bodyweight.
