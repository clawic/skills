# Learning Mandarin in Beijing — Paths, Costs, and What Actually Works

> Last updated: February 2026 | Survival phrases and the Beijing accent live in `culture.md` (canonical). This file is for actually learning the language.

---

## Set the Goal First (hours don't lie)

| Goal | Benchmark | Realistic investment |
|---|---|---|
| Survival | ~50 phrases + numbers + menu characters | 2-4 weeks of daily 30-min practice |
| Daily life functional | HSK 4 (~1,200 words) | 6-12 months at 8-10 hrs/week |
| Professional working | HSK 5-6 | 2-3 years sustained study |
| Full professional fluency | — | ~2,200 classroom hours (FSI estimate for Mandarin — 4x a European language; budget accordingly, don't promise your employer otherwise) |

HSK note: the 9-band HSK 3.0 exists since 2021, but employers and the work-permit points system still quote the 6-level scale — HSK 4+ earns 5 points toward the 85-point A-tier threshold (`visas.md`). Test at least one level below where you study: certificates lag ability.

## Study Options in Beijing

| Option | Cost | Best for | Catch |
|---|---|---|---|
| University semester program (BLCU, Tsinghua, BNU) | ≈¥12,000-13,000/semester | Full-time learners; grants X1/X2 visa route (`visas.md`) | 20 hrs/week, fixed schedule, classmates set the pace |
| Private schools (That's Mandarin, Hutong School, GoEast online) | Group ¥80-150/hr, 1-on-1 ¥150-350/hr | Working adults, flexible hours | Quality varies by teacher, not brand — trial class first |
| Private tutors | ¥100-300/hour (`culture.md`) | Targeted speaking practice | Interview for teaching skill, not just being native |
| Language exchange (免费) | Free | Speaking volume | Half the session is you teaching English — budget for that |
| Apps only | ¥0-500/yr | Maintenance and vocab | Nobody reaches HSK 4 on apps alone; pair with humans |

BLCU (Beijing Language and Culture University, Wudaokou) is the dedicated language university — the campus where "everyone is also learning Chinese" is a real immersion advantage.

## The Toolkit That Survives the Firewall

- **Pleco** — dictionary, offline, non-negotiable (`culture.md`). Buy the handwriting + OCR add-ons once and stop photographing menus into translation apps.
- **Anki** — spaced repetition for characters; syncs fine in China.
- **HelloChinese / Du Chinese** — gamified basics / graded reading; both work without a VPN.
- **Bilibili** — the YouTube substitute for immersion content; YouTube and Netflix need the VPN.
- **WeChat itself** — set the interface to Chinese once you pass HSK 3; the daily forced reading beats a textbook chapter.

## Method Decisions (settle these early)

1. **Tones from day one.** Untoned Mandarin is unparseable, not accented — retrofitting tones after six months costs more than learning them first.
2. **Read yes, handwrite no.** ~1,500 characters covers daily life reading; typing is pinyin-based so handwriting is calligraphy, not communication. Exception: you need handwriting for the HSK 5+ paper test.
3. **The Beijing r.** Locals speak with 儿化 érhuà (`culture.md`); textbooks don't. Learn standard forms, recognize the -r forms — otherwise you'll pass HSK 3 and still miss "哪儿".
4. **One deliberate-Chinese domain per week.** Order food only in Chinese, then taxi small talk, then the bank. Domain-by-domain beats "speak more" as a plan.

## Beijing-Specific Traps

| Trap | Why it fails | Do instead |
|------|-------------|------------|
| Living in the Sanlitun bubble | English works there, so practice pressure drops to zero; plateau at survival level is the norm | Housing near a local neighborhood (`neighborhoods-historic.md`) doubles daily exposure for free |
| Tutor who chats in English | You're paying ¥200/hr for a friend | Set a 90% target-language rule in lesson one; replace tutors who drift |
| "I'll pick it up by living here" | Adults don't; expats with 10 years and 50 words are common | Scheduled hours or it doesn't happen — see the table above |
| Studying characters before speaking survival | Months of flashcards, still can't buy fruit | Speaking-first for month one, characters from month two |
