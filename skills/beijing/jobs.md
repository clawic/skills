# Working in Beijing Beyond Tech — Teaching, Academia, and Employment Law Basics

> Last updated: February 2026 | Tech roles and salaries: `tech.md`. Visas: `visas.md`. Income tax: `taxes.md`.

---

## The Legal Baseline (non-negotiable)

Any paid work requires a Z visa + work permit (`visas.md`). Internships on an X1 student visa need university and PSB approval stamped into the residence permit. Working on an M or L visa is not a gray area — it is deportation plus a 5-10 year entry ban when caught, and schools get raided (`safety.md`). Every "start now, we'll convert your visa later" offer transfers all the risk to you.

## Teaching English (the biggest non-tech niche)

### Requirements (for a legal English-teaching work permit)

- Bachelor's degree (any field) + 2 years post-graduation experience, or a teaching qualification that waives experience
- Passport from a recognized native-English country (UK, US, Canada, Australia, NZ, Ireland, South Africa) for English-subject permits
- 120-hour TEFL/TESOL certificate (standard ask), clean criminal-record check, authenticated diploma

### Market After "Double Reduction" (2021 policy)

For-profit academic tutoring of school-age kids was banned — the training-center segment that once absorbed most new arrivals shrank hard. Surviving demand: international/bilingual schools, kindergartens, universities, adult and non-academic English.

| Employer type | Monthly (¥) | Package | Reality check |
|---|---|---|---|
| International school (licensed teacher) | 28,000-45,000 | Housing allowance, flights, kids' tuition | Requires home-country teaching license + experience; the only segment paying like tech |
| Kindergarten (bilingual) | 16,000-28,000 | Sometimes housing | Highest demand, most variable quality — vet hard |
| Public school program | 10,000-18,000 | Often housing | Light hours, real holidays |
| University | 7,000-15,000 | Free on-campus housing | 12-16 teaching hours/week; the time-for-money trade for writers/students of Chinese |
| Training center (adult/non-academic) | 15,000-25,000 | Rare | Evening/weekend hours; check the license type matches what they teach |

### School Red Flags

| Red flag | What it means |
|---|---|
| "Come on a tourist/M visa first" | They can't or won't sponsor legally — you carry the deportation risk |
| Work permit lists a different school/city than where you teach | Permit fraud; both you and the school are violating terms |
| Passport "held for processing" beyond days | Never surrender it for storage — a copy suffices for anything legitimate |
| Salary split official/cash | Underdeclaring your income breaks `taxes.md` records and weakens permit renewal |
| No demo class, instant offer, pushy recruiter | Churn shop; ask to speak to a current foreign teacher alone |

## Other Real Niches

| Niche | Entry route | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Academia (lecturer/researcher) | University English-taught programs, postdoc schemes | Permits handled well; points system favors PhDs (`visas.md`) |
| Embassy/international orgs | Home-country hiring portals | Beijing's diplomatic quarter is large; jobs often fill from home country, not locally |
| Hospitality/F&B management | International hotel chains | One of the few segments sponsoring non-degree-track permits, slowly |
| Translation/copywriting | In-house at Chinese firms going global | Native-language polish is the product; freelancing legally still needs a permit-holding employer |
| Acting/modeling/voice work | Agencies | Mostly gray-market gigs on wrong visas — treat as the same risk as illegal teaching |
| Trailing spouse remote work | Overseas employer, S1 visa | Tolerated in practice, not legal on a dependent visa; keep income offshore and understand `taxes.md` residency rules before assuming it's invisible |

## Employment Contract Basics (labor law you actually use)

| Item | Statutory rule |
|---|---|
| Written contract | Within 30 days of starting; no contract after a month = double-salary penalty owed to you |
| Probation | Capped by contract length: ≤1 month (contract under 1 yr), ≤2 months (1-3 yrs), ≤6 months (3+ yrs/open-ended); one probation per employer, ever |
| Annual leave | 5 days (1-10 total working years), 10 days (10-20), 15 days (20+) — on top of public holidays (`culture.md` calendar) |
| Resignation | 30 days written notice (3 days during probation); the employer cannot "refuse" a resignation |
| Severance | 1 month per year served (half-month for <6 months); +1 month if terminated without 30 days notice |
| Disputes | Labor arbitration is free, employee-friendly, and mandatory before court — keep your contract, payslips, and chat records |

Foreigners get labor-law protection only with a valid work permit — another reason the legal baseline above is the whole game.
