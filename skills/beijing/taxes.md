# Beijing Expat Taxes — IIT, the Six-Year Rule, and Keeping Clean Records

> Last updated: February 2026 | Individual income tax for foreigners. Corporate tax and social insurance employer rates: `business.md`.

---

## Tax Residency

| Days in China (calendar year) | Status | Taxed on |
|---|---|---|
| < 90 | Non-resident, treaty relief common | China-source salary paid by overseas employer often exempt (check your treaty) |
| 90-182 | Non-resident | China-source income |
| ≥ 183 | Resident | China-source income; worldwide income only after the six-year clock runs out |

**The six-year rule:** a foreigner resident ≥183 days/year for six consecutive full years becomes taxable on worldwide income from year seven. The clock resets with either a single trip abroad of more than 30 days in any calendar year, or one year under 183 days. Long-termers plan one 31-day absence before year six closes — this is standard, legal, and your HR knows it.

## IIT Brackets (annual comprehensive income)

Applied after the ¥60,000 standard deduction, social insurance, and any allowances/deductions. Formula: **tax = taxable × rate − quick deduction**.

| Taxable income (¥/yr) | Rate | Quick deduction (¥) |
|---|---|---|
| ≤ 36,000 | 3% | 0 |
| 36,000-144,000 | 10% | 2,520 |
| 144,000-300,000 | 20% | 16,920 |
| 300,000-420,000 | 25% | 31,920 |
| 420,000-660,000 | 30% | 52,920 |
| 660,000-960,000 | 35% | 85,920 |
| > 960,000 | 45% | 181,920 |

Worked example — senior engineer at ¥60,000/mo (¥720,000/yr, mid-range in `tech.md`): after the ¥60,000 standard deduction and roughly ¥60,000 of social insurance and housing fund employee contributions, taxable ≈ ¥600,000 → tax = 600,000 × 30% − 52,920 = **¥127,080**, effective ≈ 18% of gross. Withholding is cumulative month by month, so take-home visibly shrinks toward year-end — that is the system working, not an error.

## The Two Deduction Systems (pick one per year, not both)

| System | What it covers | Best for |
|---|---|---|
| **Foreigner tax-exempt allowances** (policy extended through end-2027) | Housing rent, children's education, language training, home-leave flights, meals/laundry — paid by employer against fapiao, written into the contract | High rent or international-school tuition: a ¥20,000/mo housing allowance escapes your top marginal rate |
| **Special additional deductions** (the local system) | Fixed amounts for rent, children's education, elderly care, mortgage, continuing education, medical | Modest housing costs; no fapiao workflow |

Negotiation consequence: structuring part of a package as allowances instead of raw salary is worth real money at 30-45% marginal rates — raise it at offer stage, not after signing. The allowance must be reimbursement-with-fapiao, not a cash lump labeled "housing".

## Annual Bonus — Separate Taxation (extended through end-2027)

A once-a-year bonus may be taxed separately: divide by 12, find the monthly-table rate, apply to the whole bonus. ¥120,000 bonus → 10,000/mo → 10% bracket → tax = 120,000 × 10% − 210 = **¥11,790**, versus ~¥36,000 if merged into ¥600,000 comprehensive income at 30%. Confirm payroll actually elects this — it is optional and some providers default to merging.

RSUs and options (common at `tech.md` employers): taxed as employment income at vest/exercise, with a similar separate-calculation relief — get the equity tax treatment in writing before accepting an equity-heavy offer.

## Annual Reconciliation (汇算清缴)

- Window: **March 1 – June 30** for the previous year, via the IIT app (个人所得税) or the district tax bureau.
- Required if you underpaid > ¥400 or want any refund (common after job changes, bonus elections, or missed deductions).
- Foreigners can register in the app with a passport, but first-time setup usually needs one in-person visit with passport + work permit. Do it — the payment records it generates are the document that unlocks remitting savings abroad (`resident.md`, transfer limits) and clean exits (`leaving.md`).

## Social Insurance for Foreigners

- Mandatory in Beijing: employee share ~10.5% plus housing fund (full rate table: `business.md`). Housing fund participation for foreigners is negotiable at some employers.
- **Totalization treaties** exempt some nationalities from parts of it (Germany, South Korea, Japan, Denmark, Finland, Canada, Switzerland, the Netherlands, France, Spain, Luxembourg, Serbia — scope varies by treaty; pension is the usual exemption). Bring a coverage certificate from your home system; HR will not chase this for you.
- On final departure you can withdraw your pension individual-account balance (the 8% employee share): `leaving.md`.

## Traps

| Trap | Why it fails | Do instead |
|------|-------------|------------|
| Offshore salary split to "avoid" IIT while working in Beijing | Evasion, not planning; surfaces at permanent-residence application, work-permit renewal, or bank remittance checks | Declare China-work-days income; use the legal levers above |
| Ignoring the six-year clock | Year seven quietly makes global investment income taxable | Calendar the 31-day reset trip before year six completes |
| Allowances agreed verbally | Tax bureau honors the contract and fapiao trail, not intentions | Contract clause + monthly fapiao reimbursement |
| Skipping reconciliation after a mid-year job change | Two employers each applied the ¥60,000 deduction → you owe; unpaid balances block exit paperwork | File in the March-June window every year |
| Assuming home-country taxes stopped | US citizens file FEIE/foreign tax credits regardless; others may owe on rental/investment income at home | Confirm home filing obligations separately — outside this file's scope |
