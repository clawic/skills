# Competitors — Tracking Entities, Not Coverage

Competitor and people tracking is the digest's highest-value mode: the user acts on these items. The discipline: report **moves, not mentions** — an action BY the entity, never an article ABOUT them.

## The Move Catalog

What counts as a reportable move, roughly ordered by how often it matters:

| Move | Where it surfaces first |
|---|---|
| Pricing or packaging change | Their pricing page — often silently, never announced |
| Product launch or feature ship | Changelog and release notes before the blog post |
| Executive hire or departure | Careers/leadership page edits, professional networks, trade press |
| Funding, M&A, filings | Filings and registries before reporting; wire coverage same day |
| Layoffs or restructuring | Employee posts and trackers before official confirmation — single-source rules apply (`verification.md`) |
| Partnership or major customer | Both parties' announcements; the quieter party's version usually says more |
| Outage or security incident | Status page and user complaints in real time |
| Hiring pattern shift | Careers page — a cluster of new roles in one area signals strategy quarters before launch |
| Positioning change | Homepage copy rewrite, new comparison pages naming the user's product |

A think-piece about the competitor matches none of these: it ships only if the analysis itself is second-order valuable, as analysis, attributed (`sources.md` → Vetting).

## Source Kit per Competitor

Blog + changelog + status page + careers page + pricing page + filings + their beat reporter (`sources.md` → Per-Entity Source Kits). The pages that change without announcements (pricing, careers, homepage) are the ones worth watching hardest — announced news reaches the user anyway.

## State Tracking and Diffs

- Keep last-known state per tracked entity in `preferences.md` → Entities: current pricing tiers, last release date, headcount signals, positioning line.
- A move is a **diff against that state**: "Pro tier $49 → $59, first increase since tracking began" beats "they have a $59 tier". Update the entity note every time you report a move.

## The Quiet Competitor

- No news IS data when the baseline is known: "first release in 3 months from a weekly-shipping team" is a reportable item — slowdown, pivot, or trouble.
- Report cadence breaks in both directions: a quiet company suddenly shipping weekly is the more urgent signal.
- Never fill quiet weeks with mention-coverage to make tracking look alive — that is padding with extra steps (SKILL.md Traps).

## Tracking People

- Track **statements and moves by** the person: what they published, joined, left, funded, said on record. Coverage and commentary about them ships only when it reports a move.
- A tracked person changing jobs is a double item when the destination is also tracked — say so in the why-line.

## Industry Pulse

- Beyond named entities: funding rounds in the space, regulatory moves, M&A among adjacent players, standard-body decisions. These earn body-item status when they change the user's competitive landscape; otherwise Worth Noting.
- The escalation rule: **a competitor move that directly touches a tracked user interest is a Highlight candidate regardless of outlet size** — a two-line changelog entry can outrank front-page news (SKILL.md rule 1).

## Sensitive-Claim Discipline

Layoff rumors, misconduct, financial-trouble whispers: mandatory hold per `verification.md` — reputational claims never ship on one source, whatever `single_source_policy` says. When confirmed, report the fact, not the schadenfreude; the user needs the signal, not the drama.
