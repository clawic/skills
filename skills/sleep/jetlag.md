# Jet Lag — Light Timing by Direction

The clock shifts on light and melatonin timing, not willpower. Every window below derives from Tmin = habitual wake minus 2.5 h (wake 07:00 → Tmin 04:30), converted to the destination clock.

## Decide First: Adapt or Don't

- Rule of 3: adapt only when zones crossed ≥ 3 AND nights at destination ≥ 3. Otherwise keep home sleep hours, schedule commitments into the overlap window, cover with 20-min naps and morning-only caffeine.
- Shift rates for planning: advancing (east) ~1 h/day, delaying (west) ~1.5 h/day. A 6-zone eastward trip = ~6 days to alignment; give the user this number so day-3 grogginess reads as on-schedule.
- Each adapted day, move the Tmin estimate 1 h earlier (east) or 1.5 h later (west) and re-derive the light windows.

## Eastward (the hard direction)

- Convert home Tmin to destination clock. Before Tmin, avoid bright light: sunglasses outdoors, no sunrise walks. After Tmin, seek 2-3 h of outdoor light.
- Melatonin 0.5 mg taken 5 h before target bedtime, first 3-4 nights.
- First night: bed no earlier than 22:00 destination even if wrecked; a 19:00 collapse produces a 3am wide-awake.
- Worked example, NYC → Paris (6 zones east, wakes 07:00): Tmin 04:30 home = 10:30 Paris on arrival. Day 1 sunglasses until 10:30 then outdoor light; melatonin 0.5 mg at 18:00 for a 23:00 bedtime; day 2 Tmin ≈ 09:30; aligned around day 5-6. Book nothing decision-heavy before 11:00 the first two days.

## Westward

- Bright light late afternoon and evening; avoid early-morning light for 2-3 days (it lands after home-Tmin and advances, fighting the delay).
- The signature failure is a 4am wake, not sleep onset; brief the user that it closes within 2-3 days. On the 4am wake: stay in the dark, no light, no phone — dawn light that early advances the clock back east.
- Melatonin mostly unnecessary westward.

## Extreme Crossings (> 8 zones)

\> 8 zones east: the clock may take the shorter path and resolve as a delay (antidromic re-entrainment). Plan 9-12 zone eastward trips as westward-style delays; if day-3 sleepiness lands at odd hours, re-estimate Tmin from when the user feels worst.

## Pre-Flight Shifting (critical trips)

For a competition, keynote, or negotiation in the first 48 h after landing, start at home: shift the whole schedule (sleep, light, meals) 1 h/day toward destination time for 2-3 days before departure, with light timed by the same Tmin rules (Eastman protocols). Each pre-shifted hour is an adaptation day bought before boarding. Skip for ordinary trips — the social cost usually exceeds the benefit.

## On the Plane

- Sleep on the plane only if it is night at the destination; otherwise stay awake — a mistimed 4-h plane sleep is a nap taken inside the wrong circadian day.
- Set watch and phone to destination time at boarding; eat roughly on destination meal times.
- No alcohol as a sleep aid aloft: altitude amplifies the second-half fragmentation (`substances.md`). Eye mask + earplugs beat a drink.
- Red-eye landing in a destination morning: 20-min nap on arrival day is legal; the 3-h hotel collapse is not — it lands in the avoid-light window's circadian night and cements home time.

## Multi-Leg and Recurring Travel

- Multi-city trips with < 3 nights per stop: pick ONE anchor zone (usually where the highest-stakes days are), adapt to it, treat other stops by Rule of 3.
- Frequent flyers on short rotations (the consultant pattern): stop re-adapting weekly — stay on home time permanently, exactly like flight crews, and protect the home Wake Anchor.
- Returning home: same physics, direction flipped. Eastbound home = advance rules again; most people under-plan the return because the trip "is over".

## Deliverable

Deliver trip plans as a per-day table (avoid-light window, seek-light window, melatonin dose + time, caffeine cutoff, earliest bedtime) in `~/Clawic/data/sleep/trip-<destination>.md`. One row per day from departure day to expected alignment; caffeine row follows the ≥ 8 h cutoff against that day's target bedtime.
