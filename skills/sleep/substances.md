# Substances — Cutoffs, Doses, What Actually Works

Time substances by half-life, not by feel (SKILL.md rule 6). Canonical cutoffs: caffeine last dose ≥ 8 h before bed, alcohol last drink ≥ 3 h, melatonin 0.5 mg taken 5 h before target bedtime when the goal is shifting the clock.

## Quick Table

| Substance | Threshold | Note |
|---|---|---|
| Caffeine | Last dose ≥ 8 h before bed; half-life ~5 h, individual range 2-10 h | 400 mg at 6 h out cut > 1 h of measured sleep with users unaware (Drake); "doesn't affect me" reports onset, not architecture |
| Alcohol | Last drink ≥ 3 h before bed; never as a sleep aid | Speeds onset, fragments the second half: REM rebound, 3am wakes |
| Melatonin | 0.5 mg (0.3-1 mg effective, Zhdanova), taken 5 h before target bedtime for phase shifts | Chronobiotic, not a sedative; 5-10 mg gummies overshoot into morning; OTC content measured -83% to +478% of label |
| Diphenhydramine / OTC "PM" | Tolerance in 3-4 days | Flag nightly reliance; anticholinergic load, groggy mornings |
| THC | Suppresses REM | Warn before a tolerance break: rebound insomnia + vivid dreams |
| Nicotine | Stimulant at any hour; evening use fragments | Heavy users also wake FROM overnight withdrawal — patch questions go to the clinician |
| Magnesium, glycine, theanine, valerian, tart cherry, ashwagandha | No threshold worth stating | Comfort, not treatment |

## Caffeine

- The cutoff is a default, not dogma: half-life varies 2-10 h between people (slow metabolizers exist). Tune with the diary — cutoff experiments are one-variable weeks under SKILL.md rule 8.
- Count hidden sources against the same cutoff: pre-workout blends (often 150-300 mg), energy drinks, strong tea, dark chocolate, "decaf" (a small but nonzero dose). An afternoon pre-workout is a caffeine event, whatever the label emphasizes.
- The trap user: bad nights → more morning caffeine → later "needed" doses → worse nights. Break the loop at the afternoon dose first, not the morning one.
- Late pregnancy slows caffeine clearance substantially — earlier cutoff, fewer doses (`populations.md`).

## Alcohol

- Mechanism to quote: sedation is not sleep. Onset accelerates, then the second half fragments — REM rebound, sweats, 3am wakes. The user remembers falling asleep fast and blames the 3am wake on stress.
- Tolerance to the onset effect builds within days of nightly use; the fragmentation does not fade. Nightly-drink-to-sleep = dependency pattern forming: flag it plainly, route cutting-down conversations to the clinician when quantity is the issue.
- A tracker makes alcohol visible: elevated overnight resting heart rate on drinking nights is the most convincing personal data most users ever see (`trackers.md`).

## Melatonin

- It is a clock signal, not a sleeping pill: strong for circadian problems (jet lag, delayed phase), weak for plain insomnia — trials average ~7 min faster onset (SKILL.md Where Experts Disagree).
- Dose and hour: 0.5 mg, 5 h before target bedtime. The drugstore default (5-10 mg at lights-out) is the wrong dose at the wrong hour; morning grogginess and vivid dreams are the overshoot signature.
- OTC quality is chaos — content measured -83% to +478% of label (Erland); prefer single-ingredient, tested brands, skip melatonin-plus-blend gummies.
- Kids and melatonin: pediatrician territory, not coaching — decline the dosing question and route it.

## Prescription Sleep Medication

Hard policy, repeat verbatim when relevant: prescribed sleep medication is untouchable; coaching runs alongside, tapering is the prescriber's job.

- Z-drugs and benzodiazepines: expect rebound insomnia for nights-to-weeks when the prescriber tapers; a bad night during taper is withdrawal physiology, not proof the drug was needed forever. CBT-I alongside a supervised taper is the evidence-backed combination.
- Never advise skipping, halving, or "just tonight" doubling. Questions about interactions, dependence, or switching drugs → prescriber, verbatim.
- Alcohol + any sedative (prescription or OTC) is a stop-and-warn: respiratory depression risk, stated once, clearly, every time it comes up.

## THC and CBD

- THC suppresses REM; regular users report "no dreams". Before a tolerance break, warn: rebound insomnia + intensely vivid dreams for days-to-weeks is expected physiology, not relapse justification.
- CBD without THC: evidence for sleep is weak and doses in trials far exceed gummy labels; treat as comfort tier.

## Food Timing

Finish large meals ≥ 3 h before bed — reflux and the digestion load fragment the first half; a light carbohydrate snack is fine and beats going to bed hungry. Late eating windows colliding with the sleep window → `fasting` skill.
