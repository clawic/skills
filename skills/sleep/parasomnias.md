# Parasomnias — Nightmares, Paralysis, Sleepwalking

Strange events at night sort by two axes: WHEN in the night, and whether there is dream recall. Identify first; most are benign and coachable, a short list is referral-only. Dream journaling and interpretation belong to the `dreams` skill; treatment of distressing sleep events stays here.

## Identification Table

| Event | Timing | Recall | Likely | Route |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Screaming, bolt upright, inconsolable, no memory next day | First third of night | None | Night terror (NREM) | Coach below; mostly kids |
| Walking, complex actions, blank face | First third | None or fragments | Sleepwalking (NREM) | Safety-proof below |
| Vivid frightening dream, wakes fully, tells the story | Second half | Vivid | Nightmare | IRT below |
| Awake but cannot move, chest pressure, intruder sensation | Sleep onset or waking | Full awareness | Sleep paralysis | Reassure + triggers below |
| Punching, kicking, leaping, acting the dream, mostly 50+ | Second half | Dream matches action | REM behavior disorder | SKILL.md Red Flags: neurologist |
| Loud bang or flash at sleep onset, no pain | Onset | Momentary | Exploding head syndrome | Reassure; benign |
| Whole-body jerk at onset | Onset | Falling sensation | Hypnic jerk | Reassure; worse with caffeine and sleep debt |
| Talking, mumbling | Any | None | Sleep talking | Reassure; partner earplugs (`environment.md`) |

Shared amplifier: sleep deprivation, alcohol, irregular schedules, and stress raise the frequency of every NREM event and sleep paralysis. Half of parasomnia coaching is just the Wake Anchor plus debt repayment (`performance.md`).

## Nightmares — Imagery Rehearsal Therapy (IRT)

Occasional nightmares are normal; coach when ≥ 1/week with distress or bedtime avoidance.

1. Pick ONE recurring nightmare (not the worst — start winnable).
2. Rewrite it in daytime, on paper, changing the story anywhere BEFORE the worst moment; any new ending the user prefers. The user owns the rewrite; do not author it for them.
3. Rehearse the rewritten version in imagination ~10 min daily for 1-2 weeks (Krakow's protocol). Rehearsal happens in daytime, never at bedtime.
4. Expect bleed-through: the rewritten version starts appearing, then frequency drops across nightmares generally.
5. Known trauma or suspected PTSD → clinician-delivered IRT or trauma therapy; self-serve rewriting of trauma content can backfire. Nightmares that began with a medication change → prescriber.

## Sleep Paralysis

- Mechanism, stated plainly, is the treatment: REM keeps the body paralyzed while dreaming; here the mind wakes before the switch flips back. The intruder imagery is dream content leaking into a half-awake state. Terrifying, harmless, common — most people get it at least once (Sharpless).
- Exit technique: stop fighting the big muscles; wiggle a toe or finger, move the eyes side to side, focus on breathing rhythm — episodes break in seconds-to-a-minute.
- Prevention: regular schedule, repay debt, and avoid back-sleeping if episodes cluster supine (they often do).
- Recurrent + severe distress, or with daytime sleep attacks → specialist (paralysis clusters with narcolepsy; SKILL.md Red Flags).

## Sleepwalking and Night Terrors (NREM)

- Do not wake, guide back: waking mid-episode produces confusion and panic; steer gently to bed. Next-day interrogation embarrasses kids for an event they cannot remember — skip it.
- Safety-proofing is the actual treatment: stair gates, locked windows, door chime on the sleeper's room, car keys and sharp objects out of reach. An adult sleepwalker who has left the house needs a specialist, not just a chime.
- Kids with predictable night terrors: scheduled awakenings — rouse briefly 15-30 min before the usual episode time for 1-2 weeks, then stop. Most childhood NREM events fade with age; say so, it is what parents need to hear.
- Refer: adult-ONSET sleepwalking or terrors (new in adulthood warrants workup), any injury, episodes after starting a new medication (some hypnotics cause complex sleep behaviors — prescriber, urgently), sexsomnia or violent episodes (specialist, and a safety conversation now).

## Bruxism (night grinding)

Morning jaw pain, headaches at the temples, a partner who hears grinding: dentist for a guard and wear assessment; flag that grinding clusters with apnea — snoring + grinding together strengthens the Red Flags referral rather than replacing it.
