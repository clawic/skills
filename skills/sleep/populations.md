# Populations — Teens, Pregnancy, New Parents, Menopause, 65+

The base protocols assume a healthy mid-life adult. These modifiers change targets, floors, and referral thresholds; everything not modified here runs as written.

## Teens (13-18)

- Biology, not attitude: puberty delays the clock up to ~2 h; need is 8-10 h (AASM). A 23:30-07:30 teen is on-spec, not lazy; the conflict is the 07:00 school bell.
- Weekend drift: the adult 1-h cap is unrealistic against a 2-h biological delay — teens: cap weekend drift at 2 h (marked exception to the SKILL.md 1-h line). Beyond 2 h, Monday runs on a jet-lagged clock.
- The lever order: morning light + fixed school-day wake first, phone-out-of-bedroom second, earlier bedtime last (it fails first because the clock will not cooperate until light moves it — `circadian.md` advance protocol).
- Coach the parent to negotiate, not decree: the phone leaves the bedroom for the whole household or the rule dies in a week.
- Refer: sleepiness with normal-length sleep (screen narcolepsy per Red Flags), or a phase so late school is failing — specialist, sometimes with school accommodation letters.

## Pregnancy

- Restriction is contraindicated (`insomnia.md` list): run stimulus control, Wake Anchor, and worry scheduling only; skip TIB math entirely.
- Trimester pattern to normalize: first = sleepy (progesterone), third = fragmented (bladder, hips, reflux). Fragmentation here is physiology; the goal is comfort and total hours, not consolidation.
- Side-sleeping from the third trimester, pillow between knees + under bump; going-to-sleep position is what counts — night repositioning happens and is fine.
- Reflux fragmenting the night: finish large meals ≥ 3 h before bed, elevate the head of the bed; persistent reflux → obstetric provider.
- Caffeine clears slower in late pregnancy — tighten the cutoff earlier than the standard 8 h and keep total intake within the obstetric limit the user's provider set.
- Refer fast: new snoring/gasping + morning headaches or rising blood pressure (SKILL.md Red Flags pregnancy row), restless legs (ferritin check is routine in pregnancy), any sleeping-medication question including "natural" supplements.

## New Parents

- The unit of survival is one protected 4-h block per parent per night: split the night into shifts (e.g., one parent owns 22:00-02:00, the other 02:00-06:00, the off-parent in another room with earplugs). Alternating every wake destroys both sleepers for zero gain.
- "Sleep when the baby sleeps" fails anyone with insomnia traits — daytime pressure to nap on command becomes sleep effort. Offer the block system instead.
- Feeding method changes the design, not the principle: exclusively breastfeeding → the non-feeding parent owns everything except the feed itself (diaper, resettle) during their shift.
- Postpartum red line: a parent who CANNOT sleep when the baby sleeps and the block is protected — wired-awake insomnia — or intrusive hopeless thoughts: screen for postpartum mood disorder, escalate per the user's care setup. This is the population where "insomnia" is most often a mood flag.
- Infant sleep training methods are pediatric territory: coach the parents' schedule, route baby-side decisions to the pediatrician.

## Menopause

- Hot flashes fragment the second half of the night; the sleep complaint is often the presenting symptom. Environment goes aggressive: room at the cold end of 16-19 °C, layered bedding to shed, moisture-wicking sleepwear, fan in reach (`environment.md`).
- CBT-I holds its evidence in menopause (MsFLASH trials) — run the full `insomnia.md` protocol; do not write the insomnia off as "just hormones" while the conditioned-arousal loop builds on top of the flashes.
- Alcohol is a flash trigger on top of its usual fragmentation — the ≥ 3 h cutoff is the minimum here; many do better dropping evening alcohol entirely for a diary-measured trial week.
- Hormone therapy questions → clinician, framed as: effective for flash-driven waking in suitable candidates, and it is the clinician's suitability call.

## Older Adults (65+)

- Recalibrate expectations first: sleep gets lighter, earlier, and slightly shorter with age; need is ~7-8 h and some fragmentation is normal aging, not a disorder. Chasing the sleep of a 30-year-old produces insomnia in a healthy 70-year-old.
- Restriction floor rises: 5.5 h if age 65+ (same line as `insomnia.md`); titrate more slowly and watch daytime function harder — falls are the risk restriction must never buy.
- Early evening sleepiness + 4am waking = advanced phase, the signature circadian drift of aging → evening light protocol (`circadian.md`), not sleeping pills.
- Nocturia: front-load fluids to daytime, taper the last 2 h before bed; more than 2 wakes-to-void a night → clinician (prostate, glucose, diuretic timing all live there).
- Naps are legal outside protocols: 10-20 min, finished ≥ 8 h before bedtime — the same rule as everyone, applied without guilt.
- Escalate readily: new dream enactment (Red Flags, RBD is an over-50 disease), sedating "PM" products in anyone on multiple medications (anticholinergic load — clinician), and any hypnotic prescription question (fall-risk conversation belongs to the prescriber).
