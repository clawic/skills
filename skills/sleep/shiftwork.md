# Shift Work — Damage Control, Not Adaptation

State once: full circadian adaptation to nights is rare outside never-flipping permanent night workers. The goal is minimizing debt and mistimed light, not "becoming nocturnal".

## Anchor Sleep

- One 3-4 h block at the same clock hours on both workdays and days off; the rest floats. Shift 23:00-07:00 → anchor 08:00-12:00 daily; extend to 15:00 on days off. The anchor stops the clock free-running between the two lifestyles.
- The anchor is the negotiation unit with family: protect those hours specifically (door sign, phone on silent, household briefed) instead of vaguely asking for "quiet days". A defended 4-h block beats an undefended 8-h attempt.
- Split sleep is legitimate here: anchor block + a 90-min top-up before the next shift. Consolidated 8 h is the wrong target on nights; total per 24 h is the metric.

## Light Control

- Bright light through the first half of the shift, dim the last 2 h.
- The commute home is the highest-leverage 30 min: sunrise light lands right after Tmin and yanks the clock toward daytime; dark sunglasses door-to-door.
- Day sleep needs true blackout — blackout curtains or eye mask, phone out of reach (`environment.md` for the full setup). Daylight leaking on a day sleeper is mistimed light on top of a debt.

## Caffeine and Food on Nights

- Caffeine: front-load, first half of shift only, none within 8 h of planned day sleep. The 05:00 coffee is the classic day-sleep killer.
- Eating: main meal before the shift, light snacks during, avoid heavy food in the 03:00-06:00 trough — digestion is circadian and large night meals drive reflux and the post-meal slump at the sleepiest hour.

## The First Night Shift

- Prophylactic nap the afternoon before: ~90 min (one full cycle) ending by early evening. Going in cold means 24+ h awake by the drive home.
- During the trough (roughly 03:00-06:00): schedule mechanical tasks, not judgment calls; use movement and light, not extra caffeine (cutoff already passed).
- The drive home after a night shift is the single most dangerous part of shift work. Sleepy at the wheel is a stop-condition: 20-min parked nap before driving; arrange a ride for the first night of a rotation when possible.

## Rotation Design (where the user has any influence)

- Forward only (morning → evening → night); ≥ 11 h between consecutive shifts; max 3-4 consecutive nights then ≥ 48 h recovery with two full nights.
- Backward-rotating or sub-11-h gaps ("quick returns") are the schedule features most worth spending negotiation capital on — more than shift length.

## Transition Day (after the last night)

Short block 08:00-12:00, stay up through the evening, sleep the coming night at normal hours. The full-day sleep-in eats the recovery night.

## Permanent Nights

The honest fork, present it as a choice: (a) keep night hours 7 days/week including days off — real adaptation, social cost high; or (b) flip to daytime on days off — social life intact, clock permanently misaligned, run anchor sleep forever. Most people claim (a) and live (b); the anchor exists for the (b) reality.

## On-Call

Fragmentation, not duration, is the on-call injury: five 90-min fragments do not sum to 7.5 h of sleep. Plays: anchor block still applies; after a night with 2+ callouts treat the next day as a bad-night day (`performance.md`); no schedule-based protocol (restriction, phase shifting) during on-call weeks — the pager breaks the measurement.

## When It Stops Being Coaching

Shift work disorder = shift sleepiness + window insomnia persisting ≥ 3 months despite a protocol like this → clinician referral. Prescription alertness aids and hypnotics for shift workers exist but are the clinician's call, never coached here (`substances.md` policy).
